T/C Arms Encore Prohunter in .308 Win.: Full Review

Using an Avient ­barrel on a single-­shot T/C Arms Encore ProHunter for a Canadian bear hunt.

 (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)

October 22, 2026

By Jack Oller

Single-­shot rifles are underrated. Though they usually play second fiddle to bolt-­action and semiautomatic rifles, single shots have ensured many successful hunts. No name is as synonymous with single-­shot rifles as 
Thompson/Center Arms (T/C)
. The brand’s reputation and market reach has fluctuated in recent years, but new ownership and direction may change that. As T/C reestablishes itself, I took one of its new rifles to Alberta, Canada, with Grandslam Hunting Adventures: 
T/C Encore ProHunter with an Avient barrel chambered in .308 Winchester
.
This T/C Encore ProHunter was outfitted with a prototype stainless-steel barrel covered by ceramic composite and wrapped with carbon-fiber. The combination is light, optimizes barrel temperature and ensures the barrel’s accuracy potential is realized. Single-shot rifles are usually simple, but this Encore ProHunter illustrates the use of several technologies. With a suppressor attached to a threaded barrel, a single-shot may offer near-silent reports from the muzzle. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
T/C Revival
T/C has changed hands several times since its inception in the early 1960s, so it’s worth an update on the status of manufacturing. The company got its start when the K.W. Thompson Tool Company was introduced to gunsmith Warren Center. Center had developed an interesting single-­shot break-­action pistol, which would become the iconic T/C Contender. It has been popular with handgun hunters, handloaders, and silhouette shooters since 1965.
Serving as president and CEO, Gregg Ritz oversaw the sale of T/C to Smith & Wesson in 2007. During S&W’s stewardship, the original factory location in Rochester, New Hampshire, was closed and production moved to Springfield, Massachusetts. Notably, many longstanding T/C employees did not relocate to the then-­S&W headquarters. Unfortunately, much of the company’s intrinsic knowledge was lost. Also during this time, S&W introduced several bolt-­action rifles to the product line.
The T/C Encore ProHunter was also utilized to evaluate the absorption properties of Avient’s new synthetic recoil pad technology. Shooting .308 centerfire loads from the single-shot rifle felt no different than shooting a semiautomatic with recoil-reducing qualities. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Sales declined, so S&W opted to phase out the T/C brand and quietly made it available for sale in 2021. Ritz purchased the company back from S&W in 2024 and announced T/C would return to Rochester, but manufacturing wasn’t built overnight. It’s taken T/C several years to get going again, but we are finally seeing new rifles, muzzleloaders and shotguns on store shelves. Priority is being giving to produce single-­shot Encore ProHunter centerfire rifles and Triumph break-­action muzzleloaders, but T/C hopes to reinvigorate its classic portfolio and innovate new designs. I think they’re off to a good start.

Encore ProHunter
The first firearm to come out of the Rochester facility is the Encore ProHunter. It is much the same as the original Encore introduced in 1997, but it received several aesthetic and functional improvements. For the most part, the news with the ProHunter is that the Encore system is available again, and T/C is working to build parts support and a warranty service.
An integral extractor ensures that spent cases can be removed easily from the chamber. Optics are mounted to the barrel, so removing the barrel from the action doesn’t require re-zeroing. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
The Encore ProHunter stock features an Enhanced Limbsaver AirTech recoil pad, which purportedly reduces perceived recoil by as much as 70 percent. The original Encore rifle did not have this feature, so it’s a welcome addition to the ProHunter. Additionally, compression arches in the middle of the stock also contribute to felt-­recoil reduction. The T/C Encore was designed as a simple and efficient system. It’s one of the easiest change-­barrel platforms you can find, so the stock needs to support cartridges ranging from .204 Ruger and .22 ARC to .300 Winchester Magnum and .375 H&H.
On that note, T/C currently lists 20 stainless-­steel barrel options for the Encore ProHunter, and the barrels are just as simple to change as the original. The break-­action is opened by pulling back on the triggerguard spur. A hinge pin mates the barrel to the stock and action. Remove the hinge pin and the barrel can be replaced quickly.

The optic rail atop the receiver is secured with four #6-48 screws torqued to 25 inch-pounds. The 0 MOA rail is manufactured for T/C by Evolution Gun Works (EGW). (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Availability of assorted T/C barrels can be hit and miss. There seems to be plenty of .308 Winchester barrels, but it was hard for me to find .22-­250 Remington barrels in stock online, for example. The .375 H&H was listed on T/C’s website, but it’s not ready either. I realize that delays are expected when starting from scratch. As more barrels become available, the Encore ProHunter could return as one of the best ways to try out a new cartridge without needing to buy an entirely new gun.
Composite Heat Release Technology
Avient
 developed a new approach to barrel construction involving a unique combination of ceramic and carbon fiber to reduce barrel weight and improve heat transfer and dissipation. Avient refers to it as “Composite Heat Release Technology” (CHRT). For now, Avient only works directly with firearm manufacturers. A company such as T/C sends Avient an unfinished barrel, which is then coated with a ceramic and wrapped with layers of carbon fiber. The CHRT utilizes principles of thermal inertia to accelerate heat dissipation. Keeping your barrel cool keeps groups smaller at distance for a longer time.
The triggerguard supports opening the action. Pulling backward on the triggerguard spur uses leverage to unlock the action to open the breech for loading or unloading the rifle. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
A single-­shot rifle can’t take advantage of this technology’s full potential. One of these barrels, however, would be well-­suited on a semiautomatic rifle. A single-­shot rifle doesn’t get hot very quickly, but this evaluation reinforced just how successful the Encore system is as a platform to host and test different barrels. The Avient barrel proved accurate in the woods, and it reduced the overall 7-­pound weight of the Encore ProHunter. This Avient .308 Encore ProHunter barrel set a good benchmark for future offerings. Contributor Brad Fitzpatrick offered a complete review of the Avient CHRT in the 
2027 Guns & Ammo Annual
.
A T/C Encore ProHunter comes with a section of EGW 0 MOA rail fastened to the top of the barrel with #6-48 screws. The rail can be removed and attached to other barrels when using a different cartridge. It’s an efficient and simple way to attach a scope to the rifle.
The hammer spur can be rotated to the left or right. Even with a low-mounted scope, the hammer can be accessed since it doesn’t interfere with the side of the scope’s ocular lens housing. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Triggers on single-­shot rifles are generally quite good, and the Encore ProHunter did not disappoint. With the hammer cocked, there was no take-­up of note. The trigger was measured at 4 pounds, 12 ounces. The trigger components are nitride coated, a finish that’s good for protection against corrosion but that also keeps parts working smoothly.

The hammer has a clever, patented design to allow the spur to be rotated left or right to clear a scope’s eyebox, depending on hand dominance. As a right-­handed shooter, I would adjust it to the right. A screw holds the spur in place, and it is textured with a checker-­like pattern for sure contact.
Sling-­swivel studs are located on the forend and rear of the stock for a sling. The receiver is stainless steel, and it has a normal weight around 7 pounds with an overall length of 40 inches. Single-­shot rifles are simple but that’s a huge part of what makes them so appealing. There’s not much more to describe regarding the rifle’s specs.
The Encore barrel is clearly labeled on the left side. Given the ease of barrel changes, it is recommended that you always verify which caliber barrel you’ve installed before loading and firing. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Why A Single-­Shot?
To bolt-­gun and semiauto hunters, a single-­shot rifle seems slow to shoot. However, there are many reasons why it’s good to keep a single-­shot rifle in the safe. First, they’re reliable. Feeding concerns are usually a non-­issue, and they’re easy to clean and maintain. Lefties, single shots are often ambidextrous.
The stainless-steel barrel profile is turned down and then layered in Avient’s ceramic composite and carbon fiber. Avient describes the result as Composite Heat Release Technology (CHRT). (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Like a lever action or bolt gun, a single-­shot rifle is a great suppressor host because no gas escapes the action. For those who think they cannot be shot quickly, a cartridge cuff on the buttstock positions spare shots in a convenient-­to-­reach spot, not to mention those who load from a cartridge carrier on a belt. With practice, a person can shoot the Encore ProHunter fast. Speed can be detrimental to initial skill building, though. Teaching a new shooter on a single-­shot forces them to take their time and be cognizant of the fundamentals of marksmanship. If you learn to shoot a single-­shot with precision, then other platforms should be easier to learn.
To add, those who use single-­shot rifles often embrace ethical hunting. In this era where the distance a shot is taken on an animal is pushed farther, too often the reality is that multiple shots at required for long-­range attempts. Should you really attempt the shot if you think it will take require more than one round? No one is perfect, but we should all hunt as if we only have one bullet.
The stock’s grip is features three rubberized finger placement points that offer tactile locations for comfortable hand placement. The pistol-grip profile also supports operating the triggerguard. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Hunting with a gun such as the Encore ProHunter requires an assuredness of skill. It’s our duty to make ethical shots on game, and I like single-­shot rifles because I embrace that one-­shot mindset even more when I know I can’t just rack the bolt or pull the trigger again. I have hunted with every kind of gun and like them all, but there is a greater sense of satisfaction in completing the challenge when using a single-­shot rifle.
One-­Shot Bears
It’s one thing to use a single-­shot Encore on a whitetail deer or hog, but what if you only had one round in the chamber on a dangerous game hunt? You feel extra alert when hunting with a single shot on the ground. Fortunately, I spent most of my hunt with the Encore ProHunter sitting in a treestand. Still, the thought lingers that a poor shot could only leave me with cold fingers to reload and an angry bear that can climb a tree.
For hunting and testing, the Encore ProHunter was equipped with a Leupold VX-6HD 3-18x44mm scope. Its low power was useful in the thick woods of northern Canada. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Two hours north of Edmonton, Alberta, Clayton Royer of Grandslam Hunting Adventures converted an old oil camp into his home base for spring black bear. He and his guides have scouted many great black bear spots across the “Queen’s Land.” (Should it now be referred to as the “King’s Land?”) In America, this would be relatable to public hunting land; Canadians might have more space, though.

In many ways, a spring black bear hunt is like fall whitetail hunting. It’s a lot of sitting still in a stand, waiting to see what comes along. The only difference is this game can climb the tree you’re in! With two tags in my pocket, I had a blast watching many bears cross my path — and they come quietly, too! The first nice bear was skittish. When something in the distance spooked him, I grunted, causing him to pause for a second. There was no time for hesitation, and one shot dropped him where he stood as he was about to take another step and run.
The second bear allowed me more time, but the story was the same. One shot and barely a few steps later ended my successful season. With the beauty of a black bear coat, I cannot wait for the taxidermy to return.
(Photo courtesy of Jack Oller)
Parting (Single) Shot
A single-­shot rifle requires a mindset different than when using other firearms, and there is a unique satisfaction when it’s done successfully. I look forward to the return of T/C, and it was a interesting and educational to test the Encore ProHunter with Avient’s CHRT barrel. Whatever comes next will surely deserve more attention.
