Springfield Armory 10-­8 Performance Master Class: Full Review

True to its name, the Performance Master Class is a 1911 that provides excellent shooting and next-level accuracy. Here's a full review.

 (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)

June 11, 2026

By Guns & Ammo Staff

The 1911 pistol has been a mainstay of defense and competition for more than a century. In that time, it has been made available in many forms, from minimalistic and functional to exquisite and expensive. The most desirable custom versions often require a patient spirit for fulfillment, but it is now possible to acquire a custom-­level Model 1911 built on a quality platform for a reasonable price, as far as custom guns are compared.
The ejection port is lowered and flared, and features a notch at the rear of the barrel hood to function as a loaded chamber indicator. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Springfield Armory collaborated with Hilton Yam to produce 
its 10-­8 Performance Master Class 1911
. The company name “10-­8 Performance” is a reference to law enforcement radio codes. The “10-­8” code typically means “available for assignment” or “on duty.” It’s fitting for Springfield Armory’s latest 1911, too. 
Yam is a former FBI Special Agent, and he spent much of his career on the SWAT team out of the Miami, Florida, office. He was also a principal firearms instructor. His experience building 1911s is rooted in 
him discovering a need for top-­quality 1911 components
. With Springfield Armory’s 1911 selected as as its base, the 10-­8 Performance Master Class pistol was born.
A diamond-textured set of grip panels with thumb relief for easier reach to the magazine release are fitting of the 10-8 medallion. The flat-face aluminum trigger and square-shaped triggerguard are visual cues that make this model distinctive. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
The Baseline
Springfield Armory’s 1911 starts with carbon-steel forgings, machined to pre-­fit status, and complete with an accessory rail and squared triggerguard. Slides and frames were selected and paired for best fit, numbering them to each other. Then a barrel is precisely fit to each pair, numbered to match the slide and ­frame. The barrel is stainless steel, and the muzzle is coned-shaped to a diameter large enough to fit the interior of the slide. There is no barrel bushing. Yam believes this improves its accuracy potential. The barrel also receives a target crown to protect the rifling near the muzzle. Once fitted, these are treated to a diamond-­like carbon (DLC) finish. Since the pistol lacks a bushing, it also means that there is a reverse recoil spring plug to contain the recoil spring.

The slide is machined with forward cocking serrations but the rear serrations stop short of the bottom edge of the slide, leaving them abbreviated. On top of the slide, the 10-­8 Performance Master Class has a fixed rear sight dovetailed into coverplate for the Agency Optics System (AOS). The tall frong sight features a green fiber optic. 
(Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Springfield Armory’s AOS can be adapted to fit all ­popular pistol optics. The U-­notch rear sight is excellent if you do not prefer optics on this 1911. Both sights align and are visible through the red dot for co-­witnessing. 
The muzzle end of the slide has the recoil spring tunnel recessed, machined to end in a tight radius and matching the forward end of the frame’s dustcover.

The frame’s accessory rail is slotted on the sides all the way back to the triggerguard. Three cross-­slots offer multiple options in accessory location and light/laser choices. If it is meant to fit onto a railed pistol, it should fit on the 10-­8 Performance Master Class 1911. 
The 1911 10-8 Performance Master Class has a bushingless barrel. The 9mm target barrel sports an 11-degree crown that blends neatly with the slide face and plunger. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
The triggerguard is part of the forging, but it was machined square for the 10-8. Inside is a flat-­faced aluminum trigger tuned to a clean and crisp trigger pull. At the rear, the frame has an bilateral thumb safety, an ambidextrous high-­ride grip safety with speed bump at the bottom, and a Commander-­style hammer. The slide-­stop is Yam’s design. It was machined from bar stock but the angle, shape and texture of the slide-­stop pad was made to 10-8 Performance specifications.
The magazine catch is one-sided, extended and checkered. As an additional aid to speedy reloads, the bottom of the frame has a two-­piece magazine funnel installed, too. For a non-­slip grip, the frame is machined with 20 lines-­per-­inch (LPI) checkering on the frontstrap complemented by a set of G10 panels. The mainspring housing is flat and similarly checkered as the front.
The green fiber-optic front sight is tall enough to co-witness with the rear serrated U-notch through an optic’s window. The rear sight is dovetailed to the removeable AOS coverplate. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Hilton Yam is famous in 1911 circles for his superb parts. We know that many pistolsmiths often use 10-8 Performance products in their own builds. Springfield Armory’s 10-­8 Performance Master Class model received a full suite of these parts. In addition to the careful inspection and hand-­fitting of major components, each 10-­8 Performance Master Class receives its own Yam slide-­stop, trigger, extended magazine catch, reverse recoil plug, and specially machined front and rear cocking serrations. Yam’s flat aluminum trigger is not just installed, but it’s fitted without play for a precise release of the sear, minimal overtravel, clean and consistent trigger weight. An overtravel adjustment screw is included.
Once each 10-­8 Performance Master Class pistol is built, it receives a black Cerakote finish. After quality control inspections, it’s placed into a zippered case with three magazines, a discount card to order the AOS plate needed to fit a red-dot optic of your choice, a trigger lock and manual.

The flat-face aluminum trigger tested at just less than 4 pounds. Within the trigger shoe is an overtravel adjustment screw. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
The Springfield Armory 10-­8 Performance Master Class can be had in either 9mm or .45 ACP. In 9mm, the barrel is ramped and fully supported, and the supplied magazines hold nine rounds each. In .45 ACP, the feed ramp is the classic frame-­and-­barrel geometry, and the supplied magazines hold eight rounds each. The magazines are also mated to the Master Class, as each has an extended basepad, made of a tough synthetic, so they extend just enough beyond the bottom of the magazine-well funnel. Reloads don’t require reaching up inside in order to get mags to seat.
A SureFire X300 Ultra was used to test function with the rail. The light does not extend beyond the full-length slide. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
At The Range
G&A’s sample 10-­8 Performance Master Class pistol was chambered in 9mm. An all-­steel self-­loading pistol in 9mm, it weighs just more than 2½ pounds. For that reason, it didn’t produce much felt recoil. The low recoil and clean, crisp trigger made it simple to extract the full accuracy potential from its match-grade, stainless-steel barrel. Of course, it was fitted by those who know the job. The 5-­inch length means the cartridge generates almost as much velocity as is possible from a 9mm handgun.
Magazines hold expected capacity for a 1911. The 9mm carries nine rounds, and the .45 ACP model holds eight. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
There’s one aspect of a Government-­size 1911 in 9mm that can be a plus or minus, depending on how you look at things. Some of the loads tested had ejection where the empties fell several feet away. Other loads had the brass barely clearing the ejection port. They all left, though, and there were no failures to eject. If you favor brisk ejection, you aren’t going to get it in a Government-­sized 9mm.
(Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Accuracy from the 10-8 Performance 1911 was superb, as expected. Despite temperatures in the 20s, all the loads tested produced small, uniform groups. With great looks, a solid build that delivers accuracy and a price that simply shouts, “Take me home!” what more can one ask for? 10-7.
