1971 Ruger Super Bearcat .22LR: A Perfect Gift for a Daughter

Passing Down a Classic Steel-Frame Rimfire Revolver

 The single-action Ruger Super Bearcat was produced from 1971 until 1974. The steel frame designates it as a Super Bearcat; regular Bearcats had alloy frames. (Michael Anschuetz photo)

October 03, 2026

By Joel J. Hutchcroft

The small, lightweight, .22 rimfire Bearcat single-action revolver was one of 
Bill Ruger’s joys
. According to Ruger & His Guns by R.L. Wilson, Ruger described the Super Bearcat as a “little jewel among American handguns.” I totally agree with Ruger’s statement.
The Bearcat is patterned on the Remington Pocket Model percussion revolver except it’s a .22 rimfire. (Michael Anschuetz photo)
Mechanicals
The Bearcat is a .22-caliber rimfire rendition of the classic Remington Pocket Model percussion single-action revolver. What gives the configuration reported on here the “Super” designation is the steel frame. (Regular Bearcats had black anodized alloy frames.)
All Bearcats made from 1958 to 1970 had 4.0-inch barrels, wood grips, and fixed sights. The trigger guard was either black or anodized brass. The grips were either epoxy-resin rosewood or oil-filled walnut. The ejector rod housing was either steel or alloy. These revolvers are known as “Old Model Bearcats.” The Super Bearcat (made from 1971 to 1974) was similar except for the aforementioned steel frame.
After a 20-year hiatus from production, the Bearcat was returned to the Ruger catalog in 1996 as the New Bearcat. (Actually, it was brought back in 1993 as a convertible model with an extra .22 Magnum cylinder, but the Magnum cylinders were quickly recalled, and the convertible model was dropped in 1994.) The New Bearcat had been redesigned to utilize the transfer bar firing mechanism that all New Model Ruger SA revolvers use. Since 1996 the New Bearcat has been offered with 4.0- and the current 4.2-inch barrels and in blued finish or stainless steel. There have been several variations, including a distributor special version with adjustable sights in 2015. But this report is about the Old Model Super Bearcat.

The Super Bearcat shown here weighs 25 ounces and measures 8.8 inches long. Its height is 4.13 inches, and its width is 1.22 inches at the cylinder. Its grips are the oil-filled walnut panels, and its trigger guard is the anodized brass type.
Provenance
This revolver is another gun that I purchased at Pekin Gun, and I bought it in early 1996 after being promoted from managing editor to editor of Shooting Times. I actually bought it for my daughter, who was three years old at the time.
We used the little revolver for a photo in the June 1996 issue of Shooting Times, and the good folks at Pekin Gun let us borrow it for that photo shoot. I liked the six-shooter so much I bought it. I figured it would be a perfect first handgun for my daughter when she was old enough to shoot it. According to the serial number, our revolver was made in 1971. We still have the original box and papers, which add to its appeal.

Rangetime
I don’t shoot this dainty gun very often, but when I do, I have a good time of it. Its action is smooth, and its trigger pull is a crisp and clean 3.0 pounds. I keep it locked up in the gun safe for my daughter, but I get it out every now and then, and it brings a smile to my face every time because I always remember how much fun she had the first time she fired it. She must have been 10 years old at the time, and the Super Bearcat fit her small hands perfectly. She got the hang of it pretty quickly and was able to hit some exploding targets we were testing.
With her permission, I fired it for this report, and it was respectably accurate at close range with several loads. It’s a nice revolver that I hope my daughter will pass down to her child some day.
