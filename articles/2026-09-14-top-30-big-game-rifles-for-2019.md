Top 30 Big Game Rifles for 2019

From lightweight mountain rifles to heavy-hitting brush guns, here is the definitive gear guide for your next big hunt.

 New bolt guns, levers, semiautos and new calibers highlight a stellar list. 

September 14, 2026

By Brad Fitzpatrick

After a week spent roaming the halls of the 2019 SHOT Show in Las Vegas, it’s safe to say there’s never been a better time to be in the market for a new big game rifle. No matter the caliber, the platform or the game you intend to hunt, there are new rifles launched this year that will fit your needs and your budget—from high-tech bolt guns to semiautos. And while most of the new guns on display at the 2019 SHOT Show come with modern features like threaded muzzles, Cerakote finishes and synthetic stocks with the latest camo patterns, there were also some classic rifles that received updates for the new year, including Winchester’s 1892 lever action.
Bergara
Bergara’s Ridge family of rifles, which was introduced last year, now includes two short-barreled, special purpose rifles. Available in .308 Win. and 6.5 Creedmoor, they come with 18-inch threaded barrels and measure just 37.5 inches overall. Also new is the Premier Highlander Rifle, which comes with a fiberglass camo stock, TriggerTech trigger, and a No. 5 contour threaded 416 stainless steel fluted barrel. All of the metalwork receives a Sniper Gray Cerakote finish. Bergara also introduced the Ridgeback, a target-style bolt-action hunting rifle with an M-Lok-compatible adjustable stock, heavy barrel, Premier Action and a Graphite Black Cerakote finish. The 10-pound-plus Ridgeback is also compatible with AICS-style magazines.
bergara.online/us
Blaser
The German switch-barrel, straight-pull R8 series gets a new member: the Ultimate. It sports a thumbhole synthetic stock, or you can opt for the Ultimate Leather, boasting a black/brown synthetic stock with cocoa leather inlays. If you’ve got the scratch, you can order options such as an elastomer adjustable comb stock, the company’s recoil absorption system and an adjustable recoil pad—as well as a .22 conversion kit for economical practice.

Browning
Browning is offering a couple new versions of its existing big game rifles. New to the BAR line of semiauto rifles is the BAR Mk3 DBM Wood, which comes with a detachable box magazine, lightweight alloy receiver, Picatinny rail and a Grade 2 Turkish walnut stock with an oil finish. With its 18-inch barrel, this .308 semiauto is ideal for hunting hogs or deer, especially from a blind where its short overall length is a major benefit. Also new to the BAR rifle family is the Mark III Hell’s Canyon Speed. Like the Mk3 DBM Wood, it comes with a lightweight alloy receiver, but it also receives a Burnt Bronze Cerakote metal finish and an A-TACS AU shim-adjustable synthetic stock similar to the Hell’s Canyon versions of Browning’s X-Bolt rifles.
Speaking of X-Bolts, the eye-catching new X-Bolt Pro Tungsten, which is featured on the cover, has been added to Browning’s flagship bolt-action line. It comes with a second-generation carbon fiber stock and a stainless steel barreled action. The lightweight sporter contour barrel is fluted and comes with a muzzle brake and a thread protector. Both the stock and the barreled-action are covered with an ultra-durable Cerakote Tungsten finish.

Christensen Arms
Like other Christensen Arms rifles, the new Traverse comes loaded with high-tech, shooter-friendly features, including a Monte Carlo carbon-fiber composite stock, and a light contour Christensen Arms carbon-fiber-wrapped barrel with stainless steel side baffle brake. There’s both an optics rail and a Picatinny bottom rail for mounting a bipod. Despite its beefy looks, the Traverse weighs in at as little as 7.3 pounds, making it suitable for backcountry hunters.
christensenarms.com
CMMG Resolute 300 Series. (CMMG photo)
CMMG
When I wrote about the .350 Legend cartridge in the last issue, I mentioned CMMG was quick to chamber it in one of its AR-15s. The Resolute will be available in the company’s 100, 200 and 300 series rifles. All come with 16-inch barrels, and weights are under the 6.5-pound mark. The higher you go in the series, the more features you get—from the A2/M4 styling of the 100 to full-boat Cerakote and the Geissele trigger on the 300.
cmmg.com

CVA
A company long associated with blackpowder rifles has unveiled its first bolt-action centerfire. The affordably priced CVA Cascade comes with a blued 4140 carbon steel barrel threaded, an adjustable charcoal gray synthetic stock, detachable box magazine and other hunter-friendly features. It also comes with dual front sling studs for mounting a bipod.
cvafirearmsusa.com/
CZ-USA
CZ-USA is concentrating more on its shotguns and pistols, but it’s not ignoring rifles. The 557 American push-feed rifle is now available in both 6.5 Creedmoor and 7mm-08 Rem. The American can be had with either a wood stock or a synthetic stock, and it’s a well-built and capable big game rifle.
czfirearms.com/en-us
Kimber
The Mountain Ascent is now available with a Gore Optifade SubAlpine camo stock. It’s a superb hunting rifle that’s lighter than almost any other production rifle in the price range. It comes with a reinforced carbon-fiber stock that is pillar bedded and an adjustable trigger. The 22-inch sporter contour barrel is fluted, and the bolt knob and handle have weight-saving cuts. As a result these guns weigh between four pounds, 13 ounces and six pounds, seven ounces depending upon caliber. The metalwork is stainless steel and features a satin stainless finish.
www.kimberamerica.com
Marlin
Big bore fans will appreciate the return of the Marlin 1895 444, a classic lever gun chambered in Marlin’s own hard-hitting .444 cartridge. This gun comes with a good-looking American black walnut stock, 22-inch Ballard rifling barrel with a 1:20 twist, traditional semi-buckhorn adjustable sights, and a polished blue finish. You can read RifleShooter’s review in the July/August issue or online at RifleShooterMag.com.
Also new from Marlin is the Model 336C Curly Maple rifle, which, as the name implies, comes with a classically styled curly maple stock. Like other 336 rifles, it features a 20-inch barrel with Micro-Groove rifling, adjustable semi-buckhorn rear sights, polished blued finish, and a six-round tubular magazine. It’s a stylish look for this most classic of all woods rifles.

On the opposite end of the spectrum is the new Dark, which features a black stock, Parkerized metalwork and a threaded 16.25-inch barrel. An XS ghost-ring rear sight sits at the back of an optics rail. It also sports a big-loop lever wrapped in paracord.
www.marlinfirearms.com
Mossberg
Mossberg is adding the Patriot Predator Cerakote/Strata Camo, which is reviewed elsewhere in this issue. Also new is the MVP LR Thunder Ranch. Although it has a tactical look and feel, at eight pounds and with an overall length under 40 inches the MVP LR Thunder Ranch is light enough and compact enough to make it an excellent option for big game hunting. It features a benchrest stock with adjustable comb in 
Kuiu
 Vias camo, a 20-inch threaded, fluted bull barrel and a top rail.
Mossberg has also expanded the caliber offerings for its Vortex Scoped Combo rifles (with both walnut and synthetic stocks), the Patriot Synthetic Cerakote rifles, Youth Super Bantam Scoped combos and Night Train guns. The company is also offering the existing Patriot Walnut, Patriot Synthetic and Patriot Predator rifles chambered in .450 Bushmaster.
mossberg.com

Nosler M48 Mountain Carbon. (Nosler photo)
Nosler
New this year from Nosler is the M48 Mountain Carbon, a high-tech, lightweight hunting rifle with a long list of impressive features. At the heart of this gun is Nosler’s push-feed M48 action and a match-grade, cut-rifled 24-inch free-floated carbon-fiber-wrapped barrel with a light sendero contour. The barreled action is glass and aluminum pillar bedded in a 100-percent carbon fiber Aramid-reinforced Mountain Hunter stock with textured grip panels in Granite Green. These rifles weigh in at just six pounds, making them light enough for hunting in steep country, and they also come with a Cerakote Tungsten Gray finish on the action and bottom metal, a Timney trigger, two-position safety, 5/8x24 threaded muzzle and a hinged floorplate.
nosler.com
Remington Model Seven SS HS. (Remington photo)
Remington
The new Model 783 Varmint, despite the name, is offered in a number of calibers suitable for big game hunting and comes with a heavy-contour 26-inch button-rifled carbon steel barrel, a brown laminate beavertail stock with dual front sling studs and a top rail for mounting optics. Despite its heavy barrel and long-range design, this rifle weighs in at just 7.5 pounds and will set you back just $625. You can read the full rundown elsewhere in this issue.

, which graced our March/April cover. You can still read the review at RifleShooterMag.com.

Rise Armament
Oklahoma-based Rise is offering the new 1121XR Precision Rifle in 6.5 Creedmoor. This standout AR rifle features a black-nitride bolt carrier group manufactured by the company, a 22-inch 416R stainless steel button-rifled 1:8-twist barrel that’s been air gauged and tested, RA-701 Compensator, RA-535 single-stage 3.5-pound trigger, a Magpul PRS adjustable stock, and much more. You can choose from black, FDE or foliage green color schemes. This rifle weighs in at 9.7 pounds.
risearmament.com
Ruger Hawkeye Alaskan. (Ruger photo)
Ruger
Ruger’s Hawkeye Long Range Target is engineered for shooters who want to extend the effective range of their hunting rifle. It was on the cover of the May/June issue, and you can read the review at RifleShooterMag.com. That rifle was in the initial .300 Win. Mag. chambering, and it’s now available in 6.5 Creedmoor and 6.5 PRC.
Ruger is also reintroducing the popular M77 Hawkeye Alaskan. It has a black Hogue Overmolded stock and stainless steel metalwork with a Ruger satin finish. It comes with express sights (windage-adjustable shallow V rear, large white dot front), a barrel band sling stud, Mauser-style action, three-position safety, LC6 trigger and a removable muzzle brake with a weight-matched thread cap. Barrel length is 20 inches, and weight is eight pounds.
Ruger also adds to its American line of rifles this year with a Ranch model in .350 Legend. The Ranch sports a flat dark earth synthetic stock, 16.4-inch threaded barrel and an optics rail. It’s available in both standard and compact lengths.

Savage Arms
The newest member of Savage’s MSR line of semiauto rifles is the MSR-10 Precision, which comes with a 22.5-inch heavy-contour stainless steel barrel with 5R rifling, an adjustable gas block with lock nut, a two-stage trigger, 18-inch ARCA handguard, a Magpul PRS stock and a TangoDown Battlegrip Flip Grip. The MSR 10 Precision is available in 6mm and 6.5 Creedmoor and .308 Win.
Savage also revamped its Axis and Axis II models. The Axis receives a new, ergonomic adjustable stock, button-rifled barrel, screw-in headspacing and a floating bolt head, and there are three new Axis model from which to choose: the Axis Compact, Axis Left Hand and Axis Compact Left Hand.
Axis II rifles also receive new, modern synthetic stocks with adjustable spacers as well as thread-in headspacing, a floating bolt head and the Savage AccuTrigger. There’s also a new compact version.
There are a number of brand-new Axis XP rifle/scope combo guns available that come with mounted and bore-sighted Weaver 3-9x40 scopes and the new Axis stock. These include compact versions of the Axis XP package rifles available with black, Mossy Oak Break-Up Country or Muddy Girl synthetic stocks plus a new Axis XP Stainless .

There have also been several additions to the 110 rifle family line including three new Model 110 Apex package rifles: the 110 Apex Hunter XP, which comes with blued carbon steel barrel and receiver (also in left-hand or a Muddy Girl camo stock); the 110 Apex Storm XP which features stainless steel metalwork; and the 110 Apex Predator XP, which comes with a Mossy Oak synthetic stock and a fluted, threaded barrel. You can read a review of the Apex Hunter elsewhere in this issue.
The new Savage 110 High Country features the company’s adjustable AccuFit system, a synthetic AccuStock in TrueTimber Strata with soft-touch inserts, tan PVD finish on the metalwork, a spiral-fluted bolt and barrel, threaded muzzle and more.
In honor of its 125th anniversary, Savage is also offering a Limited Edition Model 110 that features a High-grade Monte Carlo walnut stock with a polished finish and ebony fore end cap, and engraved receiver, box magazine and grip cap.
Only 1,894 of these rifles will be built, and they are available in five calibers—including .250 and .300 Savage—by custom order through Savage. Weatherby
The new Vanguard Badlands features a 24-inch (26 for magnums) No. 2 contour barrel and a push-feed action with a one-piece machined fluted bolt body and a fully-enclosed bolt sleeve. All the metalwork is treated with a durable Burnt Bronze Cerakote finish and a Badlands Approach camo stock with a signature 
Weatherby
 Monte Carlo comb. These guns come with two-stage triggers, three-position safeties, and they weigh in at 7.5 pounds.
The Mark V Camilla Ultra Lightweight is a 5.75-pound rifle with a spiderweb laminate stock that was engineered with input from top female shooters. The No. 1 contour stainless steel barrel is fluted to further reduce weight.
New additions to the Mark V family include the Subalpine and Subalpine RC (Range Certified), both of which come with hand-laid, raised-comb composite stocks in Gore Optifade Subalpine camo and fluted barrels that range from 22 to 26 inches depending upon caliber and have a flat dark earth Cerakote finish. These rifles weigh between 5.75 and 6.75 pounds, and 6.5-.300 Wby. Mag. and .300 Wby. Mag. versions come equipped with a muzzle brake. The RC versions are $500 more.
weatherby.com
Wilson Combat
This year Wilson Combat is building out its caliber offerings, primarily with the Tactical Hunter—from deer-capable .224 rounds in the form of the .22 Nosler and .224 Valkyrie to midweight punchers like the 6.5 Grendel and the .300 Ham’r. The Tactical Hunter features a lightweight flattop billet upper receiver and billet lower as well. It’s got a match-grade barrel (fluted or non-fluted) and a rifle-length gas system and adjustable gas block. Weight runs in the seven pound, 11 ounce neighborhood.

Winchester
Winchester is offering two brand-new versions of the XPR bolt-action rifles this year: the XPR Hunter Kryptek Highlander and the XPR Hunter TrueTimber Strata. As the names imply, these rifles come with synthetic stocks in their namesake camo pattern, and both of these rifles are equipped with Permacote flat dark earth finishes on their metalwork. There’s also a new Model 1892 Large Loop carbine, a sleek pistol-caliber lever gun with a 20-inch barrel and an oversized lever loop.
The biggest news at Winchester, though, might be cartridge expansions. Several Model 70 rifles—Featherweight, Featherweight Compact, Super Grade, Extreme Weather SS, and Coyote Light SR—are now available in 6.5 Creedmoor. Virtually all the XPRs, including the two new models listed above, are available in .350 Legend.
www.winchesterguns.com
