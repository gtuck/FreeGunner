While not yet a household name in the U.S., Turkish maker 
Tisas
 continues to win over customers by offering quality, feature-rich guns that are affordably priced. The PX-9 line of Tisas 9mm pistols exemplifies that affordable quality. There are eight PX-9 models of varying sizes and shapes—the latest extension of the PX-9 line is the 2.0 Duty Comp.
As the name implies, the Duty Comp is a 
duty-size pistol
 that’s compensated. Whether you’re looking for an affordable segue into competitive shooting, a home-defense gun or a concealed-carry gun that you’re willing to dress around, the PX-9 2.0 Duty Comp deserves your attention.
The Duty Comp is a polymer-frame gun with a QPQ-finished 4140 steel slide and barrel. The slide is beveled, with serrations both front and rear. The 4.11-inch barrel is button rifled, with a large port on top that aligns with a port in the slide. The ports form an integral compensator, which diverts gases up, forcing the muzzle down. The result is less muzzle flip for faster, more accurate follow-up shots.

Some compensators work, and some seem more cosmetic than functional. The Duty Comp’s compensator worked. But the Duty Comp doesn’t rely solely on the compensator to mitigate recoil. It also affords you a customizable grip for better control.
Frame and Fitment
The undercut trigger guard and beavertail aid in recoil control, as does the shelf on the takedown lever. The flat-face aluminum trigger broke cleanly at four pounds. (Photo courtesy of Yamil Sued)
The Duty Comp’s grip is full size, meaning there’s room for your pinky. The undercut trigger guard and beavertail enable you to choke up on the grip to counteract recoil forces, which want to flip the muzzle up.
Like many polymer-frame pistols, the Duty Comp comes with interchangeable backstraps. But with the PX-9 series, Tisas has taken grip customization a step further by offering interchangeable side panels. In all, there are 27 possible grip configurations.

The supplied aluminum magazine well speeds reloads, but it’s removable if concealment is your priority. (Photo courtesy of Yamil Sued)
A pistol’s weight also affects the felt recoil it produces. All things being equal, a lighter gun will be harder to control. At 1.75 pounds, the Duty Comp is a healthy weight. Its polymer frame keeps it from feeling like a boat anchor in your hands or on your hip, yet it’s heavy enough to shoot comfortably, without feeling like it’s going to jump out of your hands with every shot.
The Duty Comp has texturing around the grip and on both sides of the frame, where you can index your support side thumb to ensure consistent placement and stage your trigger finger when not firing. There’s texturing on the controls and even on the trigger.
Controls and Handling
The compensated slide and barrel divert gases up to mitigate recoil, making this a flat-shooting pistol. (Photo courtesy of Yamil Sued)
The serrated, ambidextrous slide release sits atop a raised polymer border to prevent accidental activation, which would prevent the slide from locking to the rear when the pistol is empty. The reversible magazine release is knurled, as is the trigger face and the takedown lever, which doubles as a “gas pedal” for right-handed shooters. Applying inward and downward thumb pressure on this enlarged lever can further mitigate muzzle flip for faster shot-to-shot recovery.
Of course, the most important control on a pistol is the trigger. The Duty Comp features an aluminum, flat-face trigger in a bright blue housing. There was minimal take-up to the wall, followed by a clean break at about 90 degrees, requiring four pounds of pressure. Reset was positive, although not as distinct as with some striker-fired pistols.
The Duty Comp has polymer sights. The rear sight is blacked out and serrated, while the front sight includes a white dot. But who runs open sights on pistols these days? Not me if I can help it, which is why I appreciate the Duty Comp’s slide cut. It will accommodate direct-mounted optics with the Trijicon RMR footprint.

The Duty Comp ships with an 18-round and a 20-round magazine. It’s also compatible with 
SIG Sauer P226 magazines
. The supplied aluminum magazine well makes the Duty Comp a little harder to conceal, but for competition or home defense it’s a great way to improve reloads. And if concealment is in fact your priority, you can remove the well.
Accuracy Testing
(Accuracy results provided by the author)
A loaded-chamber indicator protrudes above the slide when there is a round in the chamber. A striker status indicator displays a red dot at the back of the slide when the pistol is cocked.
For testing, I mounted an 
AmeriGlo Haven optic
 on the Duty Comp, and the gun performed well in both accuracy and offhand shooting. It loved the 
100 grain Black Hills HoneyBadger
, as you can see in the chart. Offhand, I was able to repeatedly mag-dump ragged-hole groups from seven yards.
The Duty Comp ships in a hard case with an inside-the-waistband holster, the two magazines, the grip panel kit, Allen wrenches, a punch, a cleaning kit and an owner’s manual. It comes with a one-year warranty and a lifetime service plan.
