Smith & Wesson Model 1854 Upgrades: XS Sights LVR-HG, Rail & Ghost Ring

We put XS Sights' LVR-HG handguard, Lever Rail and Ghost Ring sights to the test on a .44 Magnum Smith & Wesson Model 1854.

 (Photo by Antonio Acitelli)

October 01, 2026

By Antonio Acitelli

I have a particular fondness for lever-action rifles, and an even softer spot for the Smith & Wesson Model 1854. I’ve owned some lever guns before getting my hands on the 1854, but it was the first in my collection to feature modern touches like M-LOK attachment points and a threaded barrel, and I find myself mounting a suppressor on this gun nine times out of ten when I use it. My own model is chambered in .44 Magnum, which is my personal cartridge of choice for the platform. Yes, there are both harder-hitting and softer-shooting cartridges to choose from, but .44 Magnum strikes a good balance between the two for me, and suppressing it is a joy. When XS Sights revealed some upgrades for the 1854, specifically for the .44 Magnum version, I was interested.
The Hardware
The end of the hand guard fits snugly over the end of the tube magazine, and has a neat "1854" marking to boot. (Photo by Antonio Acitelli)
XS currently offers three distinct add-ons for the 1854: 
the LVR-HG
 handguard, 
Lever Rail optic mount, and large Ghost Ring sights
. The handguard is designed with the .44 Magnum’s 19.25-inch barrel in mind and extends to cover the entire tube magazine. A cut has been made into the side toward the forend to allow users to remove the magazine tube as needed. Nine M-LOK mounting points can be found on either side, with 10 more along the bottom of the handguard.
The original 1854 rail (bottom) in comparison to the new XS rail (top). (Photo by Antonio Acitelli)
The rail provided by XS measures about 10 inches long, making it twice the length of the standard 1854 rail, and integrates the rear Ghost Ring sight. The Ghost Ring itself is similar to that on the original 1854, though the front post now sits higher and has a clearly visible white line marked down the center. This new height allows it to sit above most suppressors.
Installation
The hand guard comes with multiple screw sets, Loctite, and a basic Allen wrench for installation. (Photo by Antonio Acitelli)
Out of the box, the LVR-HG comes with some screws, Loctite, and an Allen wrench. While these will work fine for the task at hand, I opted for a 
Real Avid Smart Torq wrench
 to make things easier. Installing the handguard is straightforward, with a 
tutorial video available on XS Sights’ YouTube channel
. There was only one step that gave me some trouble, which involved sticking a few washers in place with blue Loctite before sliding the rail over them. It’s a snug fit, so the friction of the rail moved them out of position a few times, forcing me to start the whole process over. Other than that, I spent only around 20 minutes on the actual installation.

Installing the top rail is as simple as removing a few flat head screws. (Photo by Antonio Acitelli)
Replacing the sight rail is even easier, as you’ll only need a flathead screwdriver or torque wrench. The front sight was trickier. The sight post itself is simple enough, but the new white-stripe front post needs to be pushed into place. While the tutorial mentions that you may need to file it a bit if it doesn’t push in smoothly, I found that I needed to do some fairly aggressive filing to trim it enough to press into place. Using my gun vise as an improvised pushing tool, the post went in and stayed in place.
Tricking Out the 1854
The completed build included an INFORCE Gen 3 WML light and a BANISH 46 HNT Ti suppressor. (Photo by Antonio Acitelli)
My first addition to the rifle was a hand stop midway along the length of the handguard. For this, I used a standard Magpul M-LOK hand stop kit and cut away the forward stop and midsection. Near the barrel, I installed an aluminum M-LOK-to-Picatinny rail adapter. For testing, I pulled an Inforce WML from my parts drawer, and it fit nicely. After that, I threaded on a 
BANISH HNT 46 Ti suppressor
.
The FastFire E matches the square profile of the new hand guard, and sat snugly on an elevated mount that grants access to the ghost ring sights. (Photo by Antonio Acitelli)
The new sights just eked over the top of the can, so I decided to take advantage of my new top-rail space. For this, I installed a Burris FastFire E on an elevated mount. I chose this setup because it not only gives plenty of clearance over the suppressor and provides a solid field of view, but also allows me to accommodate both suppressed and unsuppressed shooting. In previous testing with this rifle and suppressor combination, I’ve found that adding a suppressor shifts the point of impact (POI) about an inch down at 50 yards. With this new setup, I can use the irons for unsuppressed use and the FastFire E for suppressed shooting.

At the Range
At the range, the 1854 was a breeze to shoot, and benefitted from the longer handguard and handstop. Testing was done with 
Hornady
 LEVERevolution 240 gr. FTX and Hornady 300 gr. XTP Custom. (Photo by Antonio Acitelli)
The LVR-HG turns the 1854 into a tank, in the best possible way. Having access to the full length of the rifle for hand placement is freeing, and the added hand stop allows me to hold the rifle more firmly into my shoulder, which offsets the added weight. I felt that I could easily get away with adding some more accessories to this rifle as well, like ammo quivers or a barricade stop. The BANISH 46 HNT Ti performed excellently. I’ve never handed someone this rifle-and-suppressor combination without seeing them smile while shooting it.
Final Impressions
If you’re looking for more space on the 1854, or maybe want a new look for it, the upgrades from XS Sights are a great option. I may swap around the sights and parts on this rifle in the future, but the handguard, rail, and Ghost Ring sights are staying.

Antonio Acitelli
