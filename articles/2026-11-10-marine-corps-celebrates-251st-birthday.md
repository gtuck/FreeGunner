Marine Corps Celebrates 251st Birthday

In honor of the USMC, we took a look back at a few iconic marine sidearms we've reviewed.

 The Colt Rail Gun won the contract replace the Marines' MEUSOC 1911 pistols, and was designated the M45A1. Chambered in .45 ACP with an 8-round magazine as standard. (Photo by Antonio Acitelli)

November 10, 2026

By Guns & Ammo Staff

On November 10, 2026, the United States Marine Corps celebrates its 251st birthday, commemorating the November 10, 1775, resolution by the Second Continental Congress to establish two battalions of Marines. More than two and a half centuries later, the Corps remains one of the world's most recognizable military institutions, with a legacy built on service, sacrifice and an enduring commitment to its motto: 
Semper Fidelis
.
Throughout its history, the Marine Corps has continually adapted its weapons and equipment to meet the demands of the battlefield. Few firearms, however, have maintained a connection to Marine Corps identity quite like John Moses Browning's Model 1911. From its early military service to the highly specialized pistols carried by modern Marine special operations units, the 1911 has earned a distinctive place in the Corps' small-arms history.
In recognition of the Marine Corps' 251st birthday, Guns & Ammo is revisiting some stories that explore that legacy.
First is our in-depth look at the 
M45 MEUSOC 1911
, the custom-built .45 ACP pistol that served Marine Expeditionary Unit special operations forces from 1985 until 2012. Developed by the Marine Corps' Precision Weapons Section at Quantico, Virginia, these pistols transformed surplus M1911A1 frames into specialized fighting handguns. Featuring carefully fitted components, custom sights, enhanced controls and match-grade barrels, the M45 became synonymous with the Force Reconnaissance Marines and, later, Marine Raiders who carried it.

Written by retired Marine Lt. Col. Justin Dyal, M45 MEUSOC 1911: Full Review explores the pistol's development, evolution and service history, including firsthand recollections of carrying the handgun in Afghanistan. The article also examines the transition to the Colt M45A1, which succeeded the original MEUSOC pistol in 2012.
But the story of Marine handguns isn't complete without recognizing the people behind them.
In 
Unsung No More: Marine Corps Armorer's Legacy Honored
, Guns & Ammo's Eric R. Poole examines the remarkable career of Master Gunnery Sgt. Paul Blazejowski. A gifted Marine armorer and competitive shooter, Blazejowski helped build and maintain the precision handguns that supported generations of Marine Corps marksmanship competitors. His work influenced military pistol development, and his contributions extended well beyond the shooting team, helping advance the craftsmanship and technical standards associated with the 1911.
