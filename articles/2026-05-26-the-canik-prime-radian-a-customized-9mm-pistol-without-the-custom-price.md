The Canik Prime Radian: A Customized 9mm Pistol Without the Custom Price

Canik's newest 9mm pistol takes the Mete MC9 Prime to the next level with add-ons from Radian Weapons.

 The Prime Radian is built on the well-established Mete MC9 Prime platform. (Michael Anschuetz photo)

By Keith Wood

Canik was an unknown quantity to U.S. gun buyers until its handguns appeared on the scene in 2009. Since then, those guns have become extremely popular. Popularity breeds customization, and aftermarket parts for Canik handguns soon became available. Canik embraced those options, resulting in a collaboration with Radian Weapons. The Prime Radian is the best of both worlds right out of the box: the proven Canik design with high-quality upgrades from Radian.
Mete MC9 Prime Foundation
The Prime Radian is built on the well-established 
Mete MC9 Prime
 platform. This is a compact handgun designed for concealed carry. Size-wise, the MC9 sits somewhere between the Glock 19 and SIG P365, which is a useful place to be.
The frame is polymer and serialized, and a short rail section is molded into the frame’s dust cover. The Prime Radian’s frame is nicely ergonomic, with a high undercut trigger guard allowing for a high hold on the pistol. The grip panels are textured with tiny dots that provide an excellent grip. There are also textured panels on the frame for the support-hand thumb to rest. The back of the grip is extended, and there is little to no risk of being bitten by the reciprocating slide. Overall, the grip is comfortable and functional.
The gun is optic ready and features Night Fision tritium sights. The slide-lock lever is ambidextrous, and the high-undercut frame aids in control. (Michael Anschuetz photo)
Radian Weapons Upgrades
Three interchangeable backstraps are are included. Two are polymer with texturing that matches the grip, each with a different amount of arch. The third is made by Radian and machined from aluminum with deep relief cuts for an aggressive grip. The bottom edge of the backstrap dovetails onto the extended Radian magazine well.

The mag well flares outward to allow for faster reloads but isn’t so large it will unduly compromise concealment. The front lip of the mag well actually enhances the grip, in my opinion—offering a lip to prevent the pinky finger from sliding off the bottom of the frame.
This backstrap and mag well combo is a $150 upgrade when purchased from Radian for other handgun models. At this point, it’s available exclusively as part of the Prime Radian package when it comes to Canik handguns.
The Prime Radian features an extended, ambidextrous slide stop that is easy to manipulate with the strong hand but doesn’t get in the way, unlike the slide stops on some models. The magazine release is strong-side-only but within easy reach.

The Trigger
The Prime Radian has a flat trigger with a checkered safety lever serving as the trigger’s face. This is a unique feature that I don’t recall seeing elsewhere. The checkering is fine enough that I don’t see it becoming abrasive, but it’s aggressive enough to keep the pad of the finger in place during recoil.
The trigger pull has a great deal of travel before hitting a wall, which I generally think is a good thing on a carry gun. On my sample, there was a good bit of creep before the trigger broke at 4.4 pounds. The trigger face was at the 90-degree position when the pistol fired. The reset was short and very distinct.
Radian Barrel and Compensator
From afar, the Prime Radian looks as though it has the same 3.64-inch barrel as the Mete MC9 Prime. In reality, though, the slide is shortened to allow for the Radian Ramjet barrel and Afterburner compensator. Although the overall profile is the same, the front 0.6 inch of the slide’s profile is actually the compensator.
The Radian Ramjet paired with the Afterburner comp makes for a flat-shooting pistol. (Michael Anschuetz photo)
Compensators have become almost a staple on defensive firearms thanks to their ability to mitigate muzzle rise and keep the sights on target. Let’s be honest, though: part of their popularity is that they look cool.
Both the Ramjet barrel and Afterburner compensator are machined from hardened 416R stainless steel. The barrel is ramped, with a fully supported chamber and loaded-chamber indicator.

The outside of the barrel is cut with five distinctive flats. Two large ports are cut into the barrel near the muzzle, and these match the corresponding cuts on the compensator. A notch is cut on the barrel’s six o’clock position, and a tapered Torx screw holds the compensator in place. A V-shape cut ensures the comp is timed at the correct position. The Radian barrel and compensator retail for $390 when purchased separately.
The Prime Radian’s slide is aggressively machined with various cuts and angles that show off the maker’s machining prowess. There are deep slide serrations at both the front and rear, and a window is cut into the top of the slide to expose the barrel beneath.
Other Features
The loaded-chamber indicator is pinned into the slide at the rear of the generously cut ejection port and is both visual and tactile. The large external extractor is located on the right side of the slide.
Both the front and rear Night Fision night sights are dovetailed into the slide. The front sight, which sits on the slide behind the compensator, features a large and highly visible orange ring surrounding an embedded tritium lamp. The rear sight has a serrated black face and a square notch with tritium dots on either side. The sights are drift-adjustable for windage.
The slide is optic-ready, with a steel plate covering the mounting cut, which is on the Shield RMSc footprint. This allows for a direct mount with optics on that footprint, and the factory irons should co-witness. M3x10 mounting screws are included, although I did not mount an optic to this gun for testing.
The Radian mag well flares but doesn’t unduly compromise concealment. The front lip enhances the grip by keeping the pinky from sliding off. (Michael Anschuetz photo)
The Prime Radian uses a steel-bodied magazine with a polymer base plate and follower. The base plate just clears the bottom of the extended magazine well, providing enough surface to fully seat the magazine without adding unnecessary length that would affect concealability.
The standard magazines have a capacity of 17 rounds, and lower-capacity magazines are available for restricted jurisdictions. The magazines are of excellent quality with bodies that are extremely sturdy.
Disassembly
The recoil system on the Prime Radian uses dual captive recoil springs along with a two-piece steel guide rod. With some handgun models I hesitate to discuss disassembly, as the process is often identical to other similar pistols. That’s not the case with Canik handguns.

Advertisement

One might look at the dual takedown levers on the Prime Radian’s frame and assume it takes down the same way as a Glock. But I learned firsthand that assumption can lead to a frustrating scenario.
Begin the process by unloading the pistol—dropping the magazine and ensuring the chamber is empty. Dry-fire the unloaded handgun in a safe direction, retract the slide slightly and pull down the takedown tabs. Move the slide forward far enough for the frame rails to clear, and remove the slide by pulling upward.
Remove the recoil spring assembly. The Torx screw must then be removed from the compensator, allowing the comp to slide off the barrel. At this point the barrel can be removed from the slide.
Reassembly begins with reversing the process, but this is where things get tricky. Attempting to front-load the slide assembly onto the frame rails will tie up the pistol; the frame-mounted ejector gets in the way.
The slide assembly must be placed back onto the frame the same way it was removed, then slid back into position. It’s simple once you get the hang of it, but doing it wrong will result in a panicked YouTube search for “Canik slide stuck.”

The miniature pistol included in the case is actually a tool kit that serves as a wrench and contains optic mounting hardware as well as Torx bits. (Michael Anschuetz photo)
The Prime Radian ships in a high-quality hard plastic travel case with accessories including an inside-the-waistband holster, two interchangeable backstraps, basic cleaning tools and a pin punch. Canik includes a miniature version of the handgun as well, and this is actually a functional wrench and toolbox containing optic mounting screws, lock washers and two Torx bits. The muzzle is a bit holder, and everything fits inside its tiny magazine well for storage.
Range Time
Shooting the Prime Radian was a pleasure. The sample was 100 percent reliable with the three loads tested. For a defensive handgun, that level of reliability should be non-negotiable.
Recoil was very manageable, and the compensator had an obvious effect when it came to taming muzzle rise. The handgun stayed on target very well during rapid strings of fire. Interestingly, I did not notice much in terms of increased muzzle blast, which is usually an unfortunate byproduct of a compensator.
Middle-aged eyes and iron sights don’t blend as well as I’d like, but I was still able to shoot the Prime Radian reasonably well for the accuracy assessment. The 115-grain CCI Blazer loads printed a bit high at 25 yards, but the other two options shot to point of aim. The Prime Radian’s trigger was pretty creepy when it came to shooting groups, but that became far less noticeable when I stepped away from the bench.
This handgun points well, allows for a high and firm grip, tames muzzle rise and has excellent sights. When it comes to shooting a handgun well, there isn’t much more to ask for.

Final Thoughts
The Prime Radian is a great option for shooters looking for an upgraded handgun for defensive or competition use. Instead of buying a factory handgun and tossing aside perfectly good parts in order to install aftermarket components, this handgun offers them right out of the box.
If you add up the base handgun’s suggested retail with the price of the upgrades, the Prime Radian offers a ton of value. It’s essentially a $300 discount compared to the sum of its parts.
