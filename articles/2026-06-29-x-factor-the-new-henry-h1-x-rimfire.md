X-Factor: The New Henry H1 X Rimfire

The new H1 X rifle from Henry offers many of the same features found on their popular X-series centerfire rifles in a scaled-down, fun-to-shoot rimfire package. 

June 29, 2026

By Brad Fitzpatrick

The Henry name carries more than 150 years of American firearms history. The modern company behind it today isn’t resting on heritage alone. Henry isn’t afraid to break with convention when it comes to new gun designs, and that’s certainly the case with the company’s new H1 X rimfire lever rifle, a gun that rewrites the rules for what a lever-action .22 should look like.
Much of the H1 X’s design was borrowed from Henry’s centerfire X-series rifles. Like its larger cousins, the H1 X comes with a black polymer buttstock and forearm that’s designed to survive years of hard use. The stock comes with two spacers that allow owners to easily adjust the rifle’s length of pull from 13.5 to 14.0 inches. The H1 X’s pistol grip offers a full, comfortable grip while shooting, and there’s texturing on both the pistol grip and forearm to allow for a secure hold. The forearm is trim—just 1.3 inches at the widest point—and comes with M-LOK attachment points at the 3, 6, and 9 o’clock positions.
The H1 X’s alloy receiver comes with a gloss black finish that looks good alongside the rich bluing on the 16.5-inch round, blued steel barrel. ½ x 28 muzzle threads make it easy to mount a suppressor or other muzzle device to this rifle. H1 X rifles are chambered in either .22 WMR or .22 LR/L/S. Both guns have tubular magazines. The .22 WXR mag holds 10 rounds while the .22 LR/L/S versions have a capacity of 13 .22 Long Rifle cartridges, 14 .22 Longs, or 18 .22 Shorts.
Many of today’s shooters want a scope or reflex sight on their lever-action, and mounting an optic on the H1 X is simple thanks to the included Picatinny rail on the receiver. The 10 slot rail measures roughly 4.5 inches long, and that offers plenty of space to mount a variable optic or a red or green dot sight, but for those who prefer irons the Henry comes with an adjustable rear sight with green fiber optic inserts and a red fiber optic bar front sight.

One of the features that’s missing from this rifle is an external tang or crossbolt safety. In recent years, many lever actions have adopted external manual safeties, but the beauty of the lever-action design is that you can easily manipulate and verify the condition of the rifle thanks to the external hammer. To that end, the H1 X comes with a quarter cock feature. In the quarter cock position, the rifle is safe, and that allows for instant visual verification of the rifle’s condition. I much prefer the Henry’s quarter cock hammer safety to crossbolt or tang safeties.

Henry’s H1 X comes with a long list of premium features. That includes a trigger that breaks around five pounds and helps improve the rifle’s accuracy and sling studs that are recessed in the polymer stock. The comb is also higher than some other lever-action .22s, and this allows shooters to use either iron sights or an optic while maintaining contact between their cheek and the rifle comb.

Weighing in at just five pounds and with an overall length of just 34.5 inches, the H1 X is an easy rifle to carry, and even small shooters can handle the rifle’s weight effectively. It also makes the rifle easy to trek with all day in the squirrel woods or haul in a backpack or bug-out bag.
The MSRP for the H1 X .22 LR is $590 while the MSRP for the .22 WMR version is set at $690, but that’s an excellent value for an American-made rimfire that offers the quality and versatility of the henry.

Range Testing
For the evaluation I was sent a production version of the H1 X in .22 LR and I topped with a Leupold DeltaPoint Pro red dot optic. I could just have easily mounted a variable scope on the Henry’s Picatinny rail, but I decided on the red dot simply because it adds so little weight to the rifle
Now that rimfire ammunition is once again available and reasonably priced, you can spend hours on the range plinking away with the Henry for just a few dollars. It’s also lots of fun to shoot because it’s smooth, reliable, and accurate. The bolt has dual extractors and a fixed ejector that toss empties well clear of the action, and lever cycles through rounds without any hiccups. What’s more, the trigger is very good for a rimfire lever action. My test sample actually beat the five-pound manufacturer recommendation and broke at an average of 4.25 pounds for ten pulls on a Wheeler gauge.
With CCI Standard and Green Tag .22 LR ammunition, the Henry was capable of excellent accuracy, producing five-shot groups as small as .7 inches from 50 yards. The DeltaPoint Pro helps, providing a very precise aiming point and allowing for easy impact adjustments, but Henry deserves credit for outfitting the H1 X with a quality set of iron sights. The rear sight is screw adjustable for elevation, which allows for much finer point of impact adjustments that the traditional “stepped” rear sight layout that is commonly seen on .22 lever actions. There’s also a screw that, when loosened, allows the owner to drift the sight left or right in the dovetail for precise windage adjustments. The fiber optic sight layout is also easy to see, an especially important consideration when hunting small game in low light. Henry includes a wrench for making sight adjustments with each H1 X rifle.

The recoil pad is a removable hard plate, but since the Henry produces virtually no noticeable recoil a thick pad is unnecessary. Removing the plate requires the removal of two Phillips head screws. With the pad removed, you can add or remove the two length of pull (LOP) spacers. They’re rather tight, but that’s because they fit together precisely and, unlike the LOP spacers on some other rifles, they don’t detract from the clean lines of the rifle.
I test a lot of rifles, but I can’t remember when I’ve had as much fun as I did while shooting the Henry. This light, handy rifle runs through .22 ammo at a quick pace, and when I’d finished with the requisite accuracy testing I began a serious round of plinking. Some buyers believe that every new gun needs a “job,” whether that’s procuring meat or protecting your household from intruders. But my son and I had a ball shooting soda cans, clay targets, and popping balloons on our makeshift backyard range. In deference to our neighbors, who have never complained about me popping off rounds in my yard, I did add a Silencer Central Banish .22 suppressor to the rifle, and doing so drops the noise to a hearing-safe level. So quiet is the Henry with the suppressor that you could clearly hear the thwap! of those 40-grain lead round nose bullets smacking an empty soda can.
If you must have a practical reason to own a new gun the Henry offers plenty of those as well. The Henry is an outstanding small game and pest rifle, not only because of its excellent accuracy and smooth, fast-operating action but also because it is so light and durable. It’s easy to add a sling to this rifle, and at around five pounds with an optic this gun is easy to carry through the woods all day, even for small hunters. The rugged black furniture handles the rigors of the woods well. I’m a cottontail hunter, and few hunts will mar a stock worse than a few years in the briar thickets, but the Henry is designed to withstand just such abuse. And since the magazine holds over a dozen rounds of .22 LR you’ll have plenty of ammo on tap before you need to reload.
The Henry also makes a great pest control rifle. The M-LOK forearm allows you to add a bipod for stabilizing the rifle while shooting ground squirrels, and you can also mount a flashlight for hunting small game at night where legal. Also, because the Henry handles .22 Short ammunition, even if you don’t own a suppressor you can turn down the volume on this rifle. When I was young we had horses and cattle, and rats were a constant nuisance in the barn and around the corn cribs. With a light on the M-Lok forearm and a magazine tube full of .22 Short ammo I could have made short work of those vermin.
For work or play, the Henry H1 X is a fun and practical rifle that makes sense for anyone. It’s affordable to shoot and quite accurate, and the trigger is among the best I’ve ever encountered in a .22 lever action. And, like all Henry rifles, it’s made in America, which is just one more compelling reason to add this sleek lever-action rimfire to your rifle collection.
