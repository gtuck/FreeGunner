CANiK VOID-556K and -762 Suppressors: Reviewed

The VOID series suppressors are not only impressively quiet, but they are also reasonably priced when compared to the competition. Here's a full review.

 In 2026, Canik introduced four models of its VOID series. Manufactured in collaboration with Otter Creek Labs, offerings include cans for 9mm, 5.56mm and .30-caliber firearms. Guns & Ammo tested the VOID-556 and VOID-762. (Photo by Mark Fingar)

October 07, 2026

By Mark Fingar

Best known for its striker-­fire polymer pistols, including the TP9 and 
METE
 series, Canik has established itself as an accomplished name in the handgun market. With the launch of its VOID suppressor series, Canik plans to apply that same success model to the world of suppressors. By collaborating with 
Otter Creek Labs
, Canik has positioned itself smartly in a crowded field of suppressor offerings. Otter Creek Labs is known for manufacturing rugged, duty-­grade suppressors engineered for extreme conditions. 
“This partnership establishes the VOID line as a suite of high-­performance, duty-­ready tools designed for today’s discriminating customer,” said Jeff Rose, director of marketing for Century Arms and Canik USA. With an initial offering of four models, the VOID series supports the most widely used firearm platforms.
The VOID can be direct-thread mounted to a muzzle, but the rifle variants have a HUB interface to support other aftermarket mounting solutions. (Photo by Mark Fingar)
Meet the CANiK VOID Family
The 
VOID-­556
 and its shorter sibling, the 
VOID-­556K
, are made from durable 17-­4 stainless steel with a fully welded baffle stack. The material was selected for its ability to withstand the heat and pressure of high-­volume fire, especially when using short-­barreled rifles (SBRs). The full-­size VOID-­556 measures 6.1 inches and weighs 14.9 ounces, while the K model trims an inch and about 3 ounces. The K model is intended for shooters preferring maneuverability and a shorter overall length. The included 1/2x28 direct-thread adapter makes it easy to mount on bolt-­action rifles and gas-­operated semiautomatics alike.
The 
VOID-­762
 shares identical construction and length to the full-­size 556 model, but it’s bored for .30-­caliber projectiles and smaller. It is rated for cartridges ranging from .308 Winchester to .300 Winchester Magnum. An included 5/8x24 direct-thread adapter fits most threaded barrels in the .30-­caliber class.

The VOID-556K is lightweight and vented to provide low backpressure for gas-operated rifles. (Photo by Mark Fingar)
Different than the rifle variants, the VOID-­9 is manufactured using 3D-­printed titanium instead of the typical machined stainless-­steel used for rifle models. The metal 3D-­printing process allows for complex internal geometries that are impossible to create with traditional machining methods. Weighing just 7 1/2 ounces, the 9mm suppressor offers an incredibly lightweight option without sacrificing strength. It easily manages everything from standard 9mm pistols and pistol-­caliber carbines (PCCs) to both subsonic and supersonic .300 Blackout cartridges.
Rifle Suppressors
The VOID rifle suppressor bodies are a stack of welded baffles, creating a tubeless design that is threaded on each end to accept mounts and endcaps. Each baffle is shaped like a typical “K” baffle but with interlocking flow-­through geometry along the rim. When assembled and welded, the geometry is engineered to handle the inherent problem of backpressure. Traditional suppressors often increase the amount of gas directed back into the firearm’s action and toward the shooter’s face. Defeating backpressure not only lessens noxious gases surrounding the shooter, but it also minimizes the wear and tear on a firearm’s internal components. Inside the VOID, the flow-­through path begins at the blast baffle with a ring of ports that help direct and slow down the pressure. It ends in a ring of ports at the final baffle and eases out of the suppressor.
Also assisting with gas management are the VOID’s vented and non-­vented endcaps. The vented cap has a series of strategic ports that allow a portion of the gases to travel forward and easily out the front of the suppressor rather than being stalled and potentially forced back through the bore. The cost is a few decibels. The non-­vented cap is a solid endpiece with a caliber-­specific bore hole. It should be used when optimal sound reduction is desired.

Canik designed the VOID rifle line to use the HUB mounting interface. Included with each suppressor is a direct-­thread adapter, but users can swap the rear mount for almost any popular quick-­disconnect system on the market. 
With a manufacturer’s suggested retail price (MSRP) starting at $650 for rifle models, Canik has strategically positioning itself in the heart of the mainstream firearms market at a time when suppressor ownership is at an all-­time high. The VOID series suppressors are not only impressively quiet, but they are also reasonably priced when compared to the competition. By delivering premium features, durable construction, and top-­tier sound reduction, the VOID series offers high-­performance suppression to shooting enthusiasts. It’s a great time to be quiet.
