SAKO 90 Finnlight 7mm PRC: Full Review

An impressive list of features matched by a great range performance makes the 90 Finnlight an incredible value. Here's a full review.

 (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)

September 20, 2026

By Guns & Ammo Staff

SAKO is a premium rifle manufacturer based in Finland. 
The SAKO 90 Finnlight
 chambered in 7mm PRC is an example of a hunting rifle that has a combination of relevant. The “Finnlight” designation appeared in 1997 on the Model 75, configured as a stalking or mountain rifle with fluted barrel and lightweight stock. The 85 Finnlight followed in 2006, featuring a controlled-round-feed (CRF) action with six-lug bolt and adjustable single-stage trigger. The 90 arrived in 2025 with a rigid action and carbon-fiber-reinforced stock.
The aluminum bedding block captures the oblong recoil lug and a long action screw secures it to the stock. Even without the action screw, the lug-to-bedding block fit is tight. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
Key to performance of any bolt-­action rifle is the amount of engagement between the action screws and receiver. The Model 700 pattern is a reference because it is well-­known and has mediocre engagement that must be periodically checked. As a rifle throws odd flyers more frequently, first check the actions screws because they’ve probably come loose. The SAKO 90 has more action screw engagement than the M700, meaning its receiver stays firmly attached to the stock when the rifle is fired. The screws are less likely to work loose and accuracy is more consistent between groups.
The recoil lug is integral to the SAKO 90 receiver. Both action screws thread deeply into the receiver. With the stock removed, the user has easy access to the trigger adjustment screws. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
The SAKO 90 receiver is wide and flat on the bottom and has an integral oblong recoil lug that protrudes down into the aluminum bedding block of the injection-­molded polymer stock. Fitting the receiver to the stock and checking for wiggle revealed no movement between receiver and stock prior to reinstalling the action screws. Once the front action screws torque into place, the large surface area between receiver and bedding block immobilize the receiver.
The bolt was designed for tool-less disassembly for easy field maintenance. The bolt face has two ears that drop down into the magazine for positive engagement and reliable feeding. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
The receiver has low-­profile integral Picatinny-­style rail bases that allow for mounting the scope low, keeping the scope’s objective bell close to the barrel, which ensures the shooter’s head stays firmly attached to the stock during recoil. Having the head move with the rifle under recoil makes for faster follow-­up shots and also increases the probability of seeing the round impact and speeding corrections in case of an errant shot.

The bolt face uses dual-plunging ejectors, instead of the more common single ejector. Dual ejectors allowed SAKO to engineer less spring tension than what’s found on a single ejector, greatly prolonging the life of the ejector springs. Using dual ejectors also yields better results with magnum-­sized cases. Heavy cases sometimes need a shove to get them out of the receiver, so two ejectors do the job better than one.
The SAKO 90 has a soft and wide rubber recoil pad. It did an excellent job taming the recoil of 7mm PRC. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
The lug layout on the SAKO 90 bolt face is typical for a SAKO. When the bolt is open, there is one lug at 12-­o’clock and two lugs at 4- and 8-­o’clock. Putting one lug at 12-­o’clock is a great way to prevent the bolt from binding when it’s pushed off-­axis by an inexperienced shooter or by someone trying to cycle the action in a hurry.
The comb has a wide range of height adjustment. To adjust your eye position behind the stock, depress the button on the side of the stock and release to set it. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
The lugs at the 4- and 8-­o’clock positions appear new and demonstrate just how thorough SAKO is in improving its rifles. There are ears that protrude down from the bolt face and ride just inside the detachable box magazine feed lips. This thoughtful arrangement allows for substantial contact between the bolt face and cartridges feeding out of the double-­stack magazine. Even as the cartridges nose up out of the magazine, the bolt face never loses contact with the case head.

SAKO magazines are engineered per cartridge. A 7mm PRC could not feed more reliably into a rifle action. SAKO does not use a one-size-fits-all approach in designing its magazines. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
The lug geometry of the bolt face exists because SAKO uses detachable double-­stack magazines in its 90 models. Magazines frequently get treated like an afterthought, but not by SAKO. A double-­stack magazine will feed with less effort than a center-­fed magazine, yet most detachable box magazines are center fed. The reason this occurs is it’s easier to make a center-­fed detachable box magazine reliable. There are opposing feed lips to hold the cartridge being presented to the bolt face, so it can only move towards the chamber. The problem with center-­fed magazines is they take more effort to push the cartridge out of the magazine and into the chamber.
The SAKO 90 arrives with a muzzlebrake installed. Remove to thread on a can or replace it with the knurled cap. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
A correctly designed double-­stack magazine will have the top cartridge sit flush against a single feed lip and be held in place by another cartridge next to and slightly below it. Additionally, there needs to a rib in the side of the magazine that sits just in front of the cartridge shoulder to prevent the rounds from sliding back-­and-­forth under recoil. The location of the rib varies with the cartridge body length, so most of the time the rib is eliminated or placed for the longest cartridge body that’ll be used in that particular magazine (usually a .300 Winchester Magnum). This means the rib sits too far forward to do its job for any other cartridge.
The bolt stop is on the left side of the receiver. With the bolt drawn to the rear, press the button to remove the bolt. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
However, SAKO didn’t cut any corners with the magazines in the 90. Guns & Ammo’s test rifle had the rib in the correct location and the feed lips were oriented for the minimal body taper of the 7mm PRC. The magazine for the Model 90 Finnlight is a functional work of art that feeds more smoothly than almost all other magazines. The SAKO magazine also has tooless disassembly. Push the rearmost part of the follower down all the way in the magazine, grab the forward portion, lift and twist and the follower and spring pull out the top. Not only does this magazine support effortless feeding, it is also easy to maintain.
In addition to adjustments for engagement and pull weight, the trigger blade is also adjustable fore and aft for optimal position to ensure a comfortable reach with an individual’s hand. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
The trigger is just as beautifully executed as the magazine. Inserting the SAKO-supplied T-­25 Torx wrench through a small hole in the triggerguard allows the shooter to adjust the trigger between five settings, one click at a time. The trigger measured 13/4 pounds at its lightest setting and 33/4 pounds at its heaviest. The let-­off was clean with no hint of creep detected.
With the stock on, the trigger can be adjusted through a hole in the triggerguard using the provided T-handle Torx wrench. Five settings adjust pull weight between 1 3/4- to 3 3/4-pounds. (Photo by Michael Anschuetz)
The stock is polymer with metal flush cups installed at the forend’s tip and the buttstock’s toe. This allows for conventional sling use, but the stock will still rest solidly on front and rear supports at a bench or in the prone.

Typically retailing between $2,000 and $2,400, the 90 Finnlight series is one of the least expensive Model 90s offered by SAKO. These rifles were never meant to compete for the lowest price, though. Instead, what SAKO offers is a thoroughly refined hunting rifle containing every meaningful feature SAKO’s engineers could envision for its flagship. That is an impressive list, making the 90 Finnlight an incredible value.
