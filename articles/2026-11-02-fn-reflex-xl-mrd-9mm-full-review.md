FN Reflex XL MRD 9mm: Full Review

By blurring the lines between compact and subcompact 9mms, the FN Reflex XL MRD may be the most versatile EDC pistol on the market. 

 (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)

November 02, 2026

By Brad Fitzpatrick

No discussion of the world’s greatest gun companies would be complete without mentioning 
Fabrique Nationale d’Armes de Guerre
, 
or simply FN
. Founded in 1889 in Belgium, FN first drew the attention of the American shooting market at the turn of the twentieth century when they began collaborating with John Moses Browning. Some of Browning’s most famous firearm like the Auto-5 and Hi-Power were manufactured in Belgium to John Browning’s specifications. Following John Browning’s death, FN Herstal continued to manufacture class-leading firearms for both the civilian and military markets.
With 13 decades of experience designing and manufacturing firearms, it should come as no surprise that 
FN’s Reflex XL MRD
 is perhaps the most versatile everyday carry 9mm on the market. There’s no more competitive segment in the American gun market than polymer-frame personal defense pistols, but the Reflex XL MRD stands out in this crowded field thanks to this gun’s superb build quality, class-leading ergonomics, and outstanding accuracy and reliability. It isn't the first “extra large” subcompact 9mm to market, but the FN is certainly a standout.
Sweating the Small Stuff
If you gathered and compared the top dozen concealed carry 9mm pistols available today you’d quickly realize most of those guns share very similar spec sheets. Most feature polymer frames and utilize bottleneck-style magazines that promise double-digit capacities. The majority of these popular 9mms are also striker-fired, and all have an assemblage of passive safeties built into their design, though quite possibly no traditional manual safety. In these times every one of the top 9mm pistols has optics cut for mounting red dots, and every gun on the list offers a rugged surface finish that promises to stand up well to the rigors of daily carry.
The largest magazine available for the Reflex XL MRD holds 18 rounds of 9mm. No small feat for a compact handgun. (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
The FN Reflex XL MRD shares many of the same design features that are universally popular with today’s shooters. It does come with a polymer frame that’s light but strong and includes interchangeable backstraps. The FN’s trigger does indeed have a built-in passive safety, and the slide is cut for optics. 10, 15, and 18-round magazines are available for this gun which, at just 1.08-inches wide and weighing 20-ounces unloaded, offers impressive capacities for such a compact 9mm pistol. Similar, yes, but what really stands out about the FN Reflex XL MRD is how it—ahem—stands out.

First, the FN Reflex XL MRD is not a striker-fired gun.  Instead, it features an internal hammer fired design. The benefit is a smooth, consistent trigger pull without the squishiness of some striker-fired guns. The consistent, crisp four-pound trigger pull helps improve accuracy and shooter performance.
FN XL pistols come with a 3.8-inch cold hammer-forged stainless steel barrel, which is a half inch longer than the standard Reflex. The added length on the top end extends the sight plane for improved accuracy. Both the front and rear of the slide are serrated for easier manipulations, and beveling around the muzzle and along the edges of the slide cuts weight and makes the FN more comfortable to carry.
Despite measuring just 1.08-inches wide, the FN Reflex XL MRD comes with 15 and 18-round magazines. Not so long ago, compact pistols with similar capacities measured almost a half-inch wider. To increase capacity without widening the pistol, the FN uses a bottleneck magazine design, so this is a pistol you can effectively conceal even under light clothing without sacrificing magazine capacity.

The Reflex XL’s frame and grip design are excellent. The grip itself is flat on the sides to reduce the gun’s footprint, while the sides also sport microtexturing for a secure, comfortable grip. The frontstrap and backstrap come with more aggressive texturing, and the backstrap is removable. Both large and small backstrap inserts are included. The grip angle comes with a generous undercut and a large beavertail that prevents slide bite and promotes a high hold on the gun for improved recoil mitigation. The FN also comes with a Glock 43X MOS pattern rail, so mounting a compact weapon light on the Reflex XL is simple and straightforward.
The control layout on a self-defense pistol is an important consideration, and few companies are better at designing and arranging pistol controls than FN. The slide stop is compact but still large enough to operate easily, and it is positioned so a standard thumbs-forward grip won’t inadvertently drop the slide after the last shot is fired. Lots of carry guns come with transverse takedown pins, but I believe the FN’s rotating pin design is simpler to operate and safer (you don’t need to pull the trigger to disassemble the gun). The rotating lever also acts as thumb rest when firing. The oval magazine catch is reversible and easy to locate, allowing for precise mag drops and fast reloads.
Multiple textures and stippling patterns can be found on the grip, providing a solid hold when shooting. (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
As you’d expect, the Reflex XL comes with a suite of passive safeties designed to prevent the gun from firing unless the trigger is pulled. The list includes firing pin block, trigger, and fire control safeties, and these guns are also available with or without a manual safety.
There are also different color options available, too. Reflex XL owners can choose from silver, black, or Flat Dark Earth PVD finishes on the slide, and the FDE version comes with a matching frame. Regardless of the color choice, every Reflex XL MRD comes with a pair of dovetailed irons sights and a removable cover plate that allows owners to direct mount RMSc or Holosun 507K footprint optics. Even if you choose to run the Reflex XL MRD without an optic, the included blacked-out rear and luminescent tritium front sights work well in virtually any light conditions.
That’s an impressive feature set, and every FN Reflex XL MRD ships with the pistol, extra magazine, backstraps, wrenches, screws, and a digital manual in a zippered pouch. But one of the most impressive features on this gun (or any FN pistol) are the things that you don’t see. Inside the gun to fit and finish are excellent, with no burrs or machine marks. The FN is a durable carry pistol with an impressive capacity and excellent build quality. It is also, I would soon learn, great fun at the range.

FN Reflex XL MRD: Best of Both
Some pistols are optimized for concealment, while other are designed for comfortable, accurate shooting. The FN Reflex XL MRD is the rare pistol that excels at both. The skinny design and lack of rough edges allow this gun to ride close to the body and make it easy to conceal. In fact, my favorite ways to carry the gun were in a belly band holster, and it even made it into my running pack. The Reflex XL is easy to carry in an appendix or hip holster, and it’s truly remarkable that you can conceal a 9mm pistol with an 18+1 capacity under a light button-up shirt.
As nice as this pistol is to carry, it’s equally enjoyable to shoot. That’s not something you’ll normally say about most subcompact guns that prioritize a small footprint over shooter comfort and accuracy. The FN is well engineered with clever grip geometry that positions the shooter’s hand high on the gun to properly absorb recoil and maintain sight picture. Combine this with a low bore axis and substantial magazine capacity, and you may find that the FN is one of your favorite guns to shoot at the range.
(Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
The trigger on the FN Reflex XL MRD is excellent. There’s a relatively long take-up, but it’s smooth, and that isn’t so bad when the trigger break weight is just four pounds. The trigger is also very consistent, and the reset is short and tactile.
A good trigger is crucial to accuracy, and the FN Reflex XL MRD is a shooter. I shot a handful of groups at 25 yards off of the bench, and I was impressed to see that several of those groups were around the two-inch mark, which is quite good for a gun with a 3.8-inch barrel. The FN is even more impressive when on the move or in a shooting stance. During failure and Bill drills it performed exceptionally well without any failures. The grip allowed for good recoil control and the iron sights are excellent. You can put a red dot on this pistol if you like, but you don’t have to.
The front and rear slide serrations make press checks and other operations very simple. It’s also important to note that the FN’s slide is smooth and easy to cycle. Many shooters find semiauto pistols with heavy springs difficult to manipulate, but the Reflex XL MRD’s slide is lighter and smoother than most competing guns. Even shooters with low hand strength will be able to make this gun function properly.
(Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
The Reflex XL MRD is made in America, and while it isn’t radically different than other carry pistols on the market, everything about the FN is well-designed. From the trigger to the slide operation to changing out the grips, everything works as advertised and the pistol is extraordinarily reliable. It’s concealable yet shootable. In fact, this gun does everything well, which makes it an ideal concealed carry pistol from a manufacturer with a long history of making premium products.
