Savage Arms 110 Apex Hunter XP Review: Ready to Hunt 

A sub $700 Rifle and Scope Package.

 The Apex Hunter XP is a one-stop-shop hunting rifle. (Stan Trzoniec photo)

October 01, 2019

By Stan Trzoniec

In 1955, Savage retained Nicholas Brewer to design a new bolt action, and within three years, the Model 110 was on the way to dealers’ shelves. Today there is a complete line of brand-new, state-of-the-art Model 110 rifles, the company’s flagship.
The Model 110 shipped to me was the Apex Hunter XP, which features a new stock that’s fully adjustable for length of pull via four plastic spacers. (
Ed. note: This is not to be confused with the AccuFit system, which has both length-of-pull and comb-height adjustments.
)
Four stock inserts—in quarter-inch increments from one-quarter to a full inch—allow you to customize length of pull. All you need is a long-shaft Phillips screwdriver. (Stan Trzoniec photo)
Features
The rifle arrived from the factory with a length of pull of 14 inches even. That’s a bit long for me, and by exchanging one spacer for another, I quickly reduced the pull to my preferred 13.5 inches, including the supplied soft rubber recoil pad. You have a choice of 1/4-, 1/2-, 3/4- and one-inch spacers, providing a ton of flexibility. To change the inserts, all you need is a long-shank Phillips screwdriver.
The fore-end starts out on the slim side, widening as it approaches the receiver and magazine well. There is a crisp and well-designed checkering pattern with finger grooves on each side. The pistol grip has a palm swell and the same futuristic checkering design. The pattern goes around the front of the grip for a good purchase no matter the weather conditions.

The comb is a mix of classic straight line and Monte Carlo designs. There is no cheekpiece per se, and that makes sense since this gun is available in both a right- and left-handed versions.
The fore-end has a modern look to it, with finger-grooves and a stylish checkering pattern. The 22-inch barrel is joined to the action via a smooth barrel nut. (Stan Trzoniec photo)
The Apex Hunter XP lists 
17 cartridge choices
 from the .223 Rem. to .338 Win. Mag. While the most popular are included, those looking for something different are treated to the 6.5-284 Norma and .450 Bushmaster. For the southpaws, the choices are narrowed to 11 cartridges—sorry, no 6.5-284 Norma, .450 Bushmaster or .338 Win. Mag.
The machined bolt body is pure Savage, lightly jeweled for appearance and emblazoned with “Savage” and the logo. It features a gas baffle that also serves as a bolt guide, and in front of this are the twin locking lugs.

This is a push-feed action, and within the bolt face you will find a heavy extractor and a pin-type ejector. The rear of the bolt contains the shroud, and the bolt handle and knob are checkered for a non-slip surface. The bolt is removed by pulling the trigger back while the other hand presses in on the plastic bolt release on the top front of the newly designed trigger guard.
This rifle has the famed AccuTrigger, which is user-adjustable and very safe. As it came from the factory, it broke at 3.5 pounds. To adjust the trigger, remove the barreled action and, using the special tool, tweak the trigger to your personal preference. I find I like to turn it down when shooting from the bench, then turn it back up for field use.
The barrel is what I would call sporter weight, measures 0.580 inch at the muzzle and is 22 inches long on my .243 Win. sample. Depending upon the cartridge choice, barrel lengths can vary from 20 to 24 inches, but they all dispense with iron sights and are free-floated from the barrel nut to the muzzle.
My .243 Win. sample had a magazine that holds four rounds. The magazine itself has sheet-metal sides, a plastic follower and base and is finished off with a plastic latch. I thought it required more than the usual amount of pressure to seat the magazine, but it’s not going to fall out when you’re in the field, even with the roughest treatment.
The Apex Hunter XP comes with a Vortex CrossFire II scope, mounted and bore-sighted, on an EGW one-piece rail. (Stan Trzoniec photo)
Scope Included
The XP in the name means it comes scoped, in this case with a Vortex Crossfire II 3-9x40mm scope in one-piece matte-finished Vortex Hunter rings that are attached to an EGW rail. I’m a full-time commercial photographer, so I am meticulous about my lenses and glass. For the money, this scope gives decent optics with a clear field of view from 3X to 9X—although at the larger settings there is a slight loss of sharpness. The scope features the Dead-Hold BDC reticle for easy holdovers.

Performance
At the range, there were no surprises, and the gun operated without a hitch. While going through a modest break-in period, the gun delivered groups averaging 1.5 inches. Between the AccuTrigger, the soft recoil pad and the .243 Win. chambering, shooting the gun from a bench was a pleasure. The gun fed smoothly and was easy to single load. The only caveat was that I had to make sure to pull the bolt all the way back to avoid short-shucking the action.
Closing Thoughts
For the budget-minded hunter, this is really a good deal. You get a decent-shooting rifle with an adjustable stock that’s more than adequate for any hunting situation—with a capable scope all mounted and ready to go. All for under $700.
