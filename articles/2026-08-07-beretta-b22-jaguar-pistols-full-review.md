Beretta B22 Jaguar Pistols: Full Review

Both the Tac Metal and Metal Competition models are purpose-built rimfire racehorses. Here's a full review.

August 07, 2026

By James Tarr

If you heard Beretta has a new .22 pistol, whatever you might be thinking about the new model, you’d be wrong. The 
B22 Jaguar
 is completely unexpected in every way. But that’s a good thing.
First, there will be two models: the 
B22 Jaguar Tac Metal
 and the 
Metal Competition
. The Tac Metal is the basic model, but as you’ll see, there’s nothing basic about it. The B22 is intended to be a general-​purpose .22 Long Rifle pistol, great for target shooting, small-game hunting, training, plinking, or teaching new shooters. It is easily upgradable and customizable because, like many modern pistols, it is modular and built around a chassis system.
Traditionally, the frame of the pistol is the serialized part, the legal “firearm.” With the B22, what looks like the frame is technically just a grip module. The serialized firearm is the removable trigger group — the fire control system — inside the grip module. You can swap the fire control system between different grip modules and top ends quickly and easily, as you don’t have to buy a new “firearm” to do it.
The B22 in both iterations has a 5.25-inch 4140 steel barrel and uses an internal hammer-fired, single-​action-only, blowback system. It is fed by 15-round magazines (10-round magazines are available), and two magazines come with each pistol.

The barrel is threaded ½-28 standard, although there are unthreaded versions. It free-floats inside an aluminum handguard/barrel shroud that extends in one piece from the upper receiver. The barrel shroud has M-LOK accessory attachment slots every 45 degrees around the sides and a rail on the bottom and along the top. The 2.5-inch rail section on the bottom of the barrel shroud is detachable if you don’t want it.
The rail atop the receiver is 5 inches long and big enough to mount any optic you’d like. But the B22 comes standard with iron sights if you’re feeling more traditional, with a full 7-inch sight radius. The front sight has a red fiber-optic insert in the post, and the rear sight is fully adjustable for both windage and elevation.
The B22 Jaguar is designed to be completely ambidextrous. First, you work the bolt and chamber a round by grabbing the aggressively serrated cocking tab at the rear of the receiver. The magazine release is a big oval button and reversible, and you can do this without any tools. There is a bilateral manual safety lever on the frame; it is big and meant to be worked with your thumb from either side. But the most fascinating thing about this pistol, to me, is the ejection.

If you’re confused staring at the accompanying pictures, wondering if some of them got reversed, don’t worry. Your eyes don’t deceive you. There are ejection ports on both sides of this pistol. As it comes from the factory, it is set up for standard right-side ejection. But if you want, only a basic prying tool is needed to get the ejector out and replace it with a left-hand ejector, which is sold separately. Then, the casings will eject to the left side.
The grip module is a metal alloy, and grip panels mount to it. Beretta offers three different sizes of grip panels if you want to size the gun up or down to your hand, but only one pair is included with the Tac Metal model.

The trigger is gently curved, and with a single-​action-only operating system, the trigger pull is short, light, and crisp enough for any use. Beretta’s spec calls for a trigger pull between 3.5 to 4 pounds. Overall, the Tac Metal model is 8.85 inches long, 6 inches tall (with a magazine inserted), and 1.35 inches wide at the grips. Empty, it weighs 33.6 ounces. Right now, it’s just available in black.
The B22 Jaguar Metal Competition model takes the original and adds style and features. The competition model was engineered in collaboration with the Scholastic Action Shooting Program (SASP) and Tandemkross. The SASP is the largest educational shooting program in the world for youth. Tandemkross, for decades, has been known as one of the premier aftermarket accessory manufacturers for .22 pistols. It is the same gun at heart with the bilateral/ambidextrous controls and the ability to swap ejection from right to left. The differences between the standard Jaguar model and this competition model are both stylish and functional.
First, the threaded barrel is tipped with a Tandemkross Steam Demon compensator. This is a single-port model and takes a pistol with low recoil and reduces it even further. The red fiber-optic front sight is a Tandemkross model, a little bigger and brighter than what’s found on the Tac Metal model.

Clamped onto the bottom rail of the barrel shroud is the Tandemkross Accelerator Thumb Ledge, which comes back along the left side of the gun and gives you an angled ledge above the top of the triggerguard against which you can press the thumb of your support hand. Between the compensator and the ledge, this pistol is about as low recoiling as a .22 gets. The additions bring the weight of the gun to 36.4 ounces.
After the compensator, the most noticeable thing about this pistol is the color scheme. The upper receiver has a nice gray finish, which is anodized, and the grip module and the barrel sport a nice light brown finish, which is Cerakote. Paired with the black grip panels, the colors of this pistol make it stand out.
The trigger on this model has been upgraded to a skeletonized, semi-flat-faced design, which has pre- and overtravel adjustments to make the pull as short and crisp as possible. This competition model ships with all three sizes of grip panels (S, M, and L) to customize the fit to your hand. Between the grips and adjustable trigger, this pistol is even easier to individualize.
Like the Tac Metal model, this competition version is easy to disassemble for cleaning without tools. The Tac Metal model has a MSRP of $749, and the Metal Competition model has an MSRP of $969.
