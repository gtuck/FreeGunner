By Richard Nance

Unless you're new to the shooting world, you’ve probably heard the adage, “You can’t have too much ammo unless you’re in a fire or in the water.” Known for manufacturing AR-­15s, lower receivers, packs, bags, individual first aid kits (IFAKs), and chest rigs at affordable prices, Rattlesnake Tactical has also created a 34-­round 9mm Glock-­pattern double-­stack magazine. That’s twice the capacity of a standard G17 magazine, and one more than the 33-­round factory Glock mag. Rattlesnake Tactical magazines are compatible with the G19, G26, G34, G45, and G47 — but they’re not just for Glock models. Several companies make firearms that use Glock-­pattern magazines. These include the Ruger RXM, 
Palmetto State Armory
 (PSA) Dagger, and pistols from Shadow Systems and ZEV Technologies, to name a few.
Rattlesnake Tactical 34-­Round Glock-­pattern magazines also support pistol caliber carbines (PCCs) that feed from Glock-­pattern magazines. Popular examples include the KelTec Sub2000 and the Ruger PC Carbine with Glock magazine well installed. Even with the Glock magazine well, the Rattlesnake Tactical 34-­round magazine is not recommended for use with the Ruger PC Charger, though. Likewise, it’s not recommended for use with the GForce Arms Blade 6 PDW.
Extended magazines are also great to pair for a 9mm conversion kit such as those made by CAA USA, a bullpup conversion kit that includes a 16-­inch barrel such as the Meta Tactical APEX kit, or a kit that replaces the Glock-­pattern slide on a dedicated upper receiver, including those from MechTech Systems. The extra rounds are sure to come in handy. Transform a Glock-­pattern pistol into a carbine-­like platform or change it back to its original configuration. Of course, make sure that such modifications are legal in your jurisdiction and that you comply with all applicable federal and state laws.
The Rattlesnake Tactical 34-­Round 9mm double-­stack magazine is made of impact-­resistant polymer. It’s 9 inches long with a .9-­inch-­wide body. The widest part of the floorplate is 1¼ inches, and it weighs 2½ ounces without ammo.

The bottom half of the Rattlesnake Tactical magazine is textured with a scale pattern reminiscent of a rattlesnake. The logo depicting a snake preparing to strike is on both sides of the body. The texture and logo aren’t merely cosmetic, but they also contribute a non-­slip grip.
Witness holes at five, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 and 34 reveal the status of the magazine. When it’s time to load, the magazine is easily filled by hand.
If maintenance is necessary, the high-­tensile, non-­memory spring is accessible by removing the diamond-­shaped floorplate retainer. The corrosion-­resistant spring and anti-­tilt follower ensure reliable feeding, sometimes a concern with using extended magazines. To reassemble, insert the spring and follower into the body, then hold the floorplate in place while depressing it slightly. Finally, slide the baseplate on until the button from the floorplate clicks into the hole in the baseplate. When you hear the click, it’s locked in place.

Magazines were subjected to rigorous testing for this review, including drop-­tests and a cycle-­test to ensure proper functionality. They all passed.
While too long for on-­body concealed carry, the magazine is great range use. More shooting, less reloads. It could be a game changer for certain competitions where extended magazines are allowed, too. Additionally, an extended magazine is useful for your home defense plan. If you’re disturbed while not wearing pants, it’s unlikely you’ll have quick access to a spare magazine.
We used a 2-­pack of Rattlesnake Tactical 34-­round magazines for testing. Each were loaded to capacity from a bag of loose, mismatched rounds including full metal jacket (FMJ) and various jacketed hollowpoints (JHP), and cartridges with flat-­nosed bullets. Using a Glock 17 (Gen 6), I burned through several magazines. The only note recorded was that one of the flat-­nosed bullets didn’t feed. Aside from that, the Rattlesnake Tactical 34-­round magazines were without issue.
Rattlesnake Tactical’s 34-­round 9mm Glock-­pattern double-­stack magazines were designed and are manufactured in the U.S. They’re also backed by a 3-­year warranty that covers defects, wear and tear, and even unintentional damage.
