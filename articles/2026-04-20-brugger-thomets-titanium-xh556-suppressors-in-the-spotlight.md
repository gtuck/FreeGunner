Brugger & Thomet's Titanium XH556 Suppressors in the Spotlight

Does Swiss engineering meet American needs? Pat Sweeney puts 2 B&T suppressors to the test!

 A sunny afternoon, firing bursts in the name of science. God, I love this job. (Patrick Sweeney)

April 20, 2026

By Patrick Sweeney

The suppressor world has changed so much that what was state-of-the-art when I started with them now equates to just about “doorstop” performance today. One aspect is the flow-through designs we are now seeing. Gone are the days of “gas face” and coughing up black gunk after a day of practice or training. 
Brügger & Thomet (B&T)
 has their new designs, and not only did they incorporate flow-through with them, but the company also added in all the other modern advances we will soon be taking for granted. The two here are the pair of 
5.56
 suppressors, the XH and the XH-SC.
The XH556 SC, top, and the XH556 full-size, bottom. Light hardly begins to describe them. That, and rugged. (Patrick Sweeney)
They are both additive-manufactured, that is 3D printed, but with a twist. The body and baffle design is constructed of titanium additive-machined, but B&T incorporates a 718 Inconel blast baffle in the Titanium tube. The engineering geek in me would love to see just how the Inconel blast baffle is positioned and held in place while the extra Titanium is added. But I suspect that is an in-house secret, and to learn it I’d have to become a Swiss citizen just as a start. OK, I can live not knowing that. The front cap is made of stainless steel, and is a B&T design that reduces muzzle flash. Yes, suppressors can flash, I’ve seen that on various night-time competitions and training. So, reducing or eliminating flash (the powder the ammo maker uses is part of this) can be important.
The stainless steel reduced-flash front cap. (Patrick Sweeney)
The tubes of both the Sub Compact (the SC) and the non-SC XH 556 are fabricated on the back end to be HUB compatible. This way, you can fit yours with whatever mounting system you desire, not just the Surefire mount that the pair arrived here wearing. Part of the reduced backpressure of the XH556 and the XH556 SC comes due to the diameter. They are 1.75 inches in diameter, a small but useful increase in size over the common 1.5-inch diameter. I just did the math, and a seven-inch suppressor that is 1.75 inches in diameter can have as much as 36% greater volume and a seven-inch one that is 1.5 inches in diameter. That’s huge, and they are lightweight.
With the QD mount installed the SC comes up at 13.6 ounces. (Patrick Sweeney)
Neither of these has a barrel length restriction, while being full-auto rated, so your only problem there will be affording ammunition. B&T tested prototypes on the SOCOM burn down test cycle. That is, eight thirty-round magazines of M855 in a scheduled firing rate. Do this 95 times, for 22,000 rounds, and see if your suppressor has survived. B&T did, and they do. The XH556 is a “full-sized” suppressor (shorter than some common ones I have) and the XH556 SC is their Sub Compact, meant to be used on SBRs or when you just have to save the two ounces the SC shaves off of the already lightweight XH full size.

On a piston-driven SBR the B&T suppressors are very cool. (Patrick Sweeney)
They do this while scraping an impressive amount of noise off of the muzzle blast, 38 dB for the full size and 36 for the SC, but we are at the stage where everything is quiet, and we’re looking for more, which B&T delivers. Reducing back pressure is all well and good (and more fun to shot), but how to measure it? B&T says bolt speed is increased by only 2%, so there we go. I can measure the cyclic rate of a carbine or SBR, and do so with or without a suppressor attached. So that’s what I did.
The muzzle device is the mounting system for the B&T, as it is on Surefire suppressors. (Patrick Sweeney)
This takes a select-fire lower (I have access) a suitable upper (I have plenty) a proper timer (got one, naturally) and a pile of ammo. For ammo, I went to Delta Co Munitions. I used their 5.56 55 FMJ, and it took a pile of ammo. I grabbed a Bravo SBR upper and tested it without a suppressor, to determine baseline cyclic rate. Then, I put a first-generation suppressor on. I grabbed a Surefire SOCOM 556 because I’ve been using it for years and it was (and still is) an excellent example of the best first-gen suppressors to be had. Then I did the same with the two B&T XHs, the full size and the SC.
The B&T suppressors come with the wrenches needed to remove the rear mount. (Patrick Sweeney)
Well, the numbers don’t lie. Both B&T significantly reduced the amount putting a suppressor increased the cyclic rate. Curiously, despite being longer and with a larger volume, the full-sized XH 556 was actually slightly worse at reducing the increase than the SC. I suspect that is the cost of the extra decibels it cuts off the muzzle blast. That is part of the calculus of selecting a suppressor: the shorter size and weight of the SC, versus the slight reduction in cyclic rate increase. Need I remind you, this is America, you have choices, and one of those choices is: both of them.

Either one you choose will come with a Surefire-compatible, B&T-made flash-hider mount, and the QD mount installed in the HUB threads. Also, you get the tools needed to assemble or disassemble the suppressor. With the HUB rear threads, you can install the XH556 or the XH556 SC on a veritable host of suitable rifles. As they are full auto rated for 5.56, with no barrel length restrictions, then any cartridge hurling a jacketed bullet of .224” or smaller will find either of the B&Ts a suitable suppressor. Lest you think I’m picking on Surefire, while their SOCOM 556 Gen 1 upped the cyclic rate, their Gen 3 brought it back down. For more information visit
