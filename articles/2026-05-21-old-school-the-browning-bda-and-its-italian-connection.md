Old School: The Browning BDA and its Italian Connection

In this Old School series, Bob Campbell finds a Browning BDA double-action pistol with a mixed heritage.

 The Browning BDA double-action pistol has a mixed heritage. It was produced by Beretta for Fabrique Nationale and featured FN markings, but it was sold as the BDA in North America. (Bob Campbell photo)

May 21, 2026

By Bob Campbell

Most of the pistols featured in 
Old School
 series of reports are not handguns I sought out but rather firearms I ran across by chance. The Browning BDA .380 ACP is among these. The BDA or Browning Double Action is a medium-size handgun manufactured by Beretta of Italy and rebranded by Fabrique Nationale of Belgium. In Europe it was marketed by FN as the 140DA.
The BDA was a modification of the Beretta 84. It eliminated the 84’s frame-mounted safety and re-engineered a hammer-dropping decock lever and safety.
It also features an enclosed slide, which has flats near the muzzle that give it a passing resemblance to the Hi Power. The pistol is marked “Fabrique Nationale Herstal” along with the FN company logo on the slide, and also by 
“Made in Italy” and “PB” for Beretta
.
The BDA is a robust handgun with a 13-round magazine. I have seen a single example in the United States in .32 ACP, and that version’s specifications call for one less cartridge in magazine capacity.

The trigger is a smooth but long double-action first shot. The single- action trigger breaks at a nice 4.0 pounds. The BDA shows its Beretta heritage with an external draw bar. The safety decocks the hammer and the pistol may be carried safety on or off, relying on the double-action first shot for safety. The sights are small but average for the time. The hammer is an upswept spur type.
There are two generations of the BDA .380. They run approximately concurrent with a change in Beretta 84 production. The second generation features a heavier slide and recoil spring and guide rod assembly. This change occurred relatively early in production.
My information indicates the FN-marked pistols were out of production in 1986 while the Browning BDA was in production and imported until 1997. Both variants feature steel slides and aluminum frames.

All of the BDA pistols I have seen feature nicely finished smooth wood grips. A source tells me that some guns—especially nickel-plated guns, a rarity in themselves—were supplied with Pachmayr rubber grips. This makes sense since many Browning Hi Power 9mm handguns of the time were supplied with Pachmayr grips.
The BDA is easy to shoot well. Recoil is modest, and accuracy potential is excellent. In fact, this is as accurate a .380 ACP pistol as you will find. The BDA has a considerably different appearance than the 
Beretta 84
 but shares the Beretta’s reliability, and reliability with jacketed hollowpoint ammunition is excellent.
Based on its reliability and accuracy, the BDA is among a few of the vintage handguns I have covered in these pages I would recommend for personal defense. It’s an ideal size in many ways as well.
The BDA is one of the better examples of a compact personal defense handgun from a time when blued steel ruled the world. They aren’t hard to find, and current prices cover a wide range.
The pistol illustrated has obvious wear, and the medallions are missing from the grips. I bought it for $300—no box, no spare magazine. Guns in good condition and with the box run $400 to $600. I ran across a few pristine examples for $600 to $700, plus a couple over that.
