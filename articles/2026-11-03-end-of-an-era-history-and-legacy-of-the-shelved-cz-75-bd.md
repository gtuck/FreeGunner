End of an Era: History and Legacy of the Shelved CZ 75 BD

A brief retrospective on the rise, evolution, and final retirement of CZ's legendary all-steel, decocking masterpiece.

 The CZ 75 BD laying on a Czech Republic flag. (J. Scott Rupp photo)

November 03, 2026

By J. Scott Rupp

The word “iconic” gets overused when discussing firearms designs, but sometimes the shoe fits. That’s certainly the case with the CZ 75, which last year marked 50 years since its introduction. One of the first “wonder nines,” it combined a double-action/single-action trigger with high 9mm capacity.
Developed in the Czech Republic when it was a Warsaw Pact country, the CZ 75 has been widely copied and adopted by law enforcement agencies and militaries across the globe. The gun was scarce in the U.S. until the early 1990s when import restrictions were relaxed, but a few had been sneaking in prior to that. Thanks to this, the 75 gained an early fan in Col. Jeff Cooper, who championed it as the basis for the Bren Ten 10mm he helped develop.
Features
For shooters, one of the big advantages of the CZ 75 over other semiautos is its slide/frame configuration. The 75’s slide rails run inside the frame, not outside as on other guns. This permits a lighter slide and thus less reciprocating mass, and the setup lowers the bore axis as well, making the 75 incredibly soft shooting. That aspect is boosted by its all-steel construction and a curved grip that naturally guides your hand deep into the gun.
The CZ 75 had one other thing going for it. Unlike other early DA/SAs, it featured a thumb safety and could be carried cocked and locked like a 1911—a big selling point for many.

Casual U.S. handgunners may not be familiar with the CZ 75, but you can bet competition shooters are. CZ has been a major player in the action-pistol world thanks to the 75 and its variants. I had the opportunity to test the Tactical Shadow 2 in a local match a few years ago, and it shot so fast it was almost scary.
The example here is the 75 BD, a member of the second-generation, B-series 75s. Introduced in the early 1990s, the B was notable for the addition of a firing pin block. The BD made its debut in 1997, the D signifying it had a decocker rather than the traditional thumb safety.
Personal Connection
I bought this gun after visiting the CZ factory in the Czech Republic, where a day on the range there quickly convinced me I had to have one. For a long time, it was my only 9mm pistol, although sadly in recent years it’s spent more time in the safe than on the range.

Good thing I hung onto it. CZ just announced that the 75 B, BD and Compact are being put out to pasture. The 
75 will live on in modern variants like the SP-01
, P-01 and the D PCR Compact, and the design’s DNA is still found in the company’s competition models as well. But I’m glad I have a more classic example of this trendsetting pistol.
