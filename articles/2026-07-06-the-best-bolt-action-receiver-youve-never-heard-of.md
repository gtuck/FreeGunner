The Best Bolt Action Receiver You've Never Heard Of

Why this obscure, precision-engineered action is quietly outperforming the industry's biggest names

 The BE1 action utilizes a 2-lug bolt with a 90-degree bolt throw. (Rodger Holscher photo)

July 06, 2026

By Rodger Holscher 

There has been no better time to be in the long-range precision rifle game. The market these days is flooded with quality components which makes building a precision rifle easier than ever. Even with all the quality actions out there, 1934 Armory decided that they were going to throw their hat in the ring, making precision bolt actions.
1934 Armory is a fairly new company to precision bolt actions (launching in 2023), but they have long roots in precision machinery manufacturing. The parent company of 1934 Armory is Elsner Engineering Works (Hanover, PA). The Elsner company has been in business since 1934 (hence the name 1934 Armory) and has been family owned ever since. Not only do they build their own actions under their own brand, but they also build actions for some well-known companies that have been around for quite a while. 
The BE1 is machined with an integral bolt lug and 20 MOA rail. The model here is the full-threaded version. (Rodger Holscher photo)
1934 Armory offers four different actions. The BE1 is a short action 2-lug, full feature, full weight action built for hard use and reliability. The BE2 is a long action big brother to the BE1. While slightly different, they share a lot of similarities. The BE3 (short action) and BE4 (long action) are their lightened hunting actions with a three-lug bolt (60-degree bolt throw). All share a Remington 700 footprint action and accept a wide range of aftermarket Remington 700 based accessories. The BE1 is offered as a full-thread and quick change. I chose the quick-change barrel mount option.
First Impressions
I received the BE1 action at the beginning of September 2025. Immediately, I could tell the quality of the action. The action was packaged in a nice branded hard case with the bolt and action separated and in the foam cutouts. I elected to go with the Quick-Change barrel system. I had planned on this being the action for my NRL Hunter rifle chambered in 6.5 Creedmoor, so naturally it came with the standard .481” bolt face.

My first impressions of the action were great. The fit and finish of the action was impressive. Even dry, the bolt glided through the race ways like it had ball bearings. I was skeptical at first about how light and smooth the bolt lift was on the BE1 action. It was effortless so I figured that it might have a lighter firing pin spring to aid it how light the bolt lift was. I was wrong. After inquiring 1934 Armory about it, the firing pin spring is the standard 24-pound spring. 
As I dove into the action, I noticed something that isn’t well advertised about the BE1 action. The tip of the cocking cam is equipped with a pinned roller bearing. This helps with a smooth transition between the cocking ramp and the cock notch (small notch on the bolt where the cocking piece sits while you run the bolt). That small addition coupled with the 90-degree bolt throw makes this one of the smoothest out of the box action I’ve ever felt. 
Even before getting a barrel spun up, I had to take the action to my Gunsmith (Marc Soulie from Spartan Precision Rifles). He was also impressed with the BE1’s smoothness. Actions often need a break-in period before they get as smooth as the BE1 action – if they ever do.

BE1 Features
The BE1 action is packed full of features that you would expect to find on actions at a much higher price point. Coming in at a MSRP of $999.99, the BE1 is a budget friendly option that you can not ignore. 
The BE1 action is milled out of one solid piece of 4140 hardened alloy steel. 1934 Armory uses a 9-axis milling machine to do most of the milling before the action is removed to have the raceways cut. The raceways are wire EDM machined for exceptional alignment with the bore and smooth operation. 
The BE1 action features an integrated, robust, .300” recoil lug. This is milled at the same time as the action to ensure perfect alignment with the bore axis. The BE1 also features an integrated 20 MOA picatinny rail that is also milled at the same time. If you have been shooting long enough, you have felt the pain of a traditional screwed on scope base coming loose. The integrated 20 MOA rail eliminates the problems that come from the traditional screwed on scope base. 
The bolt is an oversized .718” diameter one piece bolt. The traditional Remington 700 design was not a one-piece bolt. The bolt head was high temperature silver soldiered onto the bolt body. That created a failure point that 1934 Armory eliminates with the one piece 4140 alloy bolt body. 
Improving again on the Remington 700 design, the bolt is equipped with an M16 style extractor to ensure you get reliable case extraction every time. The bolt also has a single plunger ejector that is tried and true. With the rise in popularity of the small rifle primers, the BE1 bolt features a smaller firing pin hole to accommodate the .062” firing pin. This might not sound like a big deal but let me tell you, it’s a big deal.

Last year, I was competing with a factory Tikka action. I ran into issue with piercing primers when I was shooting small rifle primer brass. This set me back a lot of time and money to fix the problem that came along with the pierced primers. The BE1 action solves that problem for me. 
The firing pin is 17-4 stainless steel and is a toolless bayonet style. With a slight clockwise turn of the firing pin assembly, you can easily remove the firing pin assembly. This allows for rapid field cleaning and maintenance. The firing pin protrusion is .060”. The firing pin cocking piece features that aforementioned cocking cam roller bearing. That cam roller bearing reduces the bolt lift pressure and aids in smooth cycling. 
The action is equipped with a side mounted bolt release that I raised slightly to avoid the need for inletting into the stock. The bolt release is slotted where the action absorbs the brunt of the impact when your bolt slams against the bolt release. 
The BE1 action accepts any Remington 700 trigger and the threaded tenon is 1.0625-16 TPI (standard Remington 700 thread pitch). The bolts are fluted and are flat nosed (as opposed to the Remington 700 which requires a counterbore be cut into the chamber). The bolt knob is oversized and can be had in steel or aluminum. The bolt knobs are male threaded and should be compatible with some aftermarket bolt knobs.
Both the action and bolt are PVD (Physical Vapor Deposition) coated. This coating gives the metal a harder surface, protects it from rusting, and makes it more resistant to wear. This coating is also extremely smooth, which allows you to run a dryer bolt but maintains the smooth feel. 
Spartan Precision Rifles spun up the barrel for the Quick-Change version. Notice the screw located directly above the recoil lug. (Rodger Holscher photo)
Quick-Change
The Quick-Change barrel version of the action has an unthreaded cut (.250” long) in the barrel tenon and uses two 10-32 set screws at the 3 and 9 o’clock to lock in the barrel. This allows a guy to put the barrel on hand tight and maintain reliability. In the field you can loosen the set screws, swap out the barrel and retighten the set screws to 28 in/lbs and be shooting again in minutes. You can also torque those barrels in the traditional way and use the set screws as an extra failsafe. The Full Thread version of the BE1 is identical to the Quick-Change version, but the barrel tenon has a full .725” of threads without the unthreaded cut. 
The BE1 action has a smaller ejection port to make it more ridged. The BE1 is compatible with BDL hinged floorplates as well as AICS magazines. The BE1 has a guaranteed head space tolerance of .001” for prefit barrel compatibility. The BE1 finishes off at 32 oz of total weight.
Testing
When I received the 1934 Armory BE1, I made it into my NRL Hunter setup for 2026. I had Marc at Spartan Precision Rifles spin up a CRB barrel for it. My first trip to the range gave me the only real issue I had with the action. The barrel started coming loose after about 30 shots. I could see it on my target when it started happening.

I chalk that issue up to user error on my part. I ordered a 28 in/lb Fix-It Stick torque limiter to make sure my set screws were torqued to the appropriate 28 in/lbs and I haven’t had an issue since. I am sitting at almost 1200 rounds through this action as of this writing and the barrel connection is a non-issue.
This action has been issue free since the barrel coming loose. I attribute a lot of that to the robust way this action is built. I never have to worry about my scope base coming loose. Being able to run the action dry means that I don’t run into issues with debris collecting in the action.

 of the season was in Hammett Idaho. That area is known for the moon dust type ground. At last year’s match in the same location, I had some big issues with my Tikka rifle. That powdery dust gets into places that can keep your action from running appropriately.
That first match with this action was a big test for it. It performed flawlessly and reliably. I have since completed two more matches with that action and have had zero issues. The BE1 action is boringly reliable.
The action feels as smooth now as it did right out of the box. I have shown the action to precision rifle instructors, snipers, competitors, and Gunsmiths and they all have same reaction to cycling the action. They cannot believe how feature-packed the action is, how smooth it is, and how economical the price of the action is. I love this action so much that I bought the Full Thread version for a PRS rifle setup that is in the works. To put it in perspective, the brand-new action is just as smooth as the action that I have 1,200 rounds on and probably another 5,000 dry fires.

Smoothness
I dug deep into some more reasons why this action is so smooth to open and close. This action is a full, cock-on-open action. If you have a bolt action rifle at home, I recommend doing this little test. Obviously make sure your weapon is clear and there is no ammo around. Slowly close your bolt and look at the bolt shroud and cocking piece on the back of your bolt. As you start to close your bolt, watch as the cocking piece is grabbed by the trigger sear as your locking lugs engage. With most actions, you will see your bolt shroud continue moving forward (towards the muzzle) as the locking lugs engage the bolt lugs. You’ll see the firing pin cocking piece stay in place as the trigger sear engages. This loads the firing pin spring and requires more force to overcome the tension on the spring. 
That added force required to overtake that spring adds to the perceived bolt throw force needed to close the bolt. With the BE1 action, the cocking piece moves forward with the bolt shroud, not cocking at all. This allows the BE1 bolt to be extremely smooth and easy to operate. While I was at it measuring the firing pin cock and travel, I noted that the firing pin on both actions, have a full .191” of fall when the trigger is pulled. That is just a little extra piece of info for those who care to know. 
Conclusion
Is the 1934 Armory BE1 the best action available under $1,000? In my opinion, for my action needs, I believe so. That is all subjective based on what I want out of an action. You will have to make that decision on your own, but you can rest assured that I will not stop at just two of these actions. The quality, reliability, features, performance and price are too hard to be. I have not been able to find another action out there with these features at this price point. 1934 Armory has a winner in the BE1 action.
