Plinking Perfection: Why You Need a .22LR AR

Master Your AR Skills for a Fraction of the Ammo Cost

 CMMG’s Resolute 300 Mk4 is a very nicely made, dedicated .22 LR AR-15 that would be perfect for training, recreation or small game hunting. (David M. Fortier photo)

July 08, 2026

By David Fortier

Are you in need of a suitable .22 semiautomatic to take advantage of the current glut of inexpensive ammunition? Well, I have been burning up copious quantities of .22 rimfire of late through one of CMMG’s Resolute 300 Mk4 rifles—a fine-looking, upper-crust AR-15 chambered in .22 Long Rifle.
CMMG is justly famous for its .22 LR conversion kits for 5.56 NATO-chambered AR-15 rifles. These are similar to the old military conversion kits in that they include a chamber adapter and simply drop in place of the standard bolt carrier assembly. However, unlike the military kits, CMMG’s .22 LR kits are known for their reliability. Due to this they have become quite popular.
Better Than a Conversion Kit 22LR
However, CMMG’s Resolute 300 Mk4 rifle is not merely a conversion based upon a 5.56 NATO barrel with a 1:7 or 1:9 twist. Rather, it is a dedicated .22 LR rifle with the proper 1:16 twist. As it is a dedicated .22 LR rifle, accuracy should be better than a 5.56 AR-15 with a conversion kit dropped into it. I have owned and used CMMG conversion kits for quite a few years now and like them very much and so was interested to see how one of its dedicated .22 LR rifles would perform.
This model is available in 10 different Cerakote finishes with my review sample being Burnt Bronze. While it’s a subjective matter, I thought the color really popped and was much to my liking.

Features
The Resolute is built using forged 7075-T6 aluminum AR-15 upper and lower receivers. The flattop upper receiver features a case deflector, forward assist, CMMG’s oversize ambidextrous charging handle and a shortened ejection-port door.
Inside the upper is a chrome-plated bolt assembly. This installs and removes as a unit and operates via simple blowback. At the front of this is a collar with integral feed ramp that slips onto the chamber of the barrel.
This model features a 17-inch barrel machined from 4140CM with an MT profile. As operation is via blowback there is no gas block. The muzzle features 1/2x28 threads and is fitted with a CMMG SV muzzle brake.

Surrounding the barrel is a CMMG RML15 free-floating handguard with M-Lok mounting points. The handguard reaches almost to the muzzle brake and allows accessories such as a bipod, white light or iron sight to be easily attached. Robust yet light, this design feels good in the hand.
The upper is pinned to a matching lower receiver. Fit was quite good without rattle while still allowing the takedown and pivot pins to be pushed through easily. Inside the lower is a Geissele two-stage SSA trigger. The Resolute 300 Mk4 is fitted with a Magpul MOE pistolgrip, oversize trigger guard and an interesting collapsible buttstock.
The stock is CMMG’s aluminum RipStock. This rides on the company’s six-position enhanced receiver extension. The two work in combination to allow the shooter to pull the stock out to the desired position without having to depress the locking latch. An ambidextrous sling plate is standard as well.
The rifle feeds from 25-round magazines that mimic a standard 30-round 5.56 mag in profile. The receivers and handguard feature a Cerakote finish. Overall length with the stock collapsed is just 32.7 inches, and the rifle weighs in at only six pounds two ounces.
Performance
As you would expect, the Resolute is quick handling and fast to the shoulder. It carries well and proved well balanced. To check its accuracy I dug out a Nightforce SHV 4-14x50mm scope that I fit to the rifle using a Geissele one-piece mount.

The CMMG ran flawlessly with all loads tested except Remington bulk pack Golden Bullets. These simply didn’t want to feed, so I stopped attempting to use them. Accuracy was good but certainly not match grade.
Where the Resolute 300 Mk4 really shined was in the fun factor. After checking its accuracy, I moved from the bench to engage my small 
Action Target rimfire dueling tree
 at 100 yards. This proved great fun, as did a host of other rimfire targets I set up.
Magazines load to capacity fairly easily. These insert with a simple upward push the same as with a standard AR-15. Pull the charging handle to the rear and release it to chamber a round. The safety and magazine release are standard AR-15, so they’re easy to manipulate. The Geissele trigger breaks cleanly.
I shot the Resolute in a variety of traditional positions as well as off barricades and a “tank trap” type obstacle. It proved a great shooting partner, ringing steel while digesting a couple bricks of inexpensive Federal value pack ammunition. While all .22 rimfires can be a bit picky, especially as they get dirty, the CMMG performed without issue other than with the one type of ammo I mentioned earlier.
Final Thoughts
CMMG’s Resolute 300 Mk4 is a good-looking rimfire rifle with a number of nice features, which one would expect considering the $1,450 suggested retail price. You can also purchase the upper and lower receiver assemblies separately for $675 if you prefer.
With .22 LR ammunition once again readily available at economical prices, I highly recommend not only stocking up, but getting out and enjoying this great lil’ cartridge on the range. A rifle such as CMMG’s Resolute would make a great shooting partner.
