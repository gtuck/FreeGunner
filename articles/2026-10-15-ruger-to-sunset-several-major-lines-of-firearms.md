Ruger to Sunset Several Major Lines of Firearms

19 lines of firearms, from revolvers to precision rifles, will be discontinued indefinitely through the end of 2026.

October 15, 2026

By Guns & Ammo Staff

Ruger has confirmed that multiple firearm lines will be discontinued effective immediately, with many more to cease production by the end of 2026. Four revolver lines have already been sunet. These include:
LCR
SP101
GP100
Bearcat
The following lines will be going through final production runs through the remainder of the year, and will cease by the start of 2027:
SR1911
LC Carbine
PC Charger
PC Carbine
LC Charger
Redhawk
Super Redhawk
Blackhawk
Super Blackhawk
Single-Series revolvers
Vaquero
Ruger No. 1
77-Series rifles
Hawkeye
Ruger Precision Rifle
Ruger's Super Redhawk recently received a new chambering in .22 Hornet. (Photo by Mark Fingar)
Ruger's public product catalog may continue displaying models during the transition, and a suspension does not necessarily mean every variant disappears from dealer inventories overnight. The culling of these 19 lines is part of the "Ruger 2030" strategy announced by the company, and Ruger has noted that the firearms that are ceasing production may not be pulled permenantly, with chances to return in the future. As Ruger moves forward, it has assured the industry that new guns will still be coming, and that it will be focusing on improved designs and innovation.
While these cuts are extensive, Ruger has plenty of major lines remaining that will be continuing onward:

Pistols
: LCP, RXM, MAX-9, Security, Ruger-5.7 and Mark IV families.
Revolvers
: Wrangler and Super Wrangler.
Rifles
: American Gen II, American Rimfire, Precision Rimfire, 10/22, Mini-14, and Harrier AR-15s.
Ruger's Glenfield and Marlin product lines will not be affected by this announcement as well. The RXD suppressor lines, produced in collaboration with Dead Air, will also be unaffected.
