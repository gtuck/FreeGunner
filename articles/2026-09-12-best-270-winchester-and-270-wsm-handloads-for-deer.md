By Lane Pearce 

My dad worked for many years at a Chevy dealership in the parts department. One of his tasks was to order non-GM items and accessories for retail stock. When I was 13 or so, he handed me a rewards catalog and asked if I needed a deer rifle for Christmas.
You can guess my response!
The pickings were slim but more than adequate: a Winchester Model 100 semiautomatic chambered in .308 Winchester or a Winchester Model 70 bolt action in .270 Winchester. I’d learned everything I knew about deer hunting from reading Jack O’Connor’s stories in 
Outdoor Life
, so choosing the Model 70 was a no-brainer.
The author with a deer he killed with one of his favorite .270 WSM loads. (Lane Pearce photo)
My Reloading Expedition
Back then, I handloaded only shotgun shells for my Winchester Model 1400 with a 12-gauge Lee Loader. I used the bathroom scales to seat the plastic shot sleeve/wad to the specified pounds. Only after I finished college, got married, and moved to Alabama did the metallic reloading bug bite me.

A.J. Wallace, my office coworker, hunted deer with a passion and also reloaded for his Remington Model 742 in .30-06. He introduced me to Wiley’s, a local sporting goods store, and there I met their senior salesman Jessie Kelly. A.J. and Jessie became my first reloading mentors. I got busy setting up my bench with an RCBS Jr. press, .270 Winchester dies, a Lyman’s beam scale, and a lathe-type case trimmer.
One of O’Connor’s favorite .270 Winchester handloads was 62 grains of Hodgdon surplus 4831 topped with the Remington 130-grain Bronze Point bullet. This is a highly compressed charge, and he reported the muzzle velocity of 3,100+ fps. I recall buying some 4831 from Wiley’s on several occasions in the early 1970s. CAUTION: O’Connor was using surplus propellant salvaged from demilled World War II 20mm cannon rounds. Several versions of 4831 have been introduced since then. Use only a current stock of powder and reloading data for that specific brand listed in a reliable load manual or manufacturer’s data sheet. 
Hodgdon’s Reloading Data Center 
is an excellent place to search online for safe load recipes.
Many years later, after my interests became more focused on reloading and range testing to develop the best handloads for various cartridges and becoming a freelance writer, I sold my old Model 70 but acquired a Kimber Model 8400 and a vintage Winchester Model 54 in .270 Winchester. And even later, I bought a Savage Model 16FSS chambered for the then-new .270 WSM (Winchester Short Mag). I had never taken a whitetail with either of my 
.270 Win. rifles
, but I sure did with that .270 WSM Savage.  I eventually added more .270 WSM rifles to my battery, including a Nosler Model 48 and a Tikka T3.

Handloading the .270s
When it comes to handloading, the .270 Winchester is easily loaded with 130-grain bullets to achieve 3,000+ fps. The .270 WSM can be charged with enough additional propellant to obtain a similar velocity with 150-grain bullets. Recoil is tolerable with a 9- or 10-pound rifle/scope combo chambered in either cartridge.
Both rounds are typical rimless rifle cartridges rated by SAAMI at a maximum average pressure of 64,000/65,000 psi. The original .270 Winchester case was based on the .30-03 Springfield round. It retains the slightly sloped shoulder and case length, with the neck diameter reduced to hold a 0.277-inch bullet.
The .270 WSM is based on a shortened and reformed .404 Jeffery case. It has a slightly tapered case, an abrupt 35-degree shoulder, and a rather short neck. Case capacity is about 20 percent greater than that of the .270 Winchester, so the .270 WSM can deliver greater velocities with comparable-weight bullets.
Both rounds are handloaded using the same methods for any typical bottleneck rifle round. Use load data from reliable source(s). Clean and inspect cases carefully. Measure case length and trim/deburr if required. Lube case body and inside the neck sparingly, full length resize/decap fired primer, wipe off residual lube, reprime, charge with powder, and inspect charged case before seating the bullet. Seat the bullet, and you’re done.
The .270 Winchester and the .270 WSM are my two most favorite rifle cartridges. They are easy to handload (the accompanying chart lists my favorite handloads for them), and they sure do deliver the goods in the hunting fields.
