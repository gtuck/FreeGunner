Die Free Co Review: Next-Gen Kung Fu Grip & Hand Stops

Straighten Your Wrist and Sharpen Your Control with DFCO's Inventive Rifle Controls.

 The Die Free Co AR accessories are slightly smaller than most but offer improved ergonomics. (Alfredo Rico photo)

By Alfredo Rico

The AR accessories market is bursting at the seams. Competition is high, which makes me wonder why anyone would want to enter the market. After I received the Kung Fu Grip, Tiger Claw, and Full Stop from Die Free Co. (DFCO), I understood why.
DFCO is a small New Hampshire-based business
 that makes its products in the U.S.A. The company’s name is inspired by the state’s famous Live Free or Die motto. While the name sounds serious, their kung fu-themed products and packaging offer a breath of fresh air. For example, the Kung Fu Grip is packaged in a resealable bag with an illustration of a ripped warrior wielding an AR and a sword. A large Chinese dragon is in the background.
Humor and creativity are greatly appreciated, but it becomes gimmicky if the products lack innovation. Fortunately, DFCO products are inventive.
Die Free Co.’s Kung Fu Grip has a steeper angle compared to an A2 grip, and it has a bobtail-type shape. Note the playful sticker included with the grip. (Alfredo Rico photo)
Kung Fu Grip
The Kung Fu Grip is an 
AR-compatible pistol grip
 that’s unlike most grips. Its distinguishable difference is the 12-degree angle. A traditional A2-style grip is 25 degrees; MagPul’s MOE K2 grip is 17 degrees.

The steep angle would designate the Kung Fu Grip a vertical grip. The benefit of a vertical grip is that it opens the wrist’s angular movement. This is useful when running a stock as short as possible or shooting in prone or kneeling positions. It allows the shooter to bring the AR closer to the body and give the elbow a wider range of fore and aft movement.
Two other key differences are it’s shorter than most grips, and it features an angled cut on the rear bottom—a design Model 1911 fans may call a bobtail cut. The grip is anywhere from 0.25 inch to 0.75 inch shorter than other grips. I have average-size hands, and the Kung Fu Grip fits my hand just right. Any shorter and it would be too small.
My only gripe is that the cut leaves a small chunk of my palm unsupported. When holding the AR in certain positions and with only a hand on the pistol grip, I can feel the palm slipping. After giving it some scrutiny, I think that if the back of the Kung Fu Grip had sharp, horizontal lines instead of smooth, vertical grooves, it would prevent the palm from slipping.

Two features that worked in the grip’s favor were the deep finger groove and the side panel texture. I typically don’t like finger grooves because they force my hand into a single position. The Kung Fu Grip’s finger groove is deep and wide, locking my middle finger into the grip, but it doesn’t feel trapped. After comparing it to other grips without finger grooves, I came to really like DFCO’s implementation of it. Plus, the aggressive texture is just right for a sure hold.
Tiger Claw
The Tiger Claw is a small polymer forward grip that is M-LOK compatible. The short grip is designed for those who shoot an AR with their support hand in a C-clamp grip. It locks the hand in place. The Tiger Claw is tall enough to support two fingers. It’s minimal but just the right size to provide excellent control of the AR.

Full Stop
The M-LOK-compatible Full Stop is made of 6061-T651 aluminum, has an anodized finish, and its face is heavily checkered. It is a barricade, hand, and finger stop, depending on where you mount it. Mounting it at the front of the handguard allows the shooter to pin the rifle against a wall or other surface for stability and accuracy. It’s smaller than most barricade stops but is highly effective and versatile.
DFCO’s accessories have a quality feel and are priced reasonably. The Kung Fu Grip and Tiger Claw are $20; the Full Stop is $40. Each is available in a variety of colors.
