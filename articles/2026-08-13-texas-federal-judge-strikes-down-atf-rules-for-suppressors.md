Texas Federal Judge Strikes Down ATF Rules for Suppressors

Landmark ruling finds ATF registration and paperwork unconstitutional after NFA tax drops to $0.

 A Texas federal court ruled that the NFA paperwork was no longer required when purchasing a suppressor. The ruling has its limits. (Alfredo Rico photo illustration)

By Alfredo Rico

 The information provided in this article is for general informational and educational purposes only. It does not constitute legal advice and should not be treated as such.)
In a historic first, the suppressor registration requirements of the National Firearms Act of 1934 were sidestepped in Texas. On August 12, 2026, Gun Owners of America, Inc. (GOA) along with Silencer Shop formally notified the Department of Justice that they intended to transfer a silencer without complying with the paperwork requirements of the National Firearms Act ("NFA"). When the clock struck midnight, they did just that.
Why is This Happening?
This stems from a provision in last year’s Big Beautiful Bill, which reduced the tax stamp for silencers, short-barreled firearms, and “any other weapons” from $200 to $0. On July 4, 2025, Silencer Shop Foundation v. ATF filed a lawsuit that challenged the constitutionality of the “tax” in a Texas federal court of on the basis that it generates no revenue. With no tax, there was no reason for the registration.
On August 5, 2026, the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas ruled that key National Firearms Act of 1934 (NFA) restrictions are unconstitutional for untaxed firearms and permanently blocked the ATF from enforcing them against the plaintiffs, their members, and their customers. The Silencer Shop v. ATF ruling applies only to suppressors. The short-barreled firearms and “any other weapons”, is a being challenged in another lawsuit.

A seven-day stay was issued by the court, giving the federal government time to respond to the ruling. With no objection from the ATF, Silencer Shop transferred unregistered suppressors to Brandon Herrera and Wes Virdell on August 13, in accordance with all other applicable federal and state laws.
A Victory with Restrictions
The Texas federal district court ruling does make it a national law. The federal government may step in and complicate matters. Also, state and local restrictions still apply.
In 27 states, buying a suppressor will be treated like a firearm where a standard background check is required. There is no NFA paperwork or waiting period. These states include Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Florida, Idaho, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Minnesota, Missouri, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Mexico, Oklahoma, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, and Wyoming.

In 15 states, the NFA process and local laws are still required. The Form 4 and other state paperwork must be filled out. These states include Alaska, Colorado, Connecticut, Georgia, Michigan, Mississippi, Montana, Nevada, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Washington, and Wisconsin.
In the 8 states where suppressors are banned, they are still not legal to possess. These states include California, Delaware, Hawaii, Illinois, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, and Rhode Island, plus Washington, D.C.
Moving Forward with Caution
In Silencer Shop’s August 13, 2026, video about the ruling, the company cautioned that the decision applies specifically to Silencer Shop and the plaintiffs in the lawsuit. Silencer Central’s CEO, Brandon Maddox, added his own warning, noting that 'customers will need to understand both the federal requirements and the laws where they live.'
Closing Thoughts
The 1934 NFA tax stamp was always a financial barrier to prevent us from getting suppressors. Now that it is reduced to $0, the paperwork used to prop up the tax is a moot point. This is a tremendous victory that will hopefully ripple into restrictive states. For additional coverage, read
