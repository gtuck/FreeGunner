The Nighthawk Double Agent: The Finest Concealed Carry 1911

The Nighthawk Double Agent 1911 double-stack is more than a tool. It's a hand-crafted pistol that is as much art as it is machine.

 The Nighthawk Double Agent is double-stack 1911 chambered in 9mm. (Brad Fitzpatrick photo)

May 14, 2026

By Brad Fitzpatrick

Double-stack 9mm 1911s are surging in popularity, and perhaps the best of all is Nighthawk’s Double Agent. Designed in collaboration with Agency Arms in Idaho, the Double Agent is a concealable 1911 with a 4.25-inch barrel and a capacity of 16 rounds.
In true Nighthawk Custom fashion, the team in Berryville, Arkansas, did not simply set out to build a 4.25-inch double-stack 9mm. Instead, they designed the Double Agent to be as concealable and easy to carry as possible.
To begin, the aluminum grip is thinner than many single-stack 1911s. When Nighthawk director of marketing Landon Stone told me this, I headed to the gun room with calipers to verify. Sure enough, the Double Agent’s aluminum grip measures exactly 1.20 inches wide. All the 1911 .45s I measured—and one single-stack 9mm 1911 as well—were wider than two inches at the grip.
The only gun with a thinner grip was a 1911 9mm with flat grip panels, but even that measured 1.17 inches wide. In other words, the Double Agent offers more than twice the capacity of a .45 1911, and a 60 percent boost in capacity compared with single-stack 9mm 1911s without increasing the width of the grip. Pretty amazing.

The aluminum grip is precision machined, fits seamlessly on the steel frame and features stylish and functional diamond texturing. It offers a secure handhold on the gun without being overly aggressive and, perhaps most importantly, it doesn’t rub against the beltline with the pistol in the appendix position. No one wants a cheese grater sanding against their belly all day.
The Double Agent features a mag well that is minimized and measures just 0.0375 inch wider than the grip itself. A black anodized finish protects the grip against wear.
The folks at Nighthawk are fully committed to the grip texturing, which the firm calls Three Facet Hex, and they apply it almost everywhere else on the pistol. The rear slide serrations are replaced by the texturing, as are the front of the slide from mid-ejection port forward and the angular cuts on the barrel hood. Even the squared-off trigger guard is embellished with texturing on the front and lower portion.

The Eight Facet Hex Key-crowned muzzle on the match-grade barrel lends a stylish touch, and the frame incorporates a single-slot accessory rail. (Brad Fitzpatrick photo)
The texturing on the slide is trimmed with a relieved border that adds another aesthetic touch to an already stylish pistol. I suspected the process of machining such an intricate slide with all its angles and acres of texturing takes a while, and Landon confirmed this.
“You’re talking about a slide that spends a lot of time on the machine,” he said.
Like other components of the Double Agent, the slide is machined from premium solid billet steel. The muzzle features a modified version of Nighthawk’s signature bull-nose profile, and the bull barrel comes with Nighthawk’s Eight Facet Hex Key fully crowned muzzle. The chamber portion features flat flutes all around.
Cerakote finishes are standard on Double Agent pistols, but the version I tested came with the optional Black Out package with a black trigger, plug and bushing, and a Diamond Like Carbon-coated black barrel.
Also included on the test pistol was Nighthawk’s Everlast recoil system. This utilizes a special flat-wire recoil spring, one-piece guide rod and plug that are, like other components, made from machined 416 stainless. The flat-wire spring compresses equally throughout slide movement to help reduce muzzle flip, and with its 15,000-round lifespan you can send a lot of bullets downrange before swapping out the spring.

The Double Agent features a grip safety and hammer that, like everything else, have been minimized to function for daily carry. The beavertail is shortened and rounded, and the hammer is trimmed down to reduce weight and footprint. Despite their smaller dimensions, these controls still function properly and are easy to operate.
Shooters rarely lower the hammer manually, but the knurled, skeletonized Double Agent hammer offers positive control when doing so.
The Double Agent features extensive texturing, and while the controls are standard 1911, all parts are precision-machined and then hand-fitted. (Brad Fitzpatrick photo)
1911 Features
The controls are 1911 standard, but of course nothing from Nighthawk is truly standard, considering the close machining tolerances, hand-fitting and smart design. The manual safety is rounded and lengthened but measures just about 0.2 inch wide, so it’s easy to operate but won’t hang up.
Standard Double Agents ship with a single manual safety on the left side of the gun, but you can add an ambidextrous safety, and the test gun was so equipped. The safety moves with positive precision, clicking with authority to the Safe and Fire positions. I can imagine the builder in Berryville testing the safety and nodding silently in approval. Other controls include the premium machined slide stop and magazine release. The Double Agent also features Nighthawk’s single-slot Recon Rail.
Double Agent pistols feature Heinie SlantPro Straight Eight night sights. The metal rear sight has been contoured to prevent snags, and the face of the rear sight is slanted slightly forward to prevent hang-ups. The rear face is also serrated to cut glare, and the Straight Eight alignment is purported to be easier for the human eye to align quickly. The tritium front sight ensures the irons are visible even in low light.
The IOS adapter plate features an integral rear sight, and the cover plate sports a Heinie SlantPro Straight Eight rear sight. These fit securely on a dovetail in the slide and can be switched without losing zero. (Brad Fitzpatrick photo)
Red Dot Optic Mount
As an add-on, the test pistol also included Nighthawk’s outstanding Interchangeable Optic System or IOS. The IOS consists of interchangeable plates that dovetail to the slide. A screw and cross pin in front of the dovetail lock the system in place while allowing you to efficiently swap out plates in a hurry if you’d like. With the IOS you can switch between different 
red dot sights
 mounted on said plates or back to the iron sights without having to re-zero.
Precision machining creates a tight, clean fit between the plate and the gun, and the cover plate matches so flawlessly to the slide that you must look carefully to see the seam between them. It’s a well-thought-out and superbly executed system that adds versatility to your pistol setup.
One Gunsmith Makes the Difference
This long list of premium features makes Nighthawk pistols appealing, but what truly sets these guns apart is the attention paid to each pistol before the gun leaves the factory. Landon told me this goes beyond the company’s “One gun, one gunsmith” ethos.

Advertisement

The gunsmith who builds your Nighthawk converts a bin of machined parts into a one-of-a-kind pistol with hand-fitted components throughout. The slide-to-frame fit, barrel fit and parts fit are accomplished by carefully filing and piecing each pistol together with a level of craftsmanship that’s very rare these days.
Landon said that just a bit too much filing—even one file stroke too many—and the gunsmith must start over. That’s why each Nighthawk pistol frame is initialed by the builder, the mark of a gunsmith’s artistry.
The flat-face trigger is built in the Nighthawk factory and proved to be excellent, breaking at the low end of the 3.5- to 3.75-pound spec. (Brad Fitzpatrick photo)
Range Testing
At the range, I’d just finished firing a 25-yard five-shot group with the Double Agent and squinted toward the target.
“No way,” I said to myself.
My eyes aren’t as good as they once were, but it certainly looked like a single large black hole positioned just above the bull. It looked like a five-shot, single-hole group.

Advertisement

I broke my rule about checking targets between groups and shuffled down to look. Sure enough, five shots were centered in one large, oblong, ragged hole. Center-to-center measurement was 0.68 inch, my single most accurate five-shot group from 25 yards with a 9mm in 13 years as a gun writer.
Topped with a Trijicon RMR red dot, the Nighthawk routinely produced groups that measured at or under an inch. That’s thanks in part to the pistol’s remarkable trigger. The flat-face design allows for even pressure, and each trigger leaves the factory having been tested to break between 3.5 and 3.75 pounds. Mine was on the low end at 3.5, and the break weight was consistent across 10 shots.
After finishing the test, I had eight rounds of 
Hornady
 American Gunner 115-grain ammunition and decided to shoot one more group. That eight-shot cluster measured 1.45 inches with my digital calipers.
Yes, the Nighthawk Double Agent is extremely accurate, but I’m equally impressed by how well the gun handles. The texturing is just right, and the Ultra Hi-Cut frontstrap locks the shooter’s hand in place. Slide-to-frame fit is smooth but not tight or gritty when cycling the action, and the grip is large enough to accommodate the largest hands without issue.
The gun shoots very flat, and some of it is due to the Everlast recoil system. There’s markedly less muzzle rise when shooting this gun versus my single-stack 1911 9mm. The gun executes very fast follow-ups, and you can play tunes on steel plates. You’re never fighting a gritty trigger, a bouncy muzzle or an uncomfortable grip like those found on some other pistols.

I’ve grown accustomed to shooting optics, but there’s no iron sight setup I shoot better than the Heinie Straight Eight sights, and I’m glad they’re included. Many other 1911 shooters will likely feel the same. The Nighthawk optics plate comes with a set of integral steel rear sights so that if you do choose to run the optic you’ll have backup irons if you need them.
1911 for Concealed Carry
Let’s talk carry dynamics. With a slide that’s less than an inch wide—1.4 inches across the ambidextrous thumb safety—and a grip that’s only 1.2 inches wide, the Double Agent is reasonably sized for daily carry.
You can carry the Double Agent in the appendix position if that’s your style, but it also fits tight against the hip in a trim 1911 holster and disappears under a light fleece jacket or vest. Even with its subtle mag well it’s an easy carry, although I did carry the gun sans optic to remove some weight and reduce its footprint.
Look closely at any Nighthawk pistol and you’ll be impressed by the fine detail work. The Double Agent is no exception. I already mentioned the almost seamless fit of the IOS plate, and that goes for the aluminum grip/steel frame fit as well. The texturing is remarkably even, and there are exquisite little touches like the octagonal muzzle, the chamber flutes and the border surrounding the slide texturing.
Final Thoughts
The Double Agent is sublime, but it isn’t cheap. Starting price is $5,899, and the test gun topped $6K, thanks to the addition of the Black Out finish ($120), Everlast recoil system ($50), ambi safety ($150) and IOS system ($350). That’s up to $6,569 before adding the optic.

Advertisement

So no, the Double Agent isn’t for most shooters. But if you want the best 9mm 1911 carry gun on the market regardless of price, this is very likely what you’re looking for.
It’s more than a tool. It’s a hand-crafted pistol that will last several lifetimes, although if something weird happens Nighthawk’s warranty covers these guns by serial number. You’re protected even if you aren’t the original owner. I know you can buy about a dozen polymer-frame, striker-fired pistols for what you’ll pay for this one, but there’s something to be said for owning a gun that’s as much art as it is machine.
