July 22, 2026

By David Faubion

Aaron Voigt might be the greatest gun designer you’ve never heard of. While working as an insurance adjuster in 2010, he had a moment of inspiration for a gun design. Ultimately, this became the LifeCard pistol, a folding, credit-card-sized .22LR that resulted in Voigt founding 
Trailblazer Firearms
. 
After the LifeCard hit the market in 2017, Voigt turned his mind to a radical pistol-caliber carbine (PCC) unlike anything else. He designed it with a rotating upper receiver to minimize overall length when not in use. This PCC is called the Pivot, and it won “American Rifleman’s” Golden Bullseye for “Tactical Gun of the Year.” 
The Stowaway uses a barrel extension like an AR. To assemble, insert the barrel into the receiver and twist on the handguard. (Photo by Mark Fingar)
Voigt’s most recent design is from his new venture called 
Dark Mountain Arms
. The 
Stowaway
 is a takedown, single-​shot rifle and pistol chambered in 5.7x28. The rifle weighs just 2.8 pounds and costs $419. The Stowaway is a minimalist design that assembles in seconds and measures less than 17 inches when stowed. 
Key features of the Stowaway are its dainty barrel, miniature barrel extension, and aluminum handguard that spins on hand-tight to lock barrel to receiver. A 3-lug bolt is manipulated on the receiver’s left side. At the rear of the action, the manual striker is cocked for each shot. For stability, the Stowaway uses an aluminum tube capped with a polymer buttstock.

Does this single-shot remind you of something? It should. Voigt designed the Stowaway as a modern version of the rimfire he carried as a kid. 
“I grew up with a Chipmunk .22LR and took it everywhere,” said Voigt. “That was formative for me. The Stowaway is the same basic concept but for adults in the modern era. It had to be tiny, lightweight, functional, robust, and inexpensive. For optics usage, it ships with a rail for adding a scope or red dot, and the muzzle is threaded for a suppressor. Unlike a .22LR, the 5.7x28 packs enough punch for 75- and even 100-yard shots.”
To achieve a weight of less than 3 pounds, everything is dainty. (Photo by Mark Fingar)
When designing the Stowaway, Dark Mountain Arms didn’t waste any time. They went from concept to shipping firearms in less than a year.

“I piddled around in CAD and sketched a few things to determine a rough cost and specs,” said Voigt. “But when I decided to do this, it was August of 2023. My business partner is a mechanical engineer. He took the design across the finish line, spending countless hours in SolidWorks making it simpler, reducing parts count, and looking at it with a critical eye so it costs less. We shipped our first gun in July of 2024.”
In addition to 5.7 rifle and pistol versions, Dark Mountain also offers 9mm barrels in pistol and rifle length as well as 9mm bolts. As a kit, the barrel and bolt costs just $199.
A tiny 3-lug bolt is operated on the left side. Before each shot, pull the striker to the rear. (Photo by Mark Fingar)
“With a barrel and bolt-head swap, the Stowaway will run any rimfire, 4.6x30, and even 9mm,” said Voigt. “The only limitation for cartridges in the Stowaway is the bolt stroke. When we build a .300 BLK/5.56 version, the entire gun will be larger to handle those cartridges.”
For being tiny and lightweight, the Stowaway shoots well. It’s accurate, intuitive to use, and returns to zero after takedown. For outdoor activities like backpacking, river rafting, cycling, and 
camping
, the Stowaway is hard to beat. It could also shine as an aircraft survival rifle, as the Stowaway will disappear in even the smallest of bush planes. 
How about hunting? With expanding bullets, the 5.7x28 isn’t the best option for edible game like squirrels or rabbits, but full-metal jacket loads would minimize meat damage. With polymer tipped bullets in the 35- to 40-grain range, the 5.7 is an excellent raccoon, coyote, or bobcat cartridge out to 100 yards.

My only knock on the Stowaway is its long, spongy trigger pull. It’s no Timney. However, for a pack rifle, that’s a moot point. For plinking, popping small critters, or even self-defense if needed, the tiny Stowaway in 5.7 or 9mm works exactly as intended. 
Equipped with a small red dot like Burris’ FastFire 4 and a Dead Air Mask, a rimfire suppressor built of titanium and 17-4 steel, this is a handy package for the modern outdoor enthusiast. It weighs nothing, measures less than 17 inches when taken down, assembles in seconds, and launches a blistering .22-caliber projectile with precision.
What will Voight and Dark Mountain Arms come up with next? I have no clue. But like his LifeCard pistol, Pivot PCC, and Stowaway pack rifle, I guarantee you it will be unique.
