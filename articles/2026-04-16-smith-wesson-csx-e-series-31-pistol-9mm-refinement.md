Evaluation time is often limited with test pistols, but I’ve been fortunate to have an example of the new 
Smith & Wesson CSX E-Series
 around the office for several months. The gun has made multiple appearances on “Handguns” TV and returned to its birthplace of Maryville, Tennessee, when I shot at S&W’s newly opened academy. During that time I’ve come to appreciate how seamlessly this gun blends compact size with high capacity and excellent accuracy.
I also firmly believe the E-Series is a considerably better gun than its predecessor. 
The original CSX
, which launched in 2022, was a good pistol and delivered on a worthwhile concept—making a metal-frame single-action 9mm that competed with polymer subcompacts in terms of price and concealability. But there were some issues with the original gun, namely a middling trigger that wasn’t as smooth and easy to operate as you might expect from a single-action gun.
Also, the slide wasn’t cut for optics. You could get away with that in 2012, but by 2022 shooters wanted a red-dot-ready 
carry gun
.

The new E-Series, launched in 2025, addresses both those issues and makes other improvements. For starters, the trigger on the new gun is considerably better than the original CSX. Break weight is 5.25 pounds on average, but the most noteworthy upgrade is that the new E-Series trigger has a light take-up, a crisp wall, clean break and short reset. Like the original design, the new CSX  E-Series features a blade-style trigger safety.
New Slide
The CSX E-Series retains its original controls but adds a slide that’s cut for optics on the Shield RMSc/Holosun K footprint. (Photo courtesy of Brad Fitzpatrick)
With a new slide that’s cut to accept RMSc and 507K-footprint optics, you have lots of options when selecting a red-dot sight. Two M4 screws hold the cover plate in place, as well as the rear sight, and I used the cover plate screws to secure a 
Burris FastFire C
 onto the pistol. I also like that S&W has stamped “M4 Threads” into the slide cutout so if you need additional screws you’ll know the thread pattern.
There are less subtle improvements on the gun as well, including wraparound texturing that covers 200 percent more grip area than on the original CSX. Subcompact and compact grip modules allow you to find the perfect fit, even if you have large hands.

Magazine capacity has also increased. Where the original CSX offered 10- and 12-round magazines, the new E-Series comes with 12-, 15- and 17-round magazines with grip extensions. That’s a substantial increase in both capacity and grip size.
Like the original CSX, the E-Series is offered with a 3.1-inch barrel, but there’s a 3.6-inch option as well. Those choices mirror micro-compact competitors like the Springfield Hellcat and SIG P365.
Weight and Balance
The CSX E-Series comes with 12-, 15- and 17-round magazines, mag extensions and an extra grip module for customizing grip fit. (Photo courtesy of Brad Fitzpatrick)
With its aluminum frame, the E-Series with 3.1-inch barrel weighs just 19.7 ounces unloaded and measures 6.1 inches long. Height is 4.6 inches, and at its widest point the pistol measures 1.1 inches, so it’s easy to conceal and carry.
1911 shooters will like this gun, and indeed the E-Series shoots very much like a scaled-down John Browning pistol. You won’t mistake the E-Series trigger for that of a custom 1911, but it’s far better than the old model’s, and it’s less mushy than the triggers on many striker-fired guns.
(Accuracy results provided by the author)
The gun’s weight, texturing and beavertail design combine with the improved trigger to make the CSX E-Series a gun you can shoot accurately and quickly. From 15 yards on a bench I managed five-shot groups  as small as 1.65 inches, and the test rounds landed in the 1.6- to 2.5-inch range.

Like any true single-action semiauto, the CSX E-Series has a manual safety, and in this case it’s ambidextrous. The safety lever isn’t particularly large, but it works and fits with the trim dimensions of the pistol. I did get a bit zealous a few times, getting the ball of my thumb too close to the top of the beavertail and taking a couple licks from the slide as it cycled.
Controls
The trigger is probably the single most important upgrade. On Fitzpatrick’s sample it broke crisply at just over 5.25 pounds. (Photo courtesy of Brad Fitzpatrick)
The ambi slide stop is well designed and easy to operate with your hand in a standard firing grip. One enhancement shooters might overlook is the new ClearSight Cut, but it’s one of the best features S&W has added to its pistols in recent years. An angled cut at the rear of the chamber vents gases away from the optic and helps keep the lens clean. It’s a noteworthy upgrade over conventional pistol designs that spew debris and gas onto your optic’s lens.
The CSX E-Series is further proof that Smith & Wesson does a fantastic job listening to its customer base. The company revamped its Bodyguard .380 and hit a home run with the new 2.0 version of that gun. It remains to be seen if the CSX E-Series wins this level of approval, but the gun is deserving. If it doesn’t, the engineers in Maryville will refine and reform it into true single-action carry perfection.
