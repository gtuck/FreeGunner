By Jace Bauserman

Ask anyone who knows me well, and they’ll tell you I’m a disorganized mess when it comes to hunting and shooting gear. My family and friends tease me constantly, and my “hot mess” lifestyle gets brought up at every family function. I regularly get: "Oh, you can afford to lose it. Another company will send you their very best.” Another family go-to is: “You're so particular about how you go about each hunt but you yard-sale your gear.”
This is not because I don’t take care of my equipment. I do. I clean firearms, inspect bows, and wash clothing after every trip. The problem is everything in between. When I’m rushing home from one hunt and preparing for the next, gear tends to collect in piles. Tools disappear beneath seats, ammunition ends up in the wrong containers, and essential equipment somehow migrates to the far corners of the garage or gun room.
I usually know I own what I need. Finding it is another story.
That’s where MTM Case-Gard products have saved me. They aren’t flashy pieces of hunting gear, and they won’t attract attention at hunting camp. Nobody is going to admire a storage crate the way they might a new shotgun, bow, or pair of binoculars. But when it comes to keeping my outdoor equipment organized, protected, and ready to go, MTM cases have become a critical part of my hunting gear arsenal.

That system wasn’t exactly efficient.

I don’t see it. I don’t hear it. By the time I realize it’s missing, it has been donated to the landscape somewhere between home and camp.
I’ve replaced the same types of equipment repeatedly because my truck lacked a reliable system for organizing the items I needed most.

A Perfect Fit for My GMC
The black MTM Truck Underseat Gear Crate measures 21.3 by 9.3 by 5.5 inches inside. MTM designed it to fit beneath most full-size crew-cab rear seats without a factory underseat tray. My truck will fit three of them.
The Truck Underseat Gear Crate is excellent for ammo, broadheads, flashlights, and other hunt-gear storage. (Photo by Jace Bauserman)
The Underseat Gear Crate is specifically designed for several popular truck applications, including 2019-2026 Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra Crew Cab models and 2015-2026 Ford F-150 SuperCrew trucks.
In my GMC, the fit is excellent. The crate slides beneath the rear seat like it was made for the truck—which, in a sense, it was.
That matters because underseat storage can be difficult to use. A fixed tray may provide space but loading and unloading gear while leaning over the backseat and working in the dark, cramped area beneath the seat is no fun. If I need to retrieve an item at camp or during a roadside repair, I don’t want to empty half the truck to reach it.
Truck Underseat Gear Crate Design
The MTM Truck Underseat Gear Crate costs less than $38, yet it offers several features I appreciate in a hard-use storage container.
The case is molded from durable, high-impact polypropylene. It features a water-resistant O-ring seal, dual strong-snap latches, and padlock tabs for added security. The included dividers let you create separate compartments, or you can remove them and use the entire crate as one open storage area.
That flexibility is important because gear changes with the season.
Spacious, the Underseat Gear Crate easily stores jumper cables, tire plug kits, and more. (Photo by Jace Bauserman)
For a summer fishing trip, I might use the crate for pliers, line, tools, and tackle. During archery season, it may carry release aids, extra batteries, bow tools, and a Thermacell. During the rifle or shotgun seasons, its contents may shift again.

I don’t need a different container every time my hunting schedule changes. I need one durable box that can adapt to the job. The dividers make that possible. I can change compartment size by removing the dividers and choosing from one of the five available slots. I can also remove the dividers.
I can separate tire-repair equipment from hand tools, keep electrical items away from lubricants, and create a dedicated space for small gear that would otherwise roll around beneath the seat. If I need one large compartment, I remove the dividers. The crate gives me options instead of forcing me to organize everything the same way.
Why Removability Matters
The removable design is one of my favorite features.
A truck’s underseat area is useful, but it is not always convenient. If I’m kneeling beside the truck, reaching beneath the seat, and searching for a tire plug kit, I’m working in a tight, shadowy space. If I’m already tired, wet, cold, or in a hurry, that process becomes even more frustrating.
With the MTM crate, I pull the entire container out. I can carry it to the tailgate or set it beside the truck. I can open it, see what’s inside, and return it when I’m finished.
That may sound like a small convenience. It isn’t.

Good storage equipment should make life easier, not simply provide another place to hide gear. The Truck Underseat Gear Crate does both. It uses space that would otherwise go to waste while staying accessible when you need it.
Sized perfectly, the Underseat Gear Crate slides under the backseat, helping you keep gear organized and mobile. (Photo by Jace Bauserman)
I’m planning to purchase two more crates and fill the entire area beneath my rear seat with them. One can hold recovery and roadside gear. Another can carry hunting tools and field accessories. The third can hold seasonal equipment.
That may sound excessive, but ask anyone who spends several months living out of a truck: there is no such thing as too much organized storage.
Final Thoughts
MTM Cases have brought order to the chaos created by my hunting schedule. Before I discovered MTM, my garage and gun room were collections of half-unpacked trips, misplaced tools, and gear waiting for a permanent home. I wasn’t careless. I was busy, and without adequate storage, busy quickly turned into clutter. My truck was a nightmare, and I was embarrassed to have anyone ride with me.
The MTM TUGC has been especially useful in my GMC. It fits beneath the rear seat, creates organization where there was previously confusion, and comes out of the truck when I need to load or unload gear. Its dividers, water-resistant seal, strong latches, and tough polypropylene construction make it ready for the daily abuse associated with hunting and travel.

Most importantly, it keeps the tools and equipment I need from disappearing into the landscape.
That means less searching, fewer replacement purchases, and more time focused on what matters: getting to camp, making the hunt, helping a buddy, and enjoying the outdoors with family and friends.
