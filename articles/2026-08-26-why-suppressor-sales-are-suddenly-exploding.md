Why Suppressor Sales Are Suddenly Exploding

Faster approvals, lower costs and growing acceptance have transformed suppressors from a niche accessory into one of the hottest segments of the firearms market.

 Suppressor sales are suddenly exploded, but what is the reason behind the sudden interest in what was once a niche item? (Photo by David M. Fortier)

August 26, 2026

By David Fortier

For decades, 
suppressors
 occupied a relatively small corner of the American firearms market. Interest certainly existed, but ownership remained limited by a combination of government regulation, lengthy approval times, additional costs and widespread misconceptions. Thirty years ago 
suppressor owners
 were part of a very small group of shooters who were willing to try to understand how to correctly submit NFA paper work. The resources readily available today did not exist back then and very few were willing to try to navigate the system.
Today, however, the suppressor market looks dramatically different. Throughout 2026, demand has surged at a pace few industry observers would have predicted only a few years ago. Manufacturers are expanding production, retailers are reporting strong sales and consumers who once had little interest in suppressors are now actively seeking them out. What was once considered a specialized product has rapidly moved into the mainstream.
Record Numbers Tell the Story
The scale of the current suppressor boom is difficult to ignore. According to figures compiled by the 
National Shooting Sports Foundation
, more than 845,000 suppressor applications were submitted during the first five months of 2026 alone. During that same period, approximately 768,000 approvals were issued. Those numbers represent a level of activity that would have seemed unrealistic not long ago.
Statistics alone do not explain why suppressor ownership is growing so rapidly, but they clearly illustrate the scope of the trend. More Americans are purchasing suppressors than ever before, and the factors driving that growth are relatively easy to identify.

Cost Is No Longer the Obstacle It Once Was
One of the most significant developments has been the elimination of the $200 federal tax burden that long accompanied suppressor purchases. For generations, that tax served as both a financial hurdle and a psychological barrier. While $200 may not seem overwhelming in the context of a premium firearm purchase, it represented an additional cost that many shooters simply chose to avoid.
Removing that burden immediately changed the equation. 
Suppressors
 became easier to justify financially, particularly for shooters who had been considering a purchase but were hesitant to absorb the added expense. What had once been viewed as a premium accessory suddenly became far more accessible. Plus, eliminating the tax opened the door for very inexpensive suppressors. Before, a $200 suppressor sounded inexpensive until you added on the $200 tax. But today, a $199 suppressor is just $199. This has led to many new economical designs being offered. Suppressors remain regulated under the National Firearms Act, but eliminating the tax removed one of the most obvious obstacles standing between interested consumers and ownership.
Faster Approvals Changed Consumer Behavior
If cost was one barrier, approval times were another. For years, suppressor buyers routinely faced waits measured in months and, in some cases, more than a year. I’ve had multiple suppressors that took 14+ months before approval. Many shooters simply lost interest after learning how long they would need to wait before taking possession of a product they had already purchased.

That situation has changed substantially. Improvements in ATF processing, particularly through electronic filing systems, have shortened wait times dramatically. In many instances, approvals are arriving in weeks or even days rather than months.
The impact of this shift extends beyond convenience. Consumers are generally far more willing to purchase a regulated product when they know they will receive it in a reasonable timeframe. The difference between waiting a couple weeks and waiting a year cannot be overstated. Faster approvals have transformed suppressor ownership from a long-term commitment into a straightforward purchasing decision.
Shooters are realizing the importance of protecting the hearing of both young and old. (Photo by David M. Fortier)
Understanding Replaced Myth
Another factor contributing to the suppressor boom is a growing understanding of what suppressors actually do. Popular culture has spent decades portraying them as devices capable of reducing gunfire to little more than a whisper. Those depictions may make for entertaining movies, but they bear little resemblance to reality.
A suppressor reduces sound, but it does not eliminate it. A suppressed centerfire rifle firing supersonic ammunition is not hearing safe. What suppressors primarily accomplish is reducing muzzle blast, limiting concussion and making firearms more comfortable to shoot. Many shooters also notice reduced recoil and less fatigue during extended range sessions. More importantly they can greatly reduce or eliminate hearing loss that would normally occur from firing unsuppressed firearms.
As knowledge has spread, suppressors have increasingly been viewed not as exotic accessories but as practical tools. That shift in perception has played a major role in expanding their appeal beyond dedicated firearms enthusiasts.

Hunters Helped Drive the Market
The hunting community deserves considerable credit for helping normalize suppressor ownership. As more states legalized suppressor use while hunting, sportsmen began recognizing their practical benefits. A suppressor can make a lightweight hunting rifle noticeably more comfortable to shoot, particularly when chambered for hard-kicking cartridges.
Reduced muzzle blast also benefits those hunting near other hunters. The ability to protect hearing while maintaining awareness of the surrounding environment is particularly appealing in the field. Unlike hearing protection worn on a shooting range, a suppressor does not interfere with the ability to hear approaching game or communicate with hunting partners. The health aspect preventing permanent hearing loss cannot be overstated. As hunters adopted suppressors in greater numbers, the market expanded beyond the tactical and recreational segments that had traditionally driven demand.
Adjustable gas systems, suppressor-ready muzzle devices and shorter barrel configurations have become increasingly common as suppressors grow in popularity. (Photo by David M. Fortier)
Industry Investment Followed Consumer Demand
Manufacturers have responded aggressively to the changing market. Many major firearms companies now offers suppressors directly or maintain partnerships with suppressor manufacturers. Product catalogs continue to grow as companies compete for customers seeking specialized solutions.
Today's buyers can choose from lightweight titanium suppressors, compact models designed for short-barreled rifles, dedicated rimfire suppressors and modular systems capable of serving multiple firearms. Competition has improved quality while increasing consumer choice. It has also driven prices down. In many respects, the suppressor market now resembles the optics market of two decades ago, rapidly expanding and increasingly competitive.
The influence extends beyond suppressors themselves. Firearm manufacturers are designing rifles and carbines specifically with suppressor use in mind. Adjustable gas systems, suppressor-ready muzzle devices and shorter barrel configurations have become increasingly common. Ammunition manufacturers are also expanding their offerings, introducing both new calibers and subsonic loads for existing calibers.
Still Plenty of Room to Grow
Despite impressive sales numbers, suppressor ownership remains relatively uncommon when compared to overall firearm ownership. Millions of gun owners have never fired a suppressed firearm. That reality suggests the market still has considerable room for expansion.
Many suppressor purchases occur after a shooter has firsthand experience with one. Friends introduce friends, range sessions spark interest and hunting partners demonstrate practical advantages in the field. Unlike many firearm accessories that rely heavily on marketing, suppressors often sell themselves once shooters experience them firsthand. That organic growth is one reason many industry observers believe the trend is far from over.
With the rise of suppressors ammunition companies have responded by introducing new cartridges and dedicated subsonic loads for existing cartridges. (Photo courtesy

From Niche Product to Mainstream Accessory
The suppressor market of 2026 bears little resemblance to the market of a decade ago. Faster approvals, lower costs, broader acceptance and increased product availability have combined to create one of the most significant shifts in the modern firearms industry.

Advertisement

It is clear that suppressors have moved well beyond their former status as niche products. They are increasingly viewed as practical tools by hunters, recreational shooters, competitors and firearms enthusiasts alike. For an industry constantly searching for clues about future consumer demand, the suppressor boom may be one of the clearest signals available. With the 
demise of NFA registration
 seemingly upon us, interest is likely to explode. We just have to wait and see what comes next.
