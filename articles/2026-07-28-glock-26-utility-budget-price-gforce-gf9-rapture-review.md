Glock 26 Utility, Budget Price: GForce GF9 Rapture Review

Does this sub-$300 clone offer true everyday carry reliability?

 Wrapping it up, the GForce G9 Rapture is a good pistol for somebody on a tight budget. (Frank Melloni photo)

July 28, 2026

By Frank Melloni 

Effectively a Glock 26 clone, the new GForce GF9 Rapture is a polymer-frame, striker-fired semiauto that keeps a traditional form factor at hand and is served by one of the largest aftermarkets available. Additionally, it’s made in the U.S.A., elevating GForce to manufacturer status from its foothold as an importer.
Features
I liked what GForce did with the grip angle. It’s closer to a 1911 
than it is to a Glock
, and that will appeal to a lot of shooters. The downside is it kills the gun’s compatibility with Glock-pattern magazines.
The GF9 Rapture comes with one 12-rounder or one 10-rounder, depending on state compliance, with options to buy more on the website—including an extended 16-round mag. Magazines run between $25 and $29, putting them on par with Glock-style mags in the pricing department. Moreover, they are made with metal bodies, making them a little better in my opinion.
The overall fit and finish of the frame exceeded my expectations for a $275 pistol, especially when you consider that additional colors are available, too. I enjoyed the granular side panels, and the same texture is found on the front- and backstraps.

While the controls are Glock compatible, the GF9 Rapture’s grip has more of a 1911 angle, which many shooters will find helps the pistol point better. (Frank Melloni photo)
There’s also a pad on each side of the frame to help your thumb stay put under recoil and provide a tactile resting place for your trigger finger. The rounded trigger guard is plain, and there’s no accessory rail, but these traits do ensure more holster compatibility.
The GF9 Rapture’s controls are compatible with most aftermarket Glock parts, should you want to upgrade. These include a right-handed slide stop and a reversible magazine release. The only safety, aside from a striker block, is the trigger lever. An ambidextrous takedown lever is located in the usual spot, and disassembly does involve pressing the trigger, a feature I’ve never cared for.
Just like the frame, the slide is available in different finishes. My sample featured the nickel-plated option. It was also the version that incorporates an optics cut, and I took advantage of this to mount a Riton 3 Tactix MPRD XL.

GForce includes two screw sets to help with the install, which is interesting. Typically, a cut is milled flat to accept the thickest screws possible. Instead, the Rapture leaves behind a pair of posts, and some ultra-slim hardware holds the sight in place. I like this setup, as the posts have no chance of coming off the slide, whereas hardware can and eventually will work loose.
The GF9 Rapture’s fiber-optic sights did co-witness with the optic.
The slide’s RMSc optic cut incorporates two threaded posts for the mounting screws and two semicircular front posts for a secure setup. (Frank Melloni photo)
Performance
GForce’s website says the gun should get a 200-round break-in period using “heavy loads,” which I could have guessed from dry handling alone. Defeating the spring took considerable force, and the slide assembly had a fair amount of grittiness, which typically works itself out on the range, but the deep cocking serrations were a help here.
During testing I thought the trigger could use a little love. I understand that the lever is a safety, but it took a noticeable amount of force to move. The actual pull was also heavy and extraordinarily gritty. The good news is the trigger can be replaced, and if it’s a utility pistol, you likely won’t notice it as much as a jaded gun writer does.
Initial groups were on par with handguns of this class and what I would deem “combat effective.” This isn’t a match-grade target pistol, but you can put pretty much any ammo in it and get minute-of-bad-guy groups inside realistic defensive distances.

Recommended

Which brings me to my favorite thing about this pistol: It eats everything. This design is one of the most reliable ones out there. I didn’t experience a single malfunction on my range day, not even during the break-in period.
The gun was a bit snappy during some controlled pairs, but again, that comes with the territory. Smaller guns will do that, and some camps say the Glock angle helps to tame this. But since the grip angle is different on the GF9 Rapture, you don’t get that perceived benefit. However, it did point better in my hand than a Glock, and I was able to get on target much faster. So, if a slower follow-up means a faster first-round hit, I’ll take that trade any day.
Wrapping it up, the GForce G9 Rapture is a good pistol for somebody on a tight budget, and if you already own a full-size Glock and want something smaller as a backup, it makes a handy companion. It’s also a good fit for those who like everything about Glocks but the grip angle, especially if they like to tinker.
Summary
I would recommend this pistol to somebody looking to put an extra gun or two around the house, somebody who wants a good base to try their hand at gunsmithing, or anybody looking for a proven self-defense design that will not break the bank.
