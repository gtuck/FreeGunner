FN 545 Tactical Review

A modern design sporting the classic .45 auto, the FN545 is a refreshing handgun to see. Here's a full review.

 (Photo by Barb Melloni)

July 17, 2026

By Barb Melloni

It’s been a hot minute since I’ve had the chance to get my hand on a .45 ACP for review, so when the opportunity came to take a closer look at the FN 545 Tactical, I took it. Fresh off the range from testing more carry gun options than I’d care to admit, I was struck with an “aha!” moment. On my desk was a bulk pack of Winchester 9mm, packaged in their limited edition USA 250 commemorative packaging. This year being the 250th anniversary of America had me thinking: What do many gun enthusiasts love just as much as 'Merica and Ol' Glory? The answer was clear… a handgun that rekindles the debate between .45 ACP and 9mm.
Taking a Closer Look at the FN 545 Tactical
The FN 545 Tactical is a striker-fired, double-action handgun chambered in .45 ACP. It features an impressive 18+1 capacity, with a 10-round option available for residents of states with capacity restrictions. Despite its increased capacity, it remains quite manageable at 31 oz. and measures 8.3 in. x 1.45 in. x 6 in. (L x W x H). A closer look at the grip reveals aggressive checkering along the front and backstraps, with a similar, yet smaller checkering pattern along the sides. An even finer stippled texture is located at the top rearward portion of the grip and frame, just below the slide stop. I appreciated the fine stippling on the rearward portion of the frame, which provided an extra layer of grip, keeping the gun secure in my hand while shooting. The only thing I’d wish was added would be that same fine stippling along the front-most portion of the frame where, for right-handed shooters, the tip of the trigger finger and support thumb naturally fall.
Three different stippling patterns can be found on the grip, designed for each part of the hand to retain proper grip during shooting. (Photo by Barb Melloni)
Continuing our way up the gun, we come to the slide. The serrations both fore and aft make easy work of slide manipulation, especially when wearing gloves or shooting in adverse conditions. Mounted atop the slide are a set of tritium three-dot suppressor-height iron sights, which FN claims to cowitness with most MRD optics. The slide is already cut to accept your preferred red dot, should you wish to forgo the irons, or at the very least utilize them as a backup sighting method. Using FN’s patented Low-Profile Optics Mounting System, shooters can mount a wide variety of red dots with ease using one of several included mounting plates. Compatibility options include Trijicon RMR/SRO, Holosun 507/508, Docter, C-More STS2, Leupold Delta Point Pro, and several Vortex offerings, just to name a few. Last but not least, we arrive at the barrel. Measuring 4.71 inches, the barrel on the 545 Tactical is threaded with a .578x28 thread pitch for out-of-the-box suppressor-ready action, further complemented by the suppressor-height iron sights mentioned previously.
The FN 545 Tactical brings the .45 into a modernized platform with 18+1 round capacity when using the extended magazine. (Photo courtesy of FN)
Next up on the docket was some well-earned range time. In an attempt to keep things interesting, I selected the widest range of .45 ACP I had available, which included the following: 
Hornady
 Critical Defense 185 grain FTX, Liberty Ammunition’s 78 grain Civil Defense +P, and Black Hills’ 230 grain JHP +P. A target was placed approximately 15 yards from our shooting position, with this distance chosen as a general middle-ground for what you may expect for accuracy, whether defending yourself against a two-legged assailant, or one of the four-legged varieties (in the case of backcountry defense). Magazine after magazine, the gun fed without issue regardless of how hot I ran the gun or which random assortment of 45 ACP I stuffed into the magazines. The gun remained well controlled during the entirety of the firing process, and I was able to get back on target quickly. The aggressive checkering along the frontstrap and backstrap did dig into my hands a little more than I would have preferred, but it wasn’t aggressive enough to stop me from sending lead downrange by any means. Coming out on top as far as accuracy was concerned was Liberty Ammunition’s 78-grain Civil Defense +P  with an average group size of one inch. Following close behind were Hornady’s Critical Defense 185-grain FTX  and Black Hills 230-grain JHP +P, with average group sizes of 1.5 inches and 1.75 inches, respectively.

A Quick Aside: The Great Caliber Debate
Nothing will stir up the comment section more than posing one simple question: Which is better for self-defense: 9mm or .45 ACP? If you’re in need of some quality entertainment, try presenting this question yourself at your local gun club one day, and sit back with some popcorn as the inevitable argument unfolds, with proponents of “the Lord’s caliber”  designed by John Moses Browning battle it out with those who are fully in support of Georg Luger’s 9mm as being superior. Points of contention that often arise include capacity -vs- size, recoil vs. shootability, general ballistics of each round, ammunition costs, and more. So, who is right? Nice try! While I refuse to enter into the ring that is potentially the longest-running caliber debate to date, it's undeniably entertaining and at times quite thought-provoking listening in to why some feel one way versus the other. What’s important is finding the right platform for your chosen cartridge, and the FN545 Tactical makes for a great one that breaks away from the classic 1911 design that dominates the normal selection of .45 Auto handguns.
Trigger pull averages between 5.5 and 7.5 pounds. (Photo Courtesy of FN)
Closing Thoughts
In the end, the FN 545 Tactical has earned its spot in my safe, and rightfully so. The inclusion of an impressive increase in capacity, optic-ready slide, suppressor height sights, and overall ergonomics all serve to keep hangduns chambered in .45 ACP still fully relevant in today’s market… even if they are overran by a horde of striker-fired polymer 9mm’s time to time. Whether you decide to pick one up for defensive use, or as an alternative to 1911's you may own, you can do so knowing FN has yet again provided a rock-solid option that’ll not only meet, but exceed your expectations.
