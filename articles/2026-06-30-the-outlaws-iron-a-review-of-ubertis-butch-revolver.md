The Outlaw's Iron: A Review of Uberti's 'Butch' Revolver

Take a trip to the Wild West with this nostalgic revolver replica.

 Inspired by historical legends, the Uberti 'Butch' replica belongs to the Outlaws and Lawmen revolver series. (J. Scott Rupp photo)

June 30, 2026

By J. Scott Rupp

His real name was Robert LeRoy Parker, but most folks know him as Butch Cassidy. The legendary outlaw plied his trade around the turn of the 20th century, robbing banks, trains and other targets—sometimes with his friend Harry Longabaugh, aka the Sundance Kid.
Uberti chose one of Cassidy’s sidearms as the inspiration for the latest addition to its 
Outlaws and Lawmen series
. The Butch is a variant of the company’s 1873 Cattleman New Model Single Action, itself a clone of the 
Colt Single Action Army
. Cassidy was known to carry an SAA, and the Butch is an exact replica of one of his favorite handguns.
It’s a six-shot chambered to .45 Colt, and Uberti has done a nice job of finishing the revolver so it looks like it could’ve actually been used in the Old West. In fact Uberti calls the finish on the 4.57-inch barrel, frame and cylinder Old West Antique.
The look calls to mind what we might describe as distressed or industrial these days. But Old West Antique exhibits a unique patina, a silvery gray that’s embellished with lighter “worn” spots throughout. It’s not over the top at all, and it really does give the Butch the appearance of having been used hard for a long time.

To complement this finish, the trigger guard and backstrap are antiqued brass, slightly rough-looking and unpolished.
The grips, by contrast, are unblemished simulated buffalo horn. They’re almost out of place considering the aesthetics on the rest of the gun, but you’ve gotta have some curb appeal. The shiny black grips accomplish this and look great. Fit and finish of grip to frame and backstrap are quite well done for a production gun.
Markings are thankfully minimal. The left side of the barrel is stamped “Mod. 1873 Cal. .45 Colt” while the top carries “Stoeger–Accoceek, MD–A. Uberti–Italy.” The serial number is stamped on the bottom of the frame, just in front of where the brass trigger guard unit attaches.

The Butch is a shooter, not a safe queen, and I do intend to get this revolver to the range soon. It’s got a great trigger, and the hammer sports a small patch of cross-hatching atop the spur. Sights are the typical blade front and topstrap-groove rear. Unlike many single actions on the market today, the Butch lacks a transfer-bar safety, so you’ll need to keep an empty chamber under the hammer.
In 2012, one of Butch Cassidy’s Colt revolvers sold at auction for $175,000. That’s pretty dear, and you might be happy to know you can pick up Uberti’s Butch for just $1,079.
