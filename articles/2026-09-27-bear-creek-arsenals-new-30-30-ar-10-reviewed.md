Bear Creek Arsenal's New .30-30 AR-10: Reviewed

Bringing .30-30 to the AR platform is a bold choice, but one that may open new avenues for this classic hunting round.

 (Photo by Barb Melloni)

September 27, 2026

By Barb Melloni

Some rifle-and-cartridge combinations seem to have been around forever; one example is the Winchester 94 and the .30-30 Winchester cartridge it was chambered for. This classic pairing has often been seen in family gun safes and deer camps, passed down from generation to generation. So, when Bear Creek Arsenal 
decided to take the tried-and-true .30-30 cartridge and put it into a modern AR-10 platform
, my first thought was: They did what? The more time I spent looking at this new rifle, the more it started to make sense. With their new release, Bear Creek took a cartridge that had spent more than 130 years taking deer and creating memories in the wooded landscape of American history and put it into a modern, semiautomatic platform, rocketing it into the 21st century.
Why Build an AR Around the .30-30?
At first, an AR-10 in .30-30 sounds like an answer to a question nobody asked. Then you start thinking about what modern sporting rifles do well. The AR platform gives a hunter adjustable ergonomics, straightforward optic mounting, familiar controls, and semiautomatic operation. Those qualities don't make the .30-30 a different cartridge, of course; they just change how the shooter interacts with it. For someone who grew up with lever guns, the BC-10 may look unconventional, and honestly, it is... but not in a bad way. For someone with considerably more time behind an AR, the opposite may be true. That is probably the strongest argument for a rifle like this. The goal of the BC-10 being chambered in .30-30 wasn't to reinvent the cartridge, but instead to present the cartridge in a format that may be more familiar and even more appealing to a different generation of shooters. The unconventional pairing could also spark interest in those who otherwise might not have given the cartridge a second glance. Aside from that, there's an undeniable appeal in seeing a cartridge introduced when smokeless powder was still relatively new standing the test of time and reappear this way. The .30-30 never really went away, but putting it in an AR-10 gives it a fresh context without pretending it suddenly became something that it's not.
A beefy upper receiver and side charging handle immediately sets the BC10 apart from other large frame ARs visually. (Photo courtesy of Bear Creek Arsenal)
An Old Cartridge in a New Package
The .30-30 Winchester cartridge was released in 1895, and once it was adopted by the Model 1894 released a year prior, it quickly became a favorite amongst hunters. Some have touted it as the most famous deer rifle-and-ammunition pairing ever made. It was a practical, soft-shooting cartridge and accurate enough for the average distances at which hunters harvested deer. Tubular magazines naturally oriented rounds nose-to-primer, which led to the widespread use of round-nose projectiles. This, of course, limited some of the .30-30's ballistic potential, especially when compared to modern cartridges and projectiles available today. The departure from the traditional lever-gun platform is a large part of what makes this rifle so interesting. Instead of sticking to what has worked for well over a century, Bear Creek broke the mold and is offering consumers something different.
The BC-10: Familiar AR Controls, Different Personality
Bear Creek’s new semi-auto BC-10, as noted above, is chambered in .30-30 and built on the AR-10 platform. It has a 20-inch SOCOM-profile barrel complete with a black nitride finish, a 1:12 twist, and a mid-length gas system. The barrel is topped with a spiral flash hider that you can remove and swap for another muzzle device of your choice. Despite its modern revamp, the design choices behind the BC-10 still honor what made the original cartridge-and-rifle pairing so popular. Instead of taking things too far by chopping the barrel down into SBR territory or stretching it into something more akin to a long-range precision build, the new BC-10's barrel is set at twenty inches. This length keeps it in line with other popular current production hunting rifles and still lets you use it in a ground blind or tree stand. If you prefer more of a spot-and-stalk approach to hunting, you'll be in for a workout: the gun weighs just over eight pounds, but hey... that sounds like a perfect excuse to skip arm day at the gym! Another feature that ties this modern revamp back to its roots is the included five-round magazine, more appropriate for the type of hunting the rifle would likely be used for versus standard-capacity thirty-round AR mags.

Standard AR controls and grip surfaces paired with the classic .30-30 make for a novel shooting experience that also performs surprisingly well. (Photo by Barb Melloni)
This .30-30 is outfitted with a 15-inch free-float MLOK split-rail handguard with plenty of space for additional accessories. While the rifle doesn’t come with any flip-up sights, it has about seven inches of Picatinny rail space for mounting your preferred optic. The new BC-10 utilizes a right-sided charging handle instead of one located in the rear. If you're like me and, for no special reason, always seem to shoot rear-charging AR's, the change in placement may seem awkward at first. Ok, let’s be honest, it’s not a matter of it may… it will, but that has nothing to do with the rifle and more to do with you as a shooter. Have you ever gone a stretch of time shooting only semi-autos, then decided to dust off your old bolt gun, only to wonder why it won't fire two rounds in succession? If you’ve solved that self-imposed question with the realization “Hey stupid, you’re shooting a bolt-action now,” then you know what I’m talking about. No big deal, just stick with it, because after even a single afternoon range trip, operation becomes quicker and much smoother than when you started. You may even come to appreciate some of the benefits of side-chargers, such as better ergonomics, the ability to maintain your cheek weld while cycling the action, and eliminating any interference with your optic.
Continuing rearward, things remain fairly straightforward. I appreciated the rubberized grip, as it felt comfortable in the hand and provided more control than the slicker, less ergonomic feel of bare-bones polymer alternatives. Last but not least, there’s the included buttstock that gives you six adjustment points to find the best fit. While it seems undersized and doesn’t include an adjustable cheek riser, the rifle has an MSRP of $899.95, which leaves enough wiggle room in the budget to swap it out as you see fit.
The buttstock is serviceable, but benefits the shooting experience by being replaced with a more adjustable alternative. (Photo by Barb Melloni)
Range Setup and Testing
Eager to see how the classic .30-30 cartridge performed out of its "modern delivery system," I topped off the BC-10 with a Burris Fullfield IV 4-16x50 riflescope, mounted securely within a Warne XSKEL One-Piece Cantilever Mount. With its Ballistic E3 illuminated reticle, you get eleven brightness levels, which are much needed during dim first- and last-light shooting conditions. Given the capabilities of .30-30 and the area to be hunted, a deer would likely be harvested at a maximum distance of 250 yards. This reticle also has reference points out to 400 yards on the lower vertical crosshair, allowing quick target acquisition at varying distances, with additional reference points to account for a crosswind should conditions get breezy and force you to rely on Kentucky windage to land your shot. After setting up 100 yards from our target, it was time to get to work. First up was 
Nosler’s Whitetail Country
, loaded with a solid, heavy base projectile and tapered jacket for optimal penetration and expansion. Second in line was 
Hornady
’s American Whitetail
, whose patented Interlock bullet design retains as much mass as possible during expansion by more or less “locking” the bullet’s core and jacket together.

Last but not least was another offering from Nosler, 
specifically their 150gr Ballistic Tip hunting load
. While the round-nose bullet profile was retained, Nosler skipped an exposed lead tip and used a polymer ballistic tip instead. This lets the projectile resist deformation, fly flatter, and drive deep into the deer, expanding rapidly even as its velocity declines. The BC-10 fed all three loadings without any hang-ups, with overall function becoming noticeably smoother after applying ALG Go-Juice. As stated before, the buttstock left much to be desired, but because I used a Caldwell rest for support and shot from a bench-rest position, it quickly became a non-issue. The glass on our Fullfield was crystal clear, letting me check groups in position rather than doing 100-yard dashes all afternoon. Looking back at the results, the three loads were relatively close to one another in all manners related to ballistics. Across the board, average group sizes fell under 2.25 inches, with SD’s and velocities following suit.
(Photo by Barb Melloni)
A New Chapter for an American Classic
Calling the BC-10 a "rebirth" of the .30-30 would be inaccurate, as the cartridge never really went away. Since its release in 1895, manufacturers have continually developed new loadings and rifles chambered to accept it. What has changed is the market, the very clientele that must be kept intrigued enough to want to give the cartridge a try. Not every shooter is going to want to run around the woods with a lever-action or even a bolt-gun, regardless of what it’s chambered in. But the second you put even the most antiquated cartridge in a modern semi-auto platform, especially one as popular as an AR, it almost becomes like new again… just like the day it first hit the shelves of small-town gun shops and hardware stores. Regardless of how you feel about the pairing, you have to give Bear Creek credit for understanding that they didn't need to reinvent an American classic. By enabling .30-30 to be utilized in a modern platform just as loved as the cartridge itself, they’ve played an important role in keeping history and tradition alive. For a cartridge that has spent over a century proving that simple can work, that seems like a fitting approach.
