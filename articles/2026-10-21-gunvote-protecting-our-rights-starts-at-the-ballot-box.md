#GUNVOTE: Protecting Our Rights Starts at the Ballot Box

Elections are fast approaching, and the NSSF have launched an initiative to help you keep our firearms safe and in the hands of the people.

October 21, 2026

By Guns & Ammo Staff

For America’s gun owners, hunters and shooting-sports enthusiasts, exercising the right to vote is an essential part of preserving the traditions and freedoms we value.
Responsible gun ownership requires preparation, knowledge and a commitment to doing things right. Whether preparing for hunting season, heading to the range, or introducing a new shooter to firearms safety, success begins long before you place a finger on the trigger. It comes from understanding what is at stake, taking the necessary steps and following through.
That is the idea behind 
#GUNVOTE
, the 
National Shooting Sports Foundation’s (NSSF)
 voter registration and education campaign. Built around the straightforward message “Don’t Risk Your Rights — #GUNVOTE,” the initiative encourages firearm owners, hunters and sportsmen to register to vote, understand the issues that affect their interests and make their voices heard at the ballot box.
The campaign’s 
main website, GunVote.org
, provides a starting point for Americans who want to know more about the electoral process, registration, and how to vote on the issues that matter. Its message is simple: Register, get informed, and vote. For those who value the Second Amendment and America’s long commitment to an armed citizenry, it is a message worth taking seriously.
The Importance of Every Vote
The right to keep and bear arms is protected by the U.S. Constitution, but the laws and policies policing and registering firearms (however unconstitutional they may be) are shaped by elected officials at every level of government. From state legislatures and governors’ offices to Congress and the White House, decisions made by public officials can have significant consequences for firearm ownership, shooting sports, hunting opportunities and the businesses that support the industry. Those decisions do not happen on their own. Elected officials respond to legislation, public opinion and the priorities of their constituents, but only if they make themselves heard.

That makes voting an important responsibility for anyone concerned about the future of firearm rights.
Elections can be decided by relatively small margins, particularly in competitive districts. Millions of Americans own firearms, and gun owners are spread throughout the country, from rural communities, to suburbs and major cities. They come from different professions, backgrounds and political affiliations, united by interests that may include recreational shooting, hunting, collecting, personal protection or simply preserving a constitutional freedom. However, a large potential voting bloc matters only when its members participate in democracy.
A gun owner who is eligible to vote but has not registered cannot cast a ballot. A registered voter who misses an election forfeits an opportunity to influence its outcome; and a voter who enters the polling place without understanding the candidates or issues may be less prepared to make an informed decision and vote for the candidate that best represents their views. #GUNVOTE seeks to close those gaps by encouraging participation before Election Day arrives.

Registration Is Only the First Step
Never assume that registration is a job that can wait until later. Election deadlines, busy work schedules, family commitments and the everyday demands of life lower the urgency of voter registration in day to day life, until suddenly the deadline has arrived, or worse, passed.
Voter registration rules vary by state, and requirements for registration, address changes, absentee voting and early voting can differ considerably. Waiting until the final days before an election can create unnecessary complications or leave eligible voters without enough time to complete the process.
Visiting GunVote.org is a practical first step for those who need to begin the registration process or want to learn more about participating in upcoming elections. Voters should also verify their registration status and check official state and local election resources for applicable deadlines, identification requirements and voting options.
Once registered, voters should take time to research the candidates seeking their support. Where do they stand on the Second Amendment? What positions have they taken on firearm legislation, hunting and shooting sports? How do their public statements, voting records and policy proposals align with the issues that matter to their communities?
An informed voter looks beyond campaign slogans and considers the available evidence. Reviewing official candidate materials, legislative voting records and credible reporting can help voters distinguish between promises, proposals and demonstrated positions.
#GUNVOTE is an educational initiative, not an endorsement of a political party or candidate. Its purpose is to encourage firearm owners and sportsmen to participate in the process and make their own informed decisions. That distinction matters. Civic engagement is strongest when voters understand the issues, evaluate the information available to them and decide for themselves which candidates best represent their interests and principles.

More Than the Second Amendment
(Photo by Mark Fingar)
For many Americans, the motivation to vote extends beyond firearm ownership alone. Hunting, conservation, public-land access and the future of the shooting sports are also important considerations.
The recent victories against the National Firearms Act (NFA) throughout 2026 have been monumental, and are a direct result of elected officials cooperating with gun owners to make suppressors and other NFA-regulated goods more widely available. Voting gives citizens an opportunity to help shape the leadership responsible for those decisions.
Make Voting Part of the Sportsman's Ethic
There is a familiar rhythm to a successful day in the field or at the range. Preparation comes first. Equipment is checked. Rules are understood. Safety remains a priority. When the time comes to act, the work put in beforehand makes a difference. Civic participation deserves the same attention.
Registering early, learning about candidates and planning when and how to vote are practical ways to prepare for an election. Checking deadlines and confirming registration may not be as exciting as sighting in a rifle before opening day, but both are examples of taking responsibility before the moment arrives. A hunting license does not put a hunter in the field, and a voter registration does not put a ballot in the box. Participation requires action.
For gun owners, hunters and shooting-sports enthusiasts, that action can help ensure that their perspectives are represented in decisions affecting their rights, recreational opportunities and communities.
It is also an opportunity to set an example. Hunting partners can remind one another to check registration deadlines. Shooting clubs and outdoor organizations can share nonpartisan voter education resources and encourage eligible members to participate.
No single election determines every aspect of America's firearms policy or outdoor future. The process is ongoing, and the officials elected today may influence decisions for years to come.
Gun owners cannot assume that someone else will represent their interests. Hunters cannot take future access and conservation opportunities for granted. And the shooting-sports community cannot afford to treat voter registration as a task that matters only when a particularly prominent election approaches; the responsibility belongs to each eligible citizen.

The NSSF's #GUNVOTE initiative offers a clear place to begin. Visit GunVote.org to learn more about voter registration and election participation, then consult official election resources to confirm your registration, understand your state's requirements and make a plan to vote.
Take the time to learn where candidates stand on the issues that matter to you. Evaluate their records and proposals. Make your own decisions, and follow through when Election Day arrives.
Protecting constitutional freedoms and preserving America's outdoor traditions require more than words. They require informed citizens who are willing to participate in the democratic process. For firearm owners and sportsmen, voting is another way to demonstrate the responsibility, preparation and stewardship that define the community.
