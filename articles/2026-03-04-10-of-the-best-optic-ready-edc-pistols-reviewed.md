10 of the Best Optic-Ready EDC Pistols: Reviewed 

These new EDC pistols leave the factory with slides cut for red dots. Check out their in-depth reviews.

March 04, 2026

By Guns & Ammo Staff

Everyday carry (EDC) is one of the largest market segments of firearms, period. There are plentiful EDC pistol options available, but the greatest demand now is for optic-ready models. An EDC handgun blends shootability and concealability. Adding a red dot adds precise aiming and rapid target transitions, provided you put the practice in.
Despite the demand for red dots on handguns, many pistols still leave the factory sans optic. To give you a starting point for factory-made handguns that ship with a red-dot ready slide, we’ve assembled 10 of our favorites, complete with extensive, complete G&A reviews by expert staff. Really, this is just a starting point, as there are many great optic-ready EDC pistols available, but we’ve put together a great variety that even includes some California-Compliant models.
Whatever you choose to carry, optic or not, be sure to practice and practice often. You can’t buy performance, and proficiency can only be bought with training. Consider your daily carry needs and choose what best fits you. There isn’t a wrong answer, as all the pistols below proved highly reliable to our contributors. Be sure to break in your handgun with at least 300 rounds before carry, and you’ll be well-prepared.
S&W M&P9 M2.0 Metal Carry Comp
In addition to leaving the factory with optic-cut slides, many new handguns also include an attached or integral compensator. G&A contributor Brad Fitzpatrick gave a 
complete review of the Smith & Wesson M&P9 M2.0 Metal Carry Comp
. Fitzpatrick was most impressed bu the gun’s shootability. The all-metal construction adds a comfortable weight, which greatly reduces felt recoil. With 17-round magazines and the metal construction, it may be heavy on a waist holster, but that wouldn’t be noticed as a backpack gun or even a fanny pack. This handgun slide is compatible with most full-size red-dot sights available on the market today, and it also has a rail for mounting lights or laser sights.

Canik Mete MC9 Prime
Handguns Editor James Tarr was highly impressed with the 
Canik Mete MC9 Prime
, a U.S.-manufactured, optic-ready, 17+1 capacity compact handgun. Tarr writes, “The Prime gives us everything we need in a carry gun, including a holster.” The Prime is the complete package, sans optic. It ships inside a lockable hard case with custom foam cutouts for the pistol and accessories. The guns ships with two 17-round magazines, multiple backstrap options, an IWB hoslter, and even a MC9 Prime keychain. Feature-rich, the MC9 Prime includes an optic-cut slide, accessory rail, flat-faced trigger shoe, tritium night sights and more.
Springfield Armory Echelon 4.0C
If capacity is a priority in your compact EDC pistol, look no further than the Springfield Armory Echelon 4.0C. With an extended 18-round magazine in addition to a flush-fit 15-round mag, the 4.0C is a great option within Sprinfield’s flagship Echelon lineup. 
Keith Wood puts the Echelon 4.0C through its paces here.
 Wood noted that the Echelon offers custom-level handgun performance in a factory package. G&A’s in-house legal expert, Wood also advises a factory gun is easier to defend in court should the need arise. The Echelon 4.0C uses Springfield’s established Agency Optic System (AOS), which is compatible with many red dot optics. A chassis design, the Echelon’s only serialized part is the Central Operating Group (COG), which surrounds the trigger. The Echelon series has many other models, too, including comped and California-compliant editions.
Ruger RXM
Rarely surprised, Handguns Editor Jim Tarr was blown away by the introduction of 
the Ruger RXM,
 a collaboration between Sturm, Ruger & Co. and Magpul. This Glock-stylized 9mm blends the expertise of both companies, and it is set up for extensive modularity. With its design, the RXM can take advantage of the extensive holster market, not to mention all the aftermarket Glock accessories it is compatible with. At the range, Tarr found the RXM to be accurate and reliable. The tritium sights sit just high enough to co-witness through most full-size red dots, too. For those that like Glock-style pistols but would prefer to buy American, then the Ruger RXM has a lot to offer.

Rost Martin RM1S
They say American manufacturing is gone, but Rost Martin has proved otherwise. This Texas-based pistol manufacturer has entered the market in force, and impressively does so at an affordable price. 
The RM1S is the subcompact model
 within the company’s line, and Jim Tarr was pleased to see such a high-performing U.S.-made handgun in a foreign-dominated market. The RM1S includes a flush-fit 12-round and extended 15-round magazine, which is essentially the standard for EDC pistols. The grip texture is particularly effective, and Tarr turned in excellent accuracy groups despite winter conditions. Of course, the RM1S leaves the factory with an optic-cut slide, and mounting a carry red dot proved a cinch.
Taurus TX9
While all three models of the 
new Taurus TX9
 are optic ready, the Taurus TX9 Compact and Subcompact are ideal EDC optic-ready pistols. Representing the latest flagship of Taurus duty-grade pistols, Tarr found the TX9 to be one of the most ergonomically advantageous designs yet, making them highly shootable. Much of the design is actually based on the Taurus TX22 rimfire handgun. The pistol is so popular that it heavily influenced the ergonomics of the TX9. For optic mounting, the TX9 takes advantage of the establish T.O.R.O. mounting system. To keep pricing down, you’ll need to order a specific-pattern mounting plate from shoptaurus.com. Like most modern EDC pistols, the TX9 is feature rich, yet it ships at the ever-affordable Taurus price point.
FN Reflex XL MRD
Everyday carry continues to be one of the most popular segments of firearms on the market. Handguns went from micro-sized back to mid-sized compacts, blending carry ability, capacity and performance. 
The FN Reflex XL MRD
 name reflects this perfectly. The original FN Reflex is ultra concealable, but Brad Fitzpatrick greatly prefers the additional grip purchase of the XL model. Though larger, it is still easy to conceal, and it offers outstanding capacity with options for 10-, 15 and 18-round magazines. Available in black or FN’s unique shade of FDE (perhaps to match your SCAR), the Reflex XL is also an internal-hammer fired pistol, as opposed to the striker-fire models for most of this roundup. The internal hammer offers a smoother, lighter trigger than most striker guns. It’s harder to manufacture, though, which requires expertise from companies such as FN.
Smith & Wesson Shield X
As opposed to the all-metal S&W Carry Comp in this roundup, 
the Shield X
 blends the design of the S&W Shield pistols with the Bodyguard 2.0 size. It’s a compact pistol with standard 15-round capacity and greatly impressed Jim Tarr on the range. Despite its compact size, the Shield X still achieves a 15-round capacity with the extended magazine. For shooting, Tarr liked the larger, squared triggerguard, and the trigger pull with smooth and consistent. The Ameriglo day/night sights co-witness through some red dots, but there are plenty of aftermarket tall sights available, too. They’re quick to swap since S&W sights are so prolific. The Shield X is available with and without a manual safety. It’s a true functional upgrade to the original Shield design.
Shadow Systems MR920 CA-Compliant
For those needing to work around the restrictive, unlawful regulations of California and similar states, California-resident Alfredo Rico was duly impressed with the 
Shadow Systems MR920 CA-Compliant model.
 Rico writes that the MR920 has the same features as many “tricked-out” Glocks he’s seen. In many ways, Shadow Systems is filling a market gap, providing features that customers now demand and that Glock arguably should already be providing. Shadow Systems does so with arguably a superior pistol and a superior price. It’s far less expensive than customizing a stock Glock. For Rico, the CA-Compliant MR920 was a joy to shoot, and it offers protection in a state that is historically limiting of feature-rich handguns.

SIG Sauer P365 CA-Compliant
Another CA-Compliant pistol of note is 
the iconic SIG Sauer P365.
 It’s the subcompact pistol that set the market on fire, and Alfredo Rico quickly took advantage when a California-compliant model became available. Though it can carry more, it’s limited to 10 rounds by state regulation. However, extra magazines are readily available, so plan your carry accordingly. In addition to being optic ready, there are extensive light and laser sight options custom-fit for the P365. For testing, Rico mounted a Riton 3 Tactix MPRD3 red dot. At 25-yards, the gun turned in tight groups with the precise 3-MOA aiming dot. Even with a compliant model, it’s hard to beat the P365.
