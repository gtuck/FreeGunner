The Best 6.5 Creedmoor Rifles Under $2000 with Reviews

If you're looking to try out the flat-shooting 6.5 Creedmoor, or just want another rifle to add to the collection, we have some favorites reviewed by G&A staff.

April 08, 2026

By Guns & Ammo Staff

The 6.5 Creedmoor has firmly established itself as one of the most capable all-around cartridges for hunters and precision shooters. Its efficiency, mild recoil, and long-range consistency make it ideal for medium to large game and long-distance target shooting.
With the cartridge's popularity on the rise, there are plenty of rifles now available to choose from. We took some of our favorite reviews for rifles priced at or below $2,000 that can serve as great introductory platforms to the round. The key considerations are accuracy potential, barrel quality, action design, weight, and field practicality. Some rifles in this category emphasize lightweight mobility, while others lean toward precision or better ergonomics.
The following rifles, tested by Guns & Ammo, represent the best-performing options in this price bracket. Full rifle specifications and pricing can be found in our reviews.
CZ 557 Eclipse
The 
CZ 557 Eclipse
 proves that a rifle doesn’t need a premium price tag to deliver excellent performance.

Built on a CNC-machined push-feed action with a cold hammer-forged barrel, the Eclipse offers durability and accuracy typically found in more expensive rifles. As G&A testers noted, “Accuracy was exceptional for any production rifle shooting factory ammunition. Three-shot groups averaged .63 inch across the board.”
Its adjustable trigger, tunable from roughly 2 to 4 pounds, is one of the best in its class, providing a crisp break that aids precision shooting. Combined with integral scope bases and a threaded barrel, the Eclipse delivers fantastic value. 
For hunters looking for maximum performance per dollar and a solid steel action, this rifle is hard to beat.

Springfield Armory Model 2020 Boundary
Built around a custom-grade action with dual locking lugs, Springfield Armory’s Model 2020 Boundary offers smooth cycling and consistent lockup. Its TriggerTech Field trigger enhances practical accuracy, while a switch from a detachable box-style to internal magazine allows for longer cartridges to be chambered, along with a streamlined profile.
Though the carbon fiber version reviewed by Guns & Ammo fell outside the $2,000 benchmark, the steel-barreled version meets it, though with a bit of added weight in this configuration.
The Boundary’s defining feature is its .75-MOA accuracy guarantee for all versions, putting it squarely into near-custom territory. For hunters who want repeatable precision without stepping into full custom pricing, it’s a clear standout. You can read 
our full review here.
Savage Impulse Mountain Hunter
Savage’s Impulse Mountain Hunter introduces a straight-pull action to this list. 
The design allows shooters to cycle the action quickly without lifting the bolt handle, maintaining sight picture for faster follow-up shots. In testing, the rifle’s lightweight build and ergonomics made it particularly effective in rugged terrain. Editor-in-Chief Eric Poole 
noted in his review
 that "...every feature point from muzzle to buttpad contains noteworthy engineering."

Our test rifle was taken afield for a blacktail hunt in Paso Robles in California, where it's light weight (under 9 pounds with scope and sling) were demonstrated, and a buck was taken with a single shot at 156 yards.
Ruger American Rifle Gen II
Ruger’s American series has long been a staple of affordable bolt-action rifles, and the Gen II builds on that legacy with improved ergonomics and modularity, including a detachable magazine well that now accepts Accuracy International (AICS)-pattern magazines.
The rifle maintains Ruger’s reputation for reliable, field-ready performance, with a lightweight design that’s easy to carry over long distances. Built as a straightforward, no-nonsense platform, it consistently delivers practical accuracy and durability.
At just $769 for base models, 
the American Gen II
 is an excellent all-rounder for those interetsed in trying out the 6.5 Creedmoor.
Howa M1500 Super Lite
Weighing in under 5 pounds, the Howa Super Lite is purpose-built for hunters covering serious ground. Storied G&A contributor Craig Boddington is familiar with long treks often required for a successfull hunt, and the promise of such a lightweight rifle was a great opportunity to test its mettle.
Despite its minimal weight, it maintains solid accuracy and dependable function. The trade-off is increased recoil, but for mountain hunters, the reduced carry weight often outweighs that drawback. Boddington 
signed off on his review
 thusly: "This is not just an impressive little rifle — it’s an impressive rifle!"
Seekins Havak PH3
The 
Seekins Havak PH3
 brings near-custom rifle performance into a factory package under $2,000.
Known for precision machining and tight tolerances, Seekins delivers a rifle with a smooth action, excellent barrel quality, and consistent accuracy. It’s a step above most production rifles in terms of fit and finish. Using the same proprietary action as the PH2, the PH3 has improved ergonomics, a new bolt throw, and is more finely tuned. Rifle editor Tom Beckstrand was more than impressed with the rifle, and when he visited Seekins Precision's HQ to learn more about the production process, the Seekins team was proud to announce that all improvements made to the platform would not change the price of the rifle.

For shooters seeking custom-level performance without custom pricing, the PH3 is one of the strongest options available.
Christensen Arms Ridgeline FFT
Delivering sub-MOA accuracy in a lightweight precision hunting platform, 
the Ridgeline FFT
 blends carbon-fiber technology with precision engineering to create a lightweight rifle that doesn’t sacrifice accuracy.
With a carbon-wrapped barrel and advanced stock design, it offers excellent stiffness and reduced weight at just 5 punds, 14 ounces. The platform is backed by a sub-MOA accuracy guarantee, making it a strong option for hunters who want both mobility and precision. Not only is this rifle light, but it has key features that make it reliable, accurate and easy to shoot.
Browning X-Bolt 2 
A refined evolution of a proven hunting rifle with excellent ergonomics and reliability, the Browning X-Bolt 2 continues the lineage of one of the most trusted hunting rifles in the field. Combining modern features with strong field performance, the rifle retains key innovations like the rotary magazine, muzzle brake, and safety system, while adding improvements such as a smoother three-lug bolt, updated receiver, and an adjustable Vari-Tech composite stock. 
You can read our full review here.
For hunters who prefer a classic bolt-action feel with modern refinements, the X-Bolt 2 is a dependable choice.
