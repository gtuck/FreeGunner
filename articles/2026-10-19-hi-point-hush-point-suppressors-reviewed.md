Hi-­Point Hush-­Point Suppressors: Reviewed

The new Hush-Point suppressors command a low price for big noise suppression. Here's a full review.

 (Photo by Mark Fingar)

October 19, 2026

By Mark Fingar

For years, Hi-­Point Firearms possessed a devoted customer base that values utility over aesthetics, with an eye on budget. At the 2026 SHOT Show, Hi-­Point announced that it was entering the growing suppressor market with 
its new Hush-­Point line
. Hi-­Point’s plan is to do exactly what it did for handguns and carbines by making suppressors affordable enough for any gun owner to enjoy.
Hush-­Point suppressors are constructed of just four primary components, plus an O-­ring. Simply disassembly allows users easy access for cleaning and keeps the design affordable. (Photo by Mark Fingar)
On January 1, 2026, the removal of the $200 NFA tax stamp fee for suppressors changed the market. Prior to that, advertising an inexpensive suppressor wasn’t entirely practical since each can effectively had a $200 starting point. This partially resulted in a field of prices that ranged between $800 and $1,200 as the norm. Hi-­Point’s entry marks the beginning of a lower-­cost suppressor category.
“Our focus was to provide an economical, simple and foolproof design to our customer base,” said Kara Brown-­Boesenberg, president of MKS Supply and the exclusive distributor of Hi-­Point Firearms. The first two suppressors to arrive were 
the Hush-­Point 22
 and 
Hush-­Point 45
. The Hush-­Point 30 is next. Don’t let the price tags fool you; these suppressors perform.
Among other firearms, the Hush-­Point 45 was tested on Hi-­Point Firearms Yeet Cannon. It will complement every model with a threaded barrel in the Hi-­Point catalog. (Photo by Mark Fingar)
Hush-­Point 22
A suppressed .22 LR is one of life’s great pleasures, mainly because “Hollywood-­quiet” sound reduction is achievable. At $259, the Hush-­Point 22 is arguably the least expensive entry into the rimfire segment today. The Hush-­Point 22 features a Cerakote-­finished 7075 aluminum body with internal threads at each end. Inside is a reversible 7075 aluminum monolithic baffle core held in place between an end cap and a 17-­4 stainless steel ½-­28 thread adapter. Just four parts.

For users shooting dirty rimfire ammunition, monolithic designs such as this are easier to maintain and clean. Disassembly is made easy with the addition of a pronounced 13⁄16-­inch hex shape on each end. Hi-­Point includes a pair of nonmarring wrenches, but a 13⁄16 wrench or socket can also be used. Once the caps are removed, the monolithic core slides out. The core is hardcoat anodized for easy cleanup in an ultrasonic cleaner. Is it easy to put back together? Assembly takes about 15 seconds.
Unscrewing the muzzle allows the monocore baffle design to be removed, providing access to the ports for maintenance or replacement of the orange O-­ring. (Photo by Mark Fingar)
The Hush-­Point 22 is rated for a range of calibers from .17 WSM to .22 LR. We tested it on a Walther P22 and a Tactical Solutions .22 LR rifle using several different types of ammunition. As expected, the resulting sound was pleasant and there were no problems with function or impact on accuracy. While it’s not full-­auto-­rated, the Hush-­Point 22 performed in parallel with suppressors twice its price point. It’s great for those who enjoy plinking.
Hush-­Point 45
Also referred to as the “9/45,” the Hush-­Point 45 suppressor was built to complement the entire caliber spectrum of Hi-­Point’s firearm lineup. It is also welcome on just about any pistol-­caliber firearm with a threaded barrel. It is designed to support everything from .380 ACP to .45 ACP. Hi-­Point even includes both 1/2x28 and .578x28 TPI (a common .45 ACP barrel thread pitch) stainless-­steel thread adapters in the box.

The Hush-­Point 45 includes both 1/2x28 and .578x28 TPI stainless thread adapters. (Photo by Mark Fingar)
Users will immediately notice the absence of a Nielsen Device (i.e., “booster”). In its place is a stainless-­steel spacer. Since Hi-­Point pistols and carbines are all fixed-­barrel blowback-­actions, the Hush-­Point 45 threads directly to its firearms and eliminates the extra weight and cost of a booster assembly. For those using a tilt-­barrel Browning-­type action, the Hush-­Point 45 incorporates universal HUB threads (1.375x24), making it compatible with a variety of mounting adapters and spring-­loaded boosters to ensure semiautomatic reliability.
Like the Hush-­Point 22, the Hush-­Point 45 has an aluminum primary housing and core. While exotic materials save weight and add full-­auto durability, those materials are significantly more expensive. The use of 17-­4 stainless steel for the mount ensures a long lifespan at a critical area. The core design differs from the Hush-­Point 22, with an integral end cap and high-­temperature O-­ring. The core slips into the body from the front and threads into the end. Robust 1-­inch hex shapes on both ends of the assembled suppressor means assembly and disassembly is a snap. Included is a pair of non-­marring 1-­inch wrenches, but any 1-­inch wrench or socket will do. The O-­ring on the core helps keep grime and fouling from reaching the internal threads. The spacer drops into a rim at the rear of the core and mates with a radial groove on the back of the adapter. It’s a simple, cost-­effective design.
Hi-­Point conveniently provides a polymer, dual-­sided wrench to remove or install the stainless-­steel endcap and thread adapters. (Photo by Mark Fingar)
At The Range
We tested the Hush-­Point 45 with a Hi-­Point Yeet Cannon, a Hi-­Point Carbine in .30 Super Carry, an SAT 9C pistol in 9mm, an HK Tactical in .45 ACP and an HK SP5K in 9mm. Taking advantage of the HUB threads, the SAR and the HK 45 Tactical had aftermarket boosters. The SP5K had an aftermarket 3-­lug adapter. Across all the firearms used, the results were on par with other popular suppressors. The sound had an excellent tone, smooth function and no impact on accuracy. Like the Hush-­Point 22, the Hush-­Point 45 wasn’t designed to be a full-­auto workhorse, but it performed well in its class.
Hi-­Point achieved all of its goals with these suppressors. The debut of the Hush-­Point series is the perfect solution for those seeking value-­driven performance, and the price structure is easy on the ears as well. 
The Hush-Point 45 (above, top) compared to the Hush-Point 22 (above, bottom). (Photo by Mark Fingar)
