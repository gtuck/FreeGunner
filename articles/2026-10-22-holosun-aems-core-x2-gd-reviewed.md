Holosun AEMS-CORE-X2-GD: Reviewed

Holosun's rugged, reliable gold dot optic is easy to use in any light conditions. Here's a full review.

 (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)

October 22, 2026

By Brad Fitzpatrick

Holsun’s 
AEMS optics
 are very popular with shooters, and with good reason. The design is simple to install and intuitive to operate, making it a good choice for carbines and other platforms. The enclosed design and rugged construction allow the Holosun to withstand the abuses of daily use. Battery life is excellent and all the AEMS’s controls are easy to locate and operate, even in total darkness.
These are all features that we’ve come to expect from Holosun’s products, but one thing that you may not expect from the 
AEMS-CORE-X2
 is the option for a 2 MOA gold dot aiming point. Traditionally, optics (including Holosun models) have used red or green dots, so the option for a gold dot is a bit outside the box.
(Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
Gold Standard
I’ll admit that I had some suspicions about a gold dot sight. After all, the world has been getting along well with red and green dot sights for some time, and I couldn’t imagine that a gold dot would offer consistent visibility across a wide range of lighting conditions.
It turns out that I was wrong. The team at Holosun did their homework when adding the gold dot option to the AEMS family. After unboxing, I tried to find a background where the gold dot would fail. Initially, I thought bright sunlight and glare would do the trick, but I could still see the dot. In dark shadow it was also easy to spot, so I started seeking out surfaces that I was sure would absorb the dot. A concrete slab in full sun didn’t do the trick, and neither did a patch of waist-high yellow grass. Finally, I used a gold reflector from a photography kit. The dot was still visible.

For shooters with color vision deficiency, of which there are a considerable number, the Holosun’s gold dot is extremely easy to discern. I often wondered why there were so few alternatives to red and green dots for those poor shooters who suffered from red-green color vision deficiency. Apparently Holosun felt the same way, and they provided a gold alternative.
Brightness is controlled through two buttons on the left side of the optic housing. (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
I’m not an ophthalmologist and won’t pretend to understand the complexities of human vision, but I do know that with red, green, and gold dot options shooters with varying types and degrees of color vision limitation will likely find a dot that works for them. The Holosun gold dot was strikingly easy for me to identify in a full range of light conditions, and I’d suggest that you stop by the local gun shop and give the AEMS-CORE-X2-GD a try. I was quite surprised by how bright the dot was, and I suspect you will be, too.
Built For Battle
Regardless of which dot color you select, all AEMS CORE X2 reflex sights offer a long list of premium features that make them an excellent choice for any shooter. The AEMS mounting interface is rugged and uses heavy-duty components so that it can withstand heavy use. The base takes a solid bite on your rifle’s rail and the rugged aluminum housing with its MAO matte finish can withstand hard use. The AEMS-CORE-X2 is IP67 rated which means the optic can be submerged in a meter of water for up to 30 minutes. The Holosun is also shock-rated to resist 1,000G vibrations, and the fully enclosed design protects against debris and other items damaging the optical system.

Rugged optical design is important when selecting an optic, but so is battery life. The AEMS-CORE-X2 runs on a single CR2032 lithium battery, and battery life is up to 50,000 hours. What’s more, the optic is located in a side-locking tray. That means you can quickly swap out the battery without having to remove the optic from your firearm. The AEMS CORE X2’s shake-awake design allows the optic to sleep during periods of inactivity to preserve battery life. Movement activates the optic and powers on the dot so you’ll always have a crisp aiming point when you need it. The Holosun comes with 10 daylight and two night vision settings.
A slide locking tray holds the CR2032 battery in place, so users can replace it without removing the optic. (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
Holosun recently updated their optical systems to reduce parallax by 50%, improving accuracy. The AEMS-CORE-X2-GD also comes with the company’s four post mount. This lower 1/3 co-witness design is compatible with existing AEMS mounting solutions, and the design is rugged and versatile. The ability to use irons as well as the dot offers redundancy that ensures you’ll have a functional sighting system when you need it most. I also like the wide 1.1 x 0.87-inch viewing window. Tight windows promote tunnel vision, but the Holosun’s open design encourages awareness and adds to the optic’s versatility. Whether you’re defending your home, shooting competition, or hunting hogs or turkeys in dense forest you’ll be able to quickly get on target while still maintaining awareness of your surroundings.
The AEMS-CORE-X2-GD’s controls are intuitive and easy to use, even in total darkness. The brightness control buttons are located on the left side of the sight body, and the front button (+) increases brightness while the rear (-) dims the dot. There’s a windage adjustment screw on the left side of the scope body and an elevation screw on the right side, both of which are marked so that you make certain to adjust correctly. A momentary touch of either the + or - button powers on the optic, and holding both simultaneously turns off the sight and turn off the shake awake feature. There are both manual and automatic brightness adjustment settings, and holding the + button for three seconds to activate manual mode (indicated by a single blink of the dot). All the tools you need to adjust windage and elevation, loosen the battery compartment, and tighten the installation nut are included with the optic.
The base of the optic takes up a solid piece of rail, and secures tightly. (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
A Sight for All Seasons
The Holosun AEMS-CORE-XS-GD is a versatile optic, but by the time I’d finished range testing the new sight it had been on three different guns. First, I tested it on a 
PSA JAKL 5.56 semiauto
 and then switched the sight to a 
Mossberg MVP Scout rifle
 in .308 Winchester. Finally, I attached the Holsun to my 
Beretta A300 Ultima 20-gauge turkey gun
. In a little less than three hours of testing I swapped the AEMS-CORE-X2-GD among guns firing 55-grain FMJ bullets, 185-grain subsonic loads, and 3-inch copper plated #5 turkey magnums. That level of versatility is hard to match.
The gold dot impressed me throughout the test. Its color varies from greenish gold to neon yellow depending upon the exact light conditions, but finding the dot was never an issue in any light. Rapidly locating the dot is easy in the Holosun’s spacious viewing window which is among the largest of any reflex sight I’ve tested.

I like the auto brightness feature on the Holosun. After losing a turkey because of an incorrect brightness setting (the bird was coming through dim woods so I lowered the intensity of the dot, only to locate him behind me in a bright alfalfa field where ambient light washed out the sight), I always keep my reflex sights on auto setting. That’s provided, of course, the optic does a good job matching brightness to ambient conditions, and Holosun optics always select an intensity level that works with ambient light conditions. Sometimes I’d like something a bit brighter or dimmer, but that’s an easy fix, and the auto setting has never failed to be visible any time I’ve used a Holosun. The AEMS-CORE-X2-GD was no exception.
A Beretta A300 Ultima Turkey 20-gauge was used for testing. (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
Rugged hardware and solid mounting surfaces mean that this sight is suited to handling some serious abuse. A half-dozen rounds of three-inch 20-gauge magnums is enough to loosen some reflex sights, but the Holosun shrugged off the abuse and the mounting nut remained tight. I also like that the mount design allows me to use my backup iron sights. The turkey gun comes with a red fiber optic dot, the .308 with a post/ghost ring setup, and both were visible and useful while the Holosun was in place.
The AEMS-CORE-X2-GD carries an MSRP of $352.93, which is very good for an optic with this array of features and such solid build quality. If you aren’t willing to give the gold dot version a try there are also more traditional red and green dot AEMS-CORE-X2 models. Regardless of which of these optics you choose, though, you’ll find that the versatile and reliable AEMS is a great all-around optic? It works on scout guns, semiauto carbine, turkey guns, and just about any other platform upon which you’d mount a reflex optic. With so much to offer it’s possible to expect the AEMS-CORE-X2 to become the new standard in reflex sights—no matter which dot color you choose.
