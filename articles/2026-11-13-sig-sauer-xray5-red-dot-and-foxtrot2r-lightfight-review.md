SIG Sauer XRAY5 Red Dot and FOXTROT2R LightFight Review

How could you improve on SIG's outstanding red dot and tactical light? By having them talk to one another via Bluetooth, of course.

 (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)

November 13, 2026

By Brad Fitzpatrick

Taken alone, SIG’s 
XRAY5 optic
 is an outstanding red dot that’s built for battle, and the company’s 
FOXTROT2R LIGHTFIGHT
 light offers class-leading lumens, candela, and runtime in a light, compact package. But where these optics truly break new ground is that the weapon light and optic actually talk to one another via Bluetooth, ensuring that your dot is always visible, whether your light is on or off.
This optic-light connection is revolutionary, but it’s not the only feature that elevates this pairing to among the best in class. Now, SIG is offering both the XRAY5 optic and FOXTROT2R LightFight weapon light as a combo, which simplifies the buying process. Here’s a look at why the XRAY5 and FOXTROT2R are both great standalone products—and why they’re even better together.
The only two buttons you'll find on the XRAY5 are on the left side, marked with up and down arrows. (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
XRAY5
There are lots of optics on the market, but none match the feature list found on the XRAY5. It comes with a 7075-aluminum housing that is designed to withstand heavy use, and the aspherical lens is protected by a grade 5 titanium lens shield on the top of the unit. The optic is fully enclosed, with a reflective front window and a rear window that is secured within the aluminum housing. The CR2032 lithium battery is housed on the side of the optic so that the SIG doesn’t need to be removed to swap batteries.
On the interior of the optic, there’s a circuit board with a 225-pixel emitter and a Bluetooth antenna. The solid-state design makes this one of the most rugged and reliable red dots ever designed, capable of withstanding the shock of heavy recoiling pistols. The durable design has allowed SIG to test the XRAY5 to over 100,000 rounds without failure or loss of zero. Additionally, SIG administered an eight-foot drop test onto concrete and submerged the optic to 132 feet. The XRAY5 isn’t just expected to survive this rough handling, but it needs to continue to function and maintain zero. If you’re relying on a red dot to survive a deadly confrontation, that level of dependability isn’t just beneficial, it’s critical.

There are just two buttons on the housing of the XRAY5; on the left side of the optic you’ll find up and down arrows, which begs a question—how do you zero this optic? Zeroing the optic can be done manually or, if your optic is connected to your smart phone, via the XRAY5 app. To adjust POI manually, hold the down button to access the zero feature designated by a 0 and direction arrows on the screen. You can use the down button to orient the arrow up, down, left, or right, and then use the up arrow to make final adjustments. On the app, you can simply adjust the point of impact left, down, right, or left as desired.
Multiple zero profiles can be stored in the XRAY5 app. (Photo courtesy of SIG Sauer)
The 3.5 MOA single red dot offers 15 different brightness settings (13 day, 2 night) and offers a runtime of 10,000 hours running. Adding to the optic’s longevity is the MOTAC motion-activated system that powers the optic down after two minutes of inactivity and turns it back on when the optic senses motion. This improves longevity and guarantees the optic will be on when you draw to fire.
The 24mm aperture (measured diagonally) offers a wide window, and the narrow aluminum housing improves awareness when you’re shooting. What’s perhaps most impressive, though, is the distortion-free viewing through the XRAY5’s wide window, that SIG Sauer calls the Zero Distortion Optical system, or ZDO. Some optics distort image clarity and color, but the ZDO's flat lens has a benefit of zero distortion, and the window is wide open and clear. It’s as close to looking through a “windowless” optic as I’ve seen, and objects through the sight appear crisp and natural.

The pixel layout allows for 80 MOA of windage and elevation adjustment, which means that you can fine-tune your optic to perfectly match your pistol. Multiple pistols, in fact, thanks to the ballistic profiles stored in the app. Traditionally, ballistic profiles have been the provenance of long-range rifles, but SIG has found a way to incorporate that technology into its XRAY5. This also means that you can zero the optic for multiple loads with a single pistol.
This is all pretty clever technology, and it’s amazing how SIG has managed to pack so many features into such a light, compact package. Height is 1.2 inches, width is 1.32 inches, and length is just 1.74 inches on the compact model, which makes this an ideal optic for either duty or daily carry guns. The XRAY5 is certainly one of the most appealing red dot optics available today, and it pairs well—quite literally—with SIG’s excellent FOXTROT2R LightFight weapon light.
FOXTROT2R LightFight Light
The optic window is generous, and gives a great clarity and field of view. (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
Handgun-mounted lights have become commonplace on both duty and concealed carry pistols, and SIG’s new FOXTROT2R LightFight is among the best. Aside from the light’s ability to communicate with your optic, the FOXTROT2R LightFight is a remarkably well-thought-out light that offers numbers you wouldn’t expect in such a compact package. Whereas most compact weapon lights in this class offer something on the order of 500 lumens, the FOXTROT2R LightFight's 700 lumens already make it, ahem, shine compared to the competition. Equally impressive is the 20,000 candelas it generates. In layman’s terms, that means a brilliant beam that’s concentrated downrange with enough ambient glow to light up any darkened room.
This powerful light is also easy to operate. It comes with four sets of interchangeable rear paddles that activate when pressed up or down. Pressing on the top of either the left or right paddle produces a momentary beam that powers off when the paddle is released. Pressing up on the paddle results in continuous light.
The FOXTROT2R-LF rests snugly under most handguns, and comes with adjustable paddles for ease of use. (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
It’s probably worth discussing the science of using lights in combat here because it relates directly to the design and functions of the FOXTROT2R LightFight. Weapon light tactics are a growing discipline, and one point that most experts emphasize is that the proper use of a momentary light feature can be highly effective in a dangerous situation. This allows you to identify the intruder, stun them with bright light (and 700 lumens/20,000 candela is quite stunning) and then shoot or move as needed quickly without maintaining a constant illuminated beam which could present you as a target. There are also times when constant illumination is required, such as when searching a house. Many weapon lights require you to manipulate your hold time on a single paddle to transition between momentary and continuous light. The SIG’s design is much more straightforward. Up for continuous, down for momentary is a routine any shooter can learn quickly and perform effectively in a defensive situation.

Having four included paddles is a nice touch. The FOXTROT2R LightFight comes with a pair of inserts that can be adjusted to fit most any pistol with a standard 1913 rail and many proprietary designs, and the ability to adjust the lugs makes the SIG easier to customize. But having four pairs of interchangeable paddles virtually ensures a consistent fit. I mounted the light on a CZ Shadow 2 Carry, and since that gun has a SA/DA trigger mechanism I wanted the paddles positioned farther ahead for more clearance inside the trigger guard. That’s no problem with the FOXTROT2R LightFight because you can simply select smaller paddles.
The “R” in the FOXTROT’s name signifies that it uses rechargeable batteries, two of which are included with each light. Having a backup battery is beneficial, especially since battery swaps—which require simply unscrewing the bezel—take a matter of seconds. Since each battery has a runtime of up to 1.5 hours, there’s plenty of illumination time available. The FOXTROT2R LightFight also uses FlexCore dual fuel technology, so CR123A batteries work as well.
Like the XRAY5, the FOXTROT2R LightFight packs a lot of excellent features into a compact footprint. Overall length is just 2.98 inches, which is smaller than many competing lights. It’s also IPX7 waterproof, so it can withstand wet and dusty conditions while still functioning properly. If it doesn’t survive, however, you’re covered by SIG’s Infinite Guarantee.
The FOXTROT2R comes with two rechargeable batteries. (Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
SIG’s Light/Optic System
LightFight technology is truly groundbreaking. There have been premium lights and optics available for years, but never before did the two communicate. This is particularly important because the use of a light can drown out a dot, making accurate shots nearly impossible. That’s not a problem with the XRAY5 and FOXTROT2R LightFight system because the optic’s brightness adjusts automatically based on whether the light is on or not. That means no more panicking to adjust when the light washes out the red dot.
I mounted both the light and optic on a 9mm CZ Shadow 2 Carry pistol. The optic provided a bright, clear aiming point in any light thanks to its 15 brightness settings, and the crisp, unobstructed view of the target made firing ten ring shots at seven yards simple. I did need to fine tune the point of impact, but that’s not a problem. It can be done manually with the optic or, more simply, with the XRAY5 app.
The FOXTROT2R LightFight is also an excellent light that floods dark spaces. 20,000 candela translates to an intense center beam, but the throw is sufficient that you won’t miss any bad guys huddled in dark corners. Best of all, if I needed to use the light for an extended period and I ate up the first battery I could swap to the pre-charged second battery instantly. With a couple of CR123A batteries picked up from the local hardware store you could run this light in full power throughout the night, and if electric power is available you can charge one battery while running the other.
(Photo by Brad Fitzpatrick)
It’s remarkable how seamlessly the optic and light communicate, and most importantly it eliminates one distraction when faced with a threat. The conversation that takes place between the optic and the light allows the shooter to focus on what’s important—surviving.
There were no function issues when testing the light and optic and the range, and the abuse doled out by the CZ didn’t cause either the XRAY5 or the FOXTROT2R LightFight to flinch. Both held up well, which is what you’d expect from SIG, but should anything unforeseen happen you can rest assured that the light and optic are covered by SIG’s impressive Infinite Guarantee.

The COMPACT XRAY5 and FOXTROT2R LightFight are available separately, or you can buy them together as a package for $649.99, which is a savings of around $50 when buying the light and optic separately. The test optic was the COMPACT version that fits RMSc footprints, and versions that fit PRO (DeltaPoint Pro), SIG-LOC COMPACT, and SIG-LOC PRO footprints are also available.
How do you make a great optic and a great light even better? Allow them to chat. That’s what SIG has done with the XRAY5 and the FOXTROT2R LightFight, and that’s why this is one of the best light/optic combos available.
