STR-45 Review: A Practical, Budget-Friendly .45 ACP Pistol

An Affordable .45 ACP Choice: How the Stoeger STR-45 Delivers Practical Features and Solid Value

 The polymer-frame STR-45 is well suited for duty or home defense applications. (Yamil Sued photo)

July 23, 2026

By Richard Nance

Although Stoeger was established in 1924, it didn’t make a striker-fired pistol until the introduction of the STR-9 in 2019. The 9mm-chambered STR-9 line ranged in size from subcompact to full-size models. The polymer-frame STR-9 was packed with features that belied its affordability. Riding the 
STR-9’s wave of success
, Stoeger introduced the STR-45, the company’s first full-size .45 ACP pistol.
Patterned after the STR-9, the STR-45 has similar ergonomics and features like interchangeable backstraps, a straight frontstrap, deep slide serrations front and rear, an ambidextrous slide stop and a reversible magazine release.
Features
The ambidextrous slide stop may seem like a little thing, but it’s a hallmark of a fighting pistol. It enables you to lock the slide open or close it from either side, and if you’ve ever tried to clear a double feed with your off hand, you can relate. Southpaws will appreciate that they can orient the magazine release on the right side of the pistol, where it’s easily accessible with their left thumb.
The STR-45 is available with tritium night sights, and the rear sight is adjustable. The slide is cut for optics, and the gun comes with four adapter plates. (Yamil Sued photo)
The STR-45 comes in four configurations. You can get it with standard three-dot white sights or with tritium night sights. I tested the latter. While the STR-45 typically ships with two 13-round, metal-bodied magazines, it’s also available with 10-rounders for restricted-capacity states. The base model has a suggested retail price of $449, while the tritium night sight model costs $100 more.

It would be easy to assume a full-size .45 ACP pistol at that price would be cheaply built, but that’s not the case with the STR-45. The glass-reinforced polymer frame sports an undercut trigger guard and abbreviated beavertail that contribute to a high hold for optimal control. Medium and large backstraps enable you to tailor the grip to best fit your hand.
The STR-45 is 7.9 inches long, 5.6 inches tall and 1.3 inches wide. It weighs 30.5 ounces with an unloaded magazine. While not unduly large, the STR-45 is a handful of gun and is ideally suited for those with medium to large hands.
Aggressive texturing on the front and back of the grip and on the face of the trigger guard is complemented by finer texturing on the sides. Textured areas above the front of the squared, oversize trigger guard provide a handy index for the thumb of your support hand and your trigger finger when not on the trigger.

The STR-45 has an ambidextrous slide stop and a reversible magazine release. There is no thumb safety, but the trigger incorporates a safety blade. (Yamil Sued photo)
The STR-45 features a three-slot accessory rail for mounting a full-size light or light/laser combo unit. The slide is cut to accommodate a wide variety of optics using one of the four supplied mounting plates.
Whether you opt to run the traditional notch-and-post sights or an optic, you’ll benefit from the STR-45’s wide, flat-face trigger. Serrations and a lip at the bottom of the trigger provide a non-slip surface for your finger. The trigger on my test model broke with five pounds of pressure, which is reasonable for a striker-fired pistol. Reset was relatively short and easy to discern.
Although the STR-45 lacks a manual safety, there is a trigger safety and a firing pin safety. The trigger safety is automatically disengaged when the trigger is pressed, so you don’t have to remember to do that in a defensive scenario.
Like the STR-9, the STR-45 uses a loaded-chamber indicator to help confirm whether there is a round in the chamber. When the chamber is loaded, the indicator protrudes above the top of the slide, providing visual and tactile confirmation of the status of the pistol. The latter is beneficial when checking the condition of the STR-45 in diminished light.
Both the frontstrap and the backstrap are aggressively textured, while the sides are more finely textured. (Yamil Sued photo)
The alloy steel slide and 4.7-inch barrel are nitride treated for durability and corrosion resistance. The all-black exterior of the STR-45 ensures there’s nothing shiny to compromise your position during stealthy movement, and the STR-45 reminded me a lot of the Glock 21 I carried as a police officer.

Performance
Before testing, I selected the appropriate mounting plate and topped the STR-45 with a Trijicon RCR. It was a good match. When I drove the pistol to the target, I picked up the red dot intuitively.
At the range, the STR-45 performed well, and it pointed naturally for me. Thanks to the high hold and just-right grip texturing, the STR-45’s comparatively stout recoil was easy to manage while shooting realistic drills from seven to 10 yards.
The STR-45 is more of a workhorse than a tack driver, but accuracy results were decent. Five-shot groups ranged from 2.5 to 3.5 inches, as you can see in the accompanying chart. Not bad for a polymer-frame, striker-fired .45 pistol.
Although the STR-45 is bigger than the guns I normally carry concealed, it’s well suited for duty or home defense applications. If you’re in the market for a full-size, feature-rich .45 striker-fired pistol, the Stoeger’s STR-45 is deserving of your consideration.
